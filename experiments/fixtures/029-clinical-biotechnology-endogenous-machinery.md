## Scientific and evidential boundary

The source audit records four established, scoped biological observations:

1. induced-proximity systems can recruit cellular degradation machinery;
2. secreted lysosomal enzymes can support cross-correction in declared systems;
3. a sequence-specific antisense mediator can redirect SMN2 splicing; and
4. a compatible pharmacological chaperone can stabilise a protein during
   trafficking and release it for later activity.

Those observations constrain the artificial interventions but are **evidence
inputs, not fixture targets**. F-029 tests only the four speculative engineering
translations. Synthetic success cannot validate the biological claims, and
synthetic failure cannot refute them. A source reproduction, clinical outcome,
EMA product record, synthetic smoke run, and confirmed AI comparison remain five
different evidential objects.

The fixture deliberately gives every proposed arm a strong conventional null:

- `CMB-X01`: calibrated suppression, direct deletion, periodic garbage
  collection, and an explicit tagged queue;
- `CMB-X02`: independent repair, central service, content-addressed multicast,
  and budget-matched gossip;
- `CMB-X03`: a versioned router, prefix/prompt, adapter, low-rank update, and
  full update; and
- `CMB-X04`: package validation with retry, replication, checkpoint reload,
  reconstruction, and a permanently bound wrapper.

If a null implements the effective behavior more cheaply, the biological
analogy is retained as source material and the transfer hypothesis is rejected.

## `CMB-X01` — Recruited maintenance versus occupancy and explicit queueing

### System and task

Each instance contains a graph of compartments with finite useful service,
target slots, and one or more capacity-limited maintenance engines. A target

$$
z_{i,t}\in\{\text{absent},\text{useful},\text{harmful}\}.
$$

positive, false negative, correlation, and staleness processes. Target births,
harmful conversions, useful recoveries, and resynthesis after removal are
generated independently of arm code. The public registry supplies permitted
target classes, engine classes, and compatibility metadata but never the latent
harm label or future event stream.

A mediator can bind a target and an engine. In `X01-RECRUIT`, removal occurs
only after a productive recruitment event consumes one engine service slot;
pairwise compatibility does not guarantee productive geometry. Every other
actionable removal arm consumes an equivalent registered maintenance/actuation
service slot, so `X01-DIRECT` does not receive free deletion. Let $T_t$, $M_t$,
$E_t$, and $C_t$ be target, free mediator, free engine, and productive complex
counts. A diagnostic accounting description is

$$
T_{t+1}=T_t+A_t-R_t+Q_t,
$$

where $A_t$, $R_t$, and $Q_t$ are the event counts occurring during step $t$
for arrivals, completed removals, and resynthesis. For `X01-RECRUIT`, $R_t$
cannot exceed productive-complex count; for every actionable arm it cannot
exceed available maintenance/actuation service or the arm's authorised removal
count. This equation is an accounting identity for the synthetic process, not
a biochemical model.

The task is to suppress or remove harmful targets while preserving useful
targets and accepted service. Every irreversible deletion requires a versioned

- probability that a pairwise-compatible encounter is productive;
