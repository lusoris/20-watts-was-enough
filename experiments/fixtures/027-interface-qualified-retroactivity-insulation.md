## Registered hypotheses

1. **H1 — connected dynamics.** A downstream binding pool can change the
   source model's free-signal trajectory even when the isolated production and
   loss law is unchanged.
2. **H2 — finite approximation support.** The reduced retroactivity factor
   predicts the full ODE only inside a verified timescale and parameter
   envelope.
3. **H3 — direction matters.** Sequestration-shaped loading can affect rising
   and falling responses differently; generic queue delay need not reproduce
   that signature.
4. **H4 — observers are interfaces.** A synchronous producer-owned reporter
   can perturb the state or timing it reports; an asynchronous reporter can
   reduce back-action while losing freshness or records.
5. **H5 — causal separation.** Direct pinning, a finite pathway-specific
   transform pool, disconnected work-matched contention, and intended feedback
   have distinct intervention signatures.
6. **H6 — no free insulation.** Copying, buffering, rate limiting, process
   isolation, and adaptive admission expose latency, memory, work, staleness,
   loss, and recovery costs.
7. **H7 — no universal energy law.** Coupling can have non-monotone relations
   with distortion, work, bandwidth, and leak robustness; logical work never
   establishes joules.
8. **H8 — conditional transfer.** The artificial translation helps only when
   consumers directly hold a finite producer-owned state across producer
   deadlines.
9. **H9 — useful coupling is objective-qualified.** A deliberately coupled
   arm can qualify only on a preregistered shaping target, remain noninferior
   to a tuned explicit filter on that target and every protected service axis,
   and improve at least one fully charged resource axis.

Failure of a direct interface is not evidence for the proposed adaptive arm.
The candidate-like arm must beat the strongest applicable ordinary systems
null under byte-identical input and equal authority.

## Common source-model generator

### Full equations

For $N$ clients, the full deterministic ODE is

$$
\dot X
=
k(t)-\delta X
+
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\left[
k_{\mathrm{off},j}C_j
-
k_{\mathrm{on},j}X(p_j-C_j)
\right],
$$

$$
\dot C_j
=
k_{\mathrm{on},j}X(p_j-C_j)
-
k_{\mathrm{off},j}C_j.
$$

The isolated arm sets $N=0$. Initial states are the nonnegative steady states
for the pre-intervention constant production flux. No post-step clipping is
permitted.

### Heterogeneous fast-binding reduction

For S-REDUCED, the algebraic bound concentration for client $j$ is

$$
\bar C_j(\bar X)
=
\frac{p_j\bar X}{K_{d,j}+\bar X}.
$$

Define the dimensionless aggregate loading slope

$$
A_N(\bar X)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\frac{p_jK_{d,j}}{(K_{d,j}+\bar X)^2}
$$

and $R_N(\bar X)=A_N(\bar X)/(1+A_N(\bar X))$. The heterogeneous reduced
trajectory is

$$
\dot{\bar X}
=
\bigl(k(t)-\delta\bar X\bigr)
\bigl[1-R_N(\bar X)\bigr].
$$

For $N=0$, the empty sum gives $A_0=R_0=0$. For $N=1$, this is algebraically
identical to the single-pool factor in the mathematical note. Initial
$\bar X$ is the free-signal component of the full model's registered
pre-intervention steady state, and every $\bar C_j$ is reconstructed from that
same $\bar X$; incompatible total mass or a negative algebraic concentration
invalidates the reduced cell. No client averaging or effective affinity is
permitted.

### Reference parameterization

Use a reference dissociation concentration
$K_*=1\ \mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}$ and
$\delta=0.01\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$. For client $j$,

$$
K_{d,j}=a_jK_*,
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{off},j}=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon_j},
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{on},j}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{off},j}}{K_{d,j}},
$$

where $a_j$ and $\varepsilon_j$ are dimensionless. Development values are

- $a_j\in\{0.25,1,4\}$;
- $\varepsilon_j\in\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$;
- $p_j/K_*\in\{0.1,1,10\}$; and
- $N\in\{0,1,4,16\}$.

For heterogeneous-client worlds, client values cycle through the ordered
Cartesian product of $a_j$, $\varepsilon_j$, and $p_j/K_*$. Every distinct
starting offset is enumerated once as a deterministic source cell; source
cells have no random seed and are never repeated as pseudo-replicates. This
rule prevents post-hoc selection of only strong or weak clients.

### Source input histories

Let $X_*=K_*$, $k_*=\delta X_*=0.01\ \mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$, and
$T_s=1/\delta=100\ \mathrm{s}$. Each history is evaluated from
$t=0$ to $t=24T_s$.

1. **Rise:** $k(t)=0.5k_*$ before $3T_s$, then $2k_*$.
2. **Fall:** $k(t)=2k_*$ before $3T_s$, then $0.5k_*$.
3. **Pulse:** $k(t)=k_*$, rises to $2k_*$ on
   $[3T_s,6T_s)$, then returns to $k_*$.
4. **Slow sine:** $k(t)=k_*[1+0.5\sin(0.1t/T_s)]$.
5. **Fast sine:** $k(t)=k_*[1+0.5\sin(2t/T_s)]$.
6. **Chirp:** $k(t)=k_*[1+0.4\sin(\phi(t))]$, where
   $\phi(t)=0.02(t/T_s)+0.04(t/T_s)^2$ radians.

Production is nonnegative in every history.

### Numerical contract

1. For a loaded cell define the conservative concentration bound

   $$
   X_{\max}=2K_*+\sum_jp_j
   $$

   and rate bound

   $$
   L_{\mathrm{cell}}
   =
   \delta
   +\sum_j k_{\mathrm{on},j}p_j
   +\max_j\left(k_{\mathrm{off},j}+k_{\mathrm{on},j}X_{\max}\right)
   \quad[\mathrm{s}^{-1}].
   $$

   Let $dt_{\mathrm{exp}}=\min\{0.02T_s,(20L_{\mathrm{cell}})^{-1}\}$.
   No maximum over an empty client set is evaluated. The implementation
   reports the bound, fastest implied rate, and solver branch for every cell.
2. If $\lceil24T_s/dt_{\mathrm{exp}}\rceil\le240000$, primary integration is
   explicit fourth-order Runge--Kutta at $dt_{\mathrm{exp}}$ with a nested
   quarter-step repeat. Otherwise use fixed-step implicit BDF2 for the vector
   $z=(X,C_1,\ldots,C_N)$:

   $$
   \frac{3z_{m+1}-4z_m+z_{m-1}}{2h}
   =f(t_{m+1},z_{m+1}),
   $$

   with backward Euler for the first step, analytic Jacobian, and exact step
   shortening at every input discontinuity and final time. Begin at
   $h=0.02T_s$ and compare with a nested $h/4$ repeat. Newton iterations solve
   the displayed residual, stop only when both the residual infinity norm is
   at most $10^{-12}K_*\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and the state increment infinity
   norm is at most $10^{-12}K_*$, and invalidate after 20 iterations.
   If convergence fails, halve $h$ and retry both runs, for at most eight
   halvings; this bounds the final primary grid at 307200 steps per trajectory.
3. The maximum primary-versus-quarter normalized trajectory difference,
   computed at nested timestamps as absolute concentration difference divided
   by $K_*$, must be at most $10^{-6}$, or the cell is invalid. Paired $N=0$
   isolated and cut-edge trajectories inherit the loaded cell's exact branch
   and grids. A separately implemented adaptive stiff solver, whose source and
   version hashes enter run identity, must agree within $10^{-5}$ normalized
   units on public-development cells before confirmation can be enabled.
4. The binding-only mass residual for each accepted step must be at most
   $10^{-10}K_*$, after accounting for production and loss.
5. Negative concentration below $-10^{-12}K_*$, a non-finite value, or a
   closure failure invalidates the run. Values are never repaired by clipping.

## Common artificial event generator

### Producer

The producer has $d=8$ leaky state components, one scalar input, and one
scalar output. The logical step is $\Delta t=0.05\ \mathrm{s}$, and each
world lasts $300\ \mathrm{s}$. For component $i\in\{1,\ldots,8\}$,

$$
\tau_i
=
0.05\left(\frac{5}{0.05}\right)^{(i-1)/7}\ \mathrm{s},
$$

$$
\rho_i=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_i},
$$

$$
x_{n+1,i}
=
\rho_i x_{n,i}+(1-\rho_i)b_u u_n,
$$

$$
y_n=\frac{h_y}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{8}x_{n+1,i}.
$$

$b_u=1\ \mathrm{NSU\,NIU^{-1}}$ is the input-to-state gain and
$h_y=1\ \mathrm{NOU\,NSU^{-1}}$ is the state-to-output gain. Thus every sum
is dimensionally homogeneous. Input $u_n$ is due at $t_n=n\Delta t$. A
physical record for due sequence $n$ contains the post-update state
$x_{n+1}$ and the 32 B metadata declared below. The scalar $y_n$ is a derived
view, not another serialized field: deriving it reads all 64 state bytes and
costs seven additions plus one multiplication by $h_y/8$. The record retains
$t_n$ as its immutable due timestamp and records the actual update-completion
time as its publication timestamp. Its identity stores
`due_sequence=n` and `state_index=n+1`. A dropped, held, or horizon-pending
sequence leaves a permanent gap; sequence IDs are never reused or compacted,
and a later queued execution keeps its original $n$ and $t_n$. Initial state
$x_0$ is never itself due sequence zero. At $t=0$, freeze
$x_{0,i}=b_u u_0$ for all components; all slots and queues are empty, producer
version is one, the next due sequence is zero, no client holds a
reference, and every client expects sequence zero. The first ten seconds are
warm-up and excluded from primary scoring but included in resource counts.

### Input families

The public generator selects one of six byte-identical causal input histories:

1. upward step from 0.25 NIU to 0.75 NIU at 75 s;
2. downward step from 0.75 NIU to 0.25 NIU at 75 s;
3. pulse from 0.5 NIU to 0.9 NIU on $[75,125)$ s;
4. sine $u(t)=0.5+0.35\sin(2\pi f t)$ with
   $f\in\{0.05,0.5\}$ Hz unless a track freezes an explicit replacement
   frequency partition;
5. bounded autoregression
   $u_{n+1}=0.98u_n+0.02(0.5)+\eta_n$, where
   $\eta_n$ is zero-mean Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.01 NIU and
   values outside $[0,1]$ use repeated reflection
   $u_{n+1}=1-|1-(v\bmod 2)|$, where $v$ is the unconstrained update and
   $v\bmod2\in[0,2)$; and
6. burst train with a 0.5 NIU baseline and seed-selected 0.4 NIU pulses whose
   interarrival times are exponential with mean 5 s and whose durations are
   uniform on $[0.05,1]$ s. The input is 0.9 NIU whenever at least one pulse
   is live and 0.5 NIU otherwise; overlaps form the union of live intervals
   and neither add amplitude nor restart another pulse's expiry.

Full-protocol random values use binary64 and the PCG64-DXSM core already frozen
in [`generator.mjs`](../workstation/fixture-027/generator.mjs). Each independent
full-protocol stream is initialized from the UTF-8 literal
`F027-v1|partition|protocol|seed-hex|cell-id|replicate|scope`, where
`partition` is `development`, `confirmation`, `transfer`, or `ablation`;
`protocol` is the exact RIN ID; `seed-hex` is exactly 16 lowercase hexadecimal
digits from that partition's committed seed pack; `cell-id` and `replicate`
are unpadded decimal integers; and `scope` is one of `input`, `hold`,
`consumer-work`, `transform-work`, `process-noise`, `fault`, or `analysis`.
The implementation hashes that literal with SHA-256 and applies its committed
128-bit PCG initialization. Public-development seeds are literal manifest
data; confirmation and transfer seeds remain sealed by digest until reveal.
Streams are never reused across scopes.

A standard-normal draw consumes exactly two fresh uniforms $U_1,U_2$ and
returns

$$
Z=\sqrt{-2\ln(\max(U_1,2^{-53}))}\cos(2\pi U_2),
$$

without caching the unused sine variate. An exponential duration of mean
$\mu$ is $-\mu\ln(1-U)$; a uniform draw on $[a,b)$ is $a+(b-a)U$.
Library-default random state, spare-variate caching, and implementation-defined
event iteration order are prohibited. The bounded RIN-T01 smoke keeps its
separate `F027-v1|phase|RIN-T01|seed-hex|scope|canonical-id` preimage; smoke
seeds and outputs cannot be relabelled as full-protocol evidence.

### Publication and consumers

The producer owns $S\in\{1,4,16\}$ publication slots. One published
eight-component binary64 state occupies 64 B plus a fixed 32 B metadata record.
Direct clients acquire a reference to the producer slot. The slot is reusable
only after all acquiring clients release it.

Client count is $N\in\{1,4,16\}$. Every client subscribes to every producer
publication. Its hold time is

$$
h_{j,n}
=
\min\left(
20\tau_p,
\max\left(
\Delta t,
\tau_p r_h e^{\sigma_h Z^h_{j,n}}
\right)
\right),
$$

where $\tau_p=1\ \mathrm{s}$ is the reference producer timescale,
$r_h\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ is a dimensionless hold-time ratio,
$\sigma_h=1$ is a dimensionless log-scale standard deviation, and
$Z^h_{j,n}$ is a seed-drawn standard-normal variate for client $j$ and
publication $n$. Each publication draws new values.

If no slot is available, the registered direct policy is one of:

1. **hold-state:** consume the input event but retain the prior producer state;
2. **drop-input:** discard that input event and record a drop;
3. **queue-input:** retain at most 32 pending input events, then drop oldest;
4. **block-clock:** delay producer logical time until a slot releases. Every
   exogenous arrival during the pause is appended to a charged FIFO of 24 B
   records. After release, inputs are delivered in original sequence at no
   more than one per $\Delta t$ of unblocked wall time; their original due
   timestamps remain fixed. The finite 300 s horizon bounds the FIFO under the
   1 MiB cap. Items still pending at 300 s are unavailable outputs, not shifted
   into an extended horizon.

These are separate arms. They may not be pooled.

For **A-DIRECT-QUEUE**, each due input first becomes a 24 B FIFO record holding
its immutable $(n,t_n,u_n)$. Allocating that record costs 8 operations and
writes an 8 B allocator record; initialization costs 3 operations and writes
24 B. The common 8-operation, 16 B-read, 16 B-write queue-entry charge applies
separately on enqueue and dequeue. Releasing the 24 B record costs 8 operations
and writes an 8 B allocator record. At an input timestamp, stage 8 appends the
new record to the tail. At stage 9, and also after the stage-14 append at any
producer-update completion or slot-release timestamp, an idle producer worker
with a free slot reserves the lowest-index free slot and dispatches exactly the
oldest waiting record. A newer arrival never overtakes queued work. The
dispatched update costs 192 operations on the 4096-operation-per-second
producer worker; its nonzero service time is the only drain-rate limit, and its
completion invokes the ordinary update/publication stages with the original
$n$ and $t_n$. After the immediate dispatch opportunity, if more than 32
records remain waiting, dequeue, release, and charge the oldest waiting
records until 32 remain; an in-service record is never evicted. Its 24 B record
remains live until update completion, when it is released. A slot release
without a simultaneous input uses the same dispatch rule. At 300 s, every
waiting record is dequeued and released, every in-service record is released
after charging partial producer work, and all corresponding outputs remain
unavailable; nothing is replayed beyond the horizon.

Consumer work for one publication is

$$
O_{j,n}=128+16d+\left\lfloor64e^{0.5Z^O_{j,n}}\right\rfloor
$$

logical scalar operations, where $Z^O_{j,n}$ is an independent seed-drawn
standard-normal variate. Reusing $Z^h_{j,n}$ for work is prohibited. Consumer
output is $h_y$ times the arithmetic mean of the acquired producer state plus
a client-specific fixed offset $0.001j$ NOU. The offset prevents accidental
identity collapse and is subtracted before task-error scoring.

At deadline $t_n+0.25$ s, every active client finalizes due sequence $n$ and
advances its expected sequence to $n+1$ whether the outcome was accepted or
missing. A later record for a finalized sequence is stale. Queueing never
changes this finalization clock.

### Client service and release timing

Unless an arm overrides routing explicitly, client $j$ owns one deterministic
FCFS worker of 4096 logical operations per second. Direct-reference work is
enqueued on acquisition, completes after all earlier work plus $O_{j,n}$, and
releases the producer slot at
$\max\{t_{\mathrm{pub}}+h_{j,n},t_{\mathrm{complete}}\}$. The output uses the
acquired record, not whatever later occupies the slot.

Unless an arm freezes a different capture path, B-SNAPSHOT, B-ACTOR,
B-BACKPRESSURE, B-SUBSTRATE, B-PRIVATE, and B-REPLICA use this common immutable-
capture rule. At publication stage 11, process clients in ascending ID order.
On the producer worker, first pay one common sequence/version/deadline
comparison. Capacity admission counts every pending capture descriptor and
completed downstream entry; an accepted descriptor immediately reserves 96 B
of destination capacity under the 1 MiB cap. A capacity rejection pays one
32 B telemetry-record charge, creates no reference or descriptor, and leaves
that due output in the denominator.

The default capture TTL is the absolute time $t_{\mathrm{pub}}+20$ s, measured
from the source record's actual publication time. It limits only completion of
the source-to-destination capture; after successful handoff, the destination's
own mailbox, transform, client-work, or release law applies. B-SNAPSHOT also
uses that instant as its destination expiry unless a public tuning grid freezes
a shorter TTL. C-ADAPT and the pre-225 explicit-filter fan-out keep their
separately declared TTL origins. No other inheriting arm may substitute a
horizon-length or admission-relative TTL.

An accepted request synchronously acquires the exact source record, then
allocates, initializes, and enqueues one 24 B descriptor containing source
identity, due sequence, destination owner, and TTL. Descriptor allocation costs
8 operations and writes an 8 B allocator record; initialization costs 3
operations and writes 24 B; enqueue/dequeue use the common queue-entry charge;
descriptor release costs 8 operations and writes an 8 B allocator record. The
producer worker executes and times the comparison, reference acquisition,
descriptor creation, enqueue, and rejection record. Thus acquisition occurs
before any later producer reuse and cannot be an uncharged atomic shadow.

Each client has one permanently assigned FCFS capture worker at 4096 logical
operations per second unless a protocol explicitly varies that rate. It
dequeues the descriptor, executes the frozen 16-operation destination
allocation plus 24-operation complete-record copy, then pays the common source-
reference release and descriptor-release charges. The 24 B descriptor and
reserved 96 B destination count in occupancy and peak bytes from admission;
after physical allocation, the reservation is replaced by the actual 96 B and
is never double-counted. Completion hands the immutable destination to the
arm's ordinary client, mailbox, backpressure, transform, or replica service and
pays any applicable handoff enqueue charge. The source is pinned throughout
queue residence and copy service, but later downstream work owns only the
destination. TTL, detachment, crash, or version cancellation stops in-service
capture work at the event time, charges completed operations, releases the
source and descriptor, releases any partial destination, and removes the
reservation before later FCFS work. B-RING, C-ADAPT, telemetry capture, and the
pre-225 B-EXPLICIT-FILTER source fan-out use their separately frozen rules.

For B-SNAPSHOT, the common capture worker is its permanently assigned FCFS
staging worker, so the frozen 40 allocation-plus-copy operations and every
descriptor operation are queued and timed explicitly. Its accepted common
descriptor holds the exact source producer record until copy completion,
cancellation, or TTL expiry; the common release charge then applies.
The producer slot therefore experiences bounded copy-time pinning but never the
later consumer-work or consumer-retention interval. Client work enters only
after its immutable copy completes. The consumer-owned copy is released after
work completion or its TTL, whichever occurs first. TTL expiry removes queued
work; if service has started, it stops at expiry and charges only completed
scalar operations. The output is stale and no worker-local shadow copy is
allowed. For B-RING, the client applies the frozen two-header rule below; no
atomic shadow copy or implicit retry is available.
B-ACTOR and B-BACKPRESSURE enqueue their immutable record and include
$O_{j,n}$ in the declared mailbox or queue worker service. Transform-service
arms complete $w_{j,n}$ before the ordinary client work begins. Output latency
is completion time minus the immutable publication due time. All direct
release, copy, queue, transform, and client-work events are retained even when
the output later misses its deadline.

### Client detachment and rejoin law

The canonical ordinary client set is IDs 1 through 16. At a scheduled
detachment, a client immediately releases every live direct reference with the
common charge; queued copies, transforms, mailbox entries, and client jobs are
cancelled and their owned records released. A job already in service stops at
that timestamp and charges its completed logical operations and bytes. An
output with due time strictly before detachment remains in the denominator and
is unavailable if it did not complete; no output is due while the client is
inactive. At rejoin, its first expected sequence is the first publication due
at or after the join timestamp. It receives no backlog and no timestamp is
shifted. These rules apply to every arm, including snapshot, actor, ring,
replica, filter, and direct-reference paths. The event order below decides all
equal-time cases.

### Event order

At equal timestamps, process events in this frozen order:

1. expire snapshot TTLs and release normally scheduled direct references;
2. apply scheduled client detachments and joins in ascending client-ID order,
   then any registered lifecycle mode switch such as the RIN-T10 filter bypass;
3. complete pending copies;
4. complete pending consumer and transform service, creating any valid
   feedback messages in ascending client-ID order;
5. complete pending feedback-message service and deliver its corrections;
6. complete C-ADAPT controller decisions and immediately execute their frozen
   direct, snapshot, or abstain route;
7. record process crash, restart, or version change;
8. deliver the next input event;
9. update the producer if its arm permits, applying already delivered
   feedback after the ordinary update;
10. publish, apply the arm's block/drop/queue rule, or enqueue a C-ADAPT
    decision request;
11. let clients acquire an immediately routed publication;
12. enqueue telemetry;
13. score deadlines; and
14. append the event record.

Changing this order creates a new generator version.

The 300 s world is half-open for creation: due sequences are exactly
$n\in\{0,\ldots,5999\}$ with $t_n<300$ s. At exactly 300 s, process only
completions, releases, watchdogs, invalidations, and deadlines already
scheduled by earlier work in the common order; do not create sequence 6000, a
new subscription, a periodic telemetry sample, or a routine checkpoint. Record
the terminal resource snapshot before artificial horizon disposal, then cancel
and release remaining work with full cleanup charges. Horizon cleanup cannot
create an accepted output or extend a deadline.

### Telemetry service and reporter lifecycle

A telemetry sample has an immutable due timestamp from its registered clock and
total payload $P\in\{32,64,512,4096,1048576\}$ B. RIN-T04 uses the 64 B,
4096 B, and 1 MiB subset; RIN-T07 uses 32 B and 512 B. For the
producer-output, latest-client-output, and total-live-state maps respectively,
the observation read is

$$
b_o\in\{64,40,64(1+L)+8\}\ \mathrm{B},
$$

where $L$ is the exact count of distinct live buffered 96 B versions at the
sample's due time, excluding the separately selected current producer record;
the extra 8 B stores that count. Multiple clients holding the same physical
record do not increase $L$. The 40 B client read is one binary64 scalar plus
32 B identity metadata from the immutable raw client-completion event already
retained for analysis. This is a non-owning event-log view, not a separately
allocated latest-output slot; a newer completion appends another event but does
not mutate the frozen older identity. A missing client value generates the
same-size missing marker inside the reporter job. Because producer and buffered records store state rather than an output
scalar, map derivation additionally costs

$$
q_o\in\{8,0,9(1+L)\}
$$

logical operations respectively: eight operations derive each state output,
then the total-live mean uses $L$ additions and one division. One telemetry job
charges

$$
O_{\mathrm{tel}}(P,b_o,q_o)
=
24+q_o+\left\lceil\frac{b_o}{8}\right\rceil
+2\left\lceil\frac{P}{8}\right\rceil
$$

logical operations, reads $b_o+P$ B, writes $P$ B, and retains $P+24$ B while
materialized. The second payload pass is the checksum. Queue-entry allocation,
enqueue, dequeue, release, and telemetry metadata use the common table and are
additive. Every reporter worker is deterministic FCFS at 4096 logical
operations per second; rates and payloads therefore create real queueing rather
than nominal labels.

At telemetry stage 12, freeze the observation-set identity after same-time
publication and acquisition: producer-output selects the current completed
producer record, latest-client selects that client's current output record,
and total-live selects the current producer record plus every distinct live
buffered record, ordered by due sequence, version, owner, then slot. A latest-
client selection instead records the immutable event-log identity and owns no
source pin. The set,
$L$, and a digest of its identities enter the raw telemetry record. Logical TTL
and client acceptance still expire on schedule even if telemetry temporarily
prevents physical reclamation.

Producer-owned synchronous telemetry executes the complete job on the producer
worker and delays later updates. B-SNAPSHOT telemetry pins every pin-capable
physical record in the frozen observation set from the due time until one dedicated
reporter staging worker has copied all $b_o$ bytes, computed the map, and
materialized the reporter-owned payload, then releases every pin in the frozen
order. Bytes retained past logical expiry remain charged, but an expired record
cannot become an accepted client output. B-ACTOR telemetry synchronously copies
only the frozen $b_o$-byte observation into its bounded mailbox at the due time and
serializes the $P$-byte payload on its actor worker. Disconnected work-matched
telemetry substitutes deterministic filler for the observation while replaying
the same due clock, $O_{\mathrm{tel}}$ including $q_o$, bytes, allocation, queue, and service
class; it cannot hold a producer or client record.

Because $1048576+24$ B exceeds the common 1 MiB interface cap, every 1 MiB
payload is an intentional overload-boundary cell: capped arms reject it at
admission, keep the scheduled sample in the denominator, and report the full
attempt and rejection charges. No arm may silently enlarge the cap or omit the
descriptor.

RIN-T04's reporter-crash intervention occurs at 150 s and restarts at 155 s
with reporter version incremented by one. A job in service stops at the crash
and charges completed operations; queued jobs and reporter-owned records are
invalidated and pay normal release plus one 32 B invalidation record each.
Before invalidating those jobs, every in-service or queued B-SNAPSHOT reporter
job releases and charges every source pin in its frozen observation set in the
registered record order. Those source records remain owned by their producer or
buffer; aborting a reporter job cannot leak the telemetry references or convert
logical expiry into accepted service.
Samples due in $[150,155)$ are unavailable, remain in the due denominator, and
are not replayed. Restart charges 64 logical operations, reads the last 64 B
sequence/version checkpoint, writes one 32 B restart record, and resumes at the
next original clock tick with empty queues. Producer state and input clocks are
unchanged.

### Checkpoint and restart law

At $t=0$ and every positive multiple of 5 s, after the event record is
appended, an available producer writes a checkpoint. If $L$ publication slots
are occupied and $Q_c$ interface jobs are queued, its size is
$96+32L+24Q_c+256\mathbf 1[\text{C-ADAPT}]$ B and its write charge is
$24+8(L+Q_c)+64\mathbf 1[\text{C-ADAPT}]$ logical operations. The checkpoint
contains producer state, next logical/input/due sequences, version,
slot ownership, queued-job identities, and adaptive state. A process that is
unavailable at the checkpoint timestamp writes nothing.

For C-ADAPT, $L$ includes `pending-controller` slots and $Q_c$ includes queued
or in-service controller descriptors. The 256 B adaptive block stores the
controller availability time, frozen EWMA values, pending sequence/version/due
identities, issued route state, and staging workloads visible to each completed
decision. Resume cannot recompute a decision from later queue state.

Whenever a registered schedule makes the producer process unavailable, its
C-ADAPT controller worker and C-ADAPT staging workers enter the same failure
domain. At the crash event, after earlier equal-time completions, in-service
controller or copy work stops and charges only completed operations; queued
descriptors, pending-controller slots, queued copies, and their references are
cancelled, released, and given one 32 B invalidation record each. No controller
decision or C-ADAPT copy completes during downtime. Restart replay restores the
producer state, sequence state, and causal EWMA history needed to audit those
cancellations, but it neither recomputes an old decision nor redelivers an old
client output. After replay and version increment, controller and staging
availability clocks are set to the declared restart-completion timestamp with
empty queues; the next post-restart due input is the first work they may accept.

Each replayable input, ownership, release, queue, or feedback event writes one
48 B append-only log record at 8 logical operations during normal running.
Restart reads and charges the complete latest checkpoint at
$12+4(L+Q_c)+32\mathbf 1[\text{C-ADAPT}]$ logical operations, then reads each
post-checkpoint event record at 8 operations and 48 B. It replays records in
original event order and charges 192 additional producer operations per
replayed update. An input whose due timestamp lies inside declared process
downtime is logged as unavailable and reconstructs no producer update; it is
never shifted into replay. Replay reconstructs state but does not redeliver
historical client outputs. Only after replay does the producer increment its
version, invalidate older downstream records, and accept new work. Thus the
T06 crash at 210 s restores the 205 s checkpoint, while the T07 crash at 220 s
restores the 215 s checkpoint; checkpoint bytes, log bytes, reads, writes,
replay operations, and invalidation are all charged.

### Frozen hostile service schedule

RIN-T06 alone uses the following 300 s schedule; no other protocol may inherit
it implicitly. RIN-T07 instead uses its complete ten-episode table below. All
interval boundaries are half-open and event ties obey the common event order.

| Time (s) | Frozen event law |
| ---: | --- |
| $[0,30)$ | Nominal common generator. |
| $[30,90)$ | Consumer and transform requests created during each one-second block are released together at the block's closing timestamp, ordered first by producer sequence and then client ID. Direct-reference holds use the truncated Pareto law below. |
| $[90,120)$ | Odd-numbered clients cancel at 90 s and create no new subscriptions. Correct cleanup releases their live references at 90 s; the fault intervention withholds those releases until its watchdog at 110 s. All clients rejoin at 120 s. |
| $[120,150)$ | Nominal service resumes with the same input history and stream positions. |
| $[150,180)$ | Every 32nd scheduled direct-reference release, counted globally from sequence zero then client ID, is withheld until exactly 20 s after its scheduled release. |
| $[180,210)$ | Nominal service resumes; delayed references retain their original expiry events. |
| $[210,212)$ | The producer process is unavailable. Due inputs and outputs stay in their denominators and are marked unavailable, not shifted. |
| $[212,240)$ | Restart from the last committed producer state at or before 210 s, increment producer version, invalidate every older queued or copied record, and charge replay and invalidation work. |
| $[240,300)$ | Nominal service with the restarted version; no hostile fault is silently repaired. |

During the heavy-tail interval, a fresh `hold` uniform $U\in[0,1)$ gives

$$
h^{\mathrm{hostile}}_{j,n}
=
\min\left\{20\ \mathrm{s},
(0.25\ \mathrm{s})(1-U)^{-1/1.5}\right\}.
$$

The same scheduled hold and fault events are supplied to every paired arm,
including arms that do not act on a direct reference. Cleanup performs 64
logical operations and writes one 32 B telemetry record per recovered
reference. A missing recovery event, changed subscription denominator, or
post-hoc extension of the horizon invalidates the world.

### Shared-resource load

The disconnected work-matched load receives no producer output and cannot pin
a producer slot. For every connected consumer event it replays the same
logical-operation count, read bytes, write bytes, allocation count, and
service-duration draw on the registered shared worker. In exclusive placement,
the replay runs on a separate simulated worker and cannot delay the producer.
In shared placement, producer and replay events use deterministic
first-come-first-served service.

One producer update costs exactly 192 logical operations. The generic shared
worker and each exclusive counterpart process 4096 logical operations per
second; arrivals tie-break by due timestamp, producer-before-replay class,
producer sequence, and client ID. A producer update delayed by the worker
still consumes its immutable scheduled $u_n$ and executes in logical sequence
order. It changes state only on service completion, publishes with that actual
completion timestamp, and retains the original due timestamp for latency and
miss scoring. Queued updates are neither merged nor skipped. Thus $Q$ can
change wall-time service and time-indexed observations but cannot silently
change the input sequence or logical transition law.

The match is checked from observed event totals. A relative mismatch above
0.5% on operations or bytes invalidates the paired world.

### Pathway-specific transform service

This control always consumes an immutable 96 B publication snapshot. Its
common acquisition may pin the producer record only until snapshot completion;
the later transform request never owns or extends that producer reference.
Each client requests

$$
w_{j,n}
=
64+8d+\left\lfloor32e^{0.4Z^{v}_{j,n}}\right\rfloor
$$

logical scalar operations, where $Z^{v}_{j,n}$ is an independent
seed-drawn standard-normal variate. Each server processes 4096 logical
operations per second. The primary shared-versus-private causal comparison
fixes $K=N$: the shared arm uses one FCFS queue feeding $N$ identical servers,
while the private arm assigns one identical server permanently to each client.
Server count, per-request service law, and aggregate nameplate capacity are
therefore equal. Cells with $K\ne N$ are capacity-sensitivity diagnostics and
cannot identify a $V$ effect. Queue and server metadata plus idle reserved
capacity are charged. The producer runs on an exclusive worker in both arms.
Client one exposes the
most recent completed sequence at each due step; an older sequence is stale,
and no completed sequence is missing.

This is a pathway-specific finite-service null, not generic CPU contention and
not a biochemical reproduction. It may change client latency, staleness,
accepted service, and $D_C$. Any change in producer logical state or nonzero
$D_U$ beyond numerical tolerance invalidates the arm.

### Intended feedback channel

When enabled, client $j$ returns a signed correction
$g_{j,n}\in[-0.01,0.01]$ NSU after completing sequence $n$. Let
$z^{\mathrm{corr}}_{j,n}$ be the completed client output after subtracting its
fixed $0.001j$ NOU offset. The causal reference is
$r_n=(1\ \mathrm{NOU\,NIU^{-1}})u_n$, and

$$
g_{j,n}
=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-0.01,0.01]\ \mathrm{NSU}}
\left[
(0.02\ \mathrm{NSU\,NOU^{-1}})
(r_n-z^{\mathrm{corr}}_{j,n})
\right].
$$

Invalid, missing, or deadline-expired client output creates no feedback
message. A valid message is created at client completion, occupies 48 B,
costs 32 logical operations, and enters one exclusive FCFS feedback server of
4096 logical operations per second. Equal-time messages enter in ascending
client-ID order, so delivery occurs after the message's own service completes.
The producer sums all corrections delivered before the next logical step,
clips the sum to $[-0.02,0.02]$ NSU, and adds it equally to its eight state
components after the ordinary update. Feedback generation is deterministic
and consumes no random stream; adding a `feedback` PRNG scope is therefore
prohibited in this version. Every log record contains producer sequence,
client ID, creation and delivery times, value, 48 written bytes, and 32
operations. Replaying the log with reads disabled and disabling the log while
reads continue are mandatory interventions.

### Frozen ordinary-interface and candidate laws

These are synthetic benchmark laws, not empirical constants or recommended
production defaults. One publication record is exactly 96 B: eight binary64
state values (64 B) and 32 B of sequence, timestamp, version, and integrity
metadata. The following logical charges are additive and are never converted
to joules:

| Operation | Logical operations | Bytes read | Bytes written |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| allocate or release one 96 B record | 16 | 0 | 16 |
| initialize or overwrite one record | 12 | 0 | 96 |
| acquire or release one direct reference | 8 | 16 | 16 |
| copy one complete record | 24 | 96 | 96 |
| enqueue or dequeue one queue entry | 8 | 16 | 16 |
| compare sequence, version, and deadline | 3 | 24 | 0 |
| emit one 32 B telemetry record | 12 | 16 | 32 |

The fixture freezes each arm as follows:

1. **B-RING.** Capacity is selected from $c\in\{1,4,16,64\}$. The producer
   writes sequence $n$ to slot $n\bmod c$ after paying one record overwrite.
   Every client stores its next expected sequence. When its staging worker
   reaches a read, it first pays the common comparison charge and reads the
   slot's sequence, version, deadline, and integrity header. A larger sequence
   marks every skipped sequence stale; a smaller sequence is unavailable, and
   either mismatch performs no allocation or copy. A matching header starts a
   charged 96 B destination allocation and complete-record copy without
   pinning the ring slot. At copy completion, before delivery, the worker pays
   a second common comparison charge and rereads that source header. The copy
   is accepted only when both headers are identical, integrity-valid, and equal
   the expected sequence and version. Any intervening overwrite makes the
   exact due output stale: discard and release the destination after charging
   all attempted allocation, copy, bytes, service time, and comparisons. There
   is no retry. Equal-time copy completion precedes producer overwrite under
   the common event order. This seqlock-style validation prevents a torn record
   from becoming accepted while preserving the ring's no-pin behavior.
2. **B-SNAPSHOT.** Every subscribed client receives a separately allocated
   immutable 96 B copy. The copy expires 20 s after publication whether or not
   it was read; expiry pays one release charge and follows the common
   cancel-at-expiry work law. Copy/allocation service uses the common $N$
   client-assigned staging workers. Copy completion after the 0.25 s service
   deadline remains late and cannot be relabelled as accepted. A snapshot copy
   holds its exact producer source only from descriptor enqueue through copy
   completion, cancellation, or TTL expiry, with common acquire/release
   charges; consumer work cannot extend that pin. In every comparison with C-ADAPT, the
   static snapshot null is tuned over TTLs $\{0.25,1,4,20\}$ s with the same
   public trials and selection rule; its best frozen TTL enters confirmation.
3. **B-ACTOR.** Each client owns one FCFS mailbox with capacity
   $c\in\{1,4,16,64\}$. An arrival to a full mailbox rejects the newest
   message under the common pre-copy admission rule and records a dropped
   output. Pending capture descriptors reserve mailbox entries until their
   immutable 96 B copies replace them; one client worker serves completed
   messages at 4096 logical operations per second
   using the frozen $O_{j,n}$ demand. Enqueue, dequeue, allocation, copy, and
   release charges all apply.
4. **B-BACKPRESSURE.** One immutable-copy FCFS queue feeding $N$ identical
   4096-operation-per-second workers uses a public pair
   $(L,H)\in\{(0,1),(1,3),(4,12)\}$. Admission closes immediately when queue
   occupancy reaches $H$ and reopens only after capture completion, service
   completion, dequeue, or cancellation leaves occupancy at most $L$. While
   closed, new client deliveries are rejected newest and counted as
   dropped before source acquisition. Queue length includes accepted capture
   descriptors, completed immutable messages, and in-service entries so copy
   latency cannot evade the watermarks. Producer progress may still experience
   the common bounded capture pin but never waits on later queue service. The comparison therefore retains
   the common $N$-server nameplate capacity while testing queue/admission law.
5. **B-RESERVE.** Public development fixes the compared candidate's peak
   retained interface bytes $B_{\max}$, worker-class reservation vector and
   rates, $C_{\mathrm{prov}}$, executed-operation allowance, and maximum
   transform demand in any closed one-second window
   $O_{\max}^{1\mathrm{s}}$. Confirmation reserves
   $S_{\mathrm{res}}=\max\{S,\lceil B_{\max}/96\rceil\}$ direct slots and
   $K_{\mathrm{res}}=\max\{N,\lceil O_{\max}^{1\mathrm{s}}/4096\rceil\}$
   identical servers, plus an idle matched worker for every additional
   candidate controller, staging, feedback, reporter, filter, recovery, or
   replica class at the same rate and provisioned duration, subject to the
   common 1 MiB cap. All reserved bytes and reference-worker seconds are
   reported, including idle capacity; a cap violation invalidates rather than
   silently downsizes the arm.
6. **C-ADAPT.** When update $n$ completes at $c_n$, freeze due sequence $n$,
   version, original due time $t_n$, and causal exponentially weighted means
   $\bar q_n$, $\bar s_n$, and $\bar r_n$ of slot-occupancy fraction,
   client deadline slack in seconds, and timely-release indicator using
   $\bar v_n=(1-\lambda)\bar v_{n-1}+\lambda v_n$. The finite public grid is
   $\lambda\in\{1/8,1/4,1/2\}$,
   $q_0\in\{0.5,0.75,0.9\}$,
   $s_0\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ s,
   $r_0\in\{0.9,0.99\}$, and snapshot TTL
   $T_{\mathrm{TTL}}\in\{0.25,1,4\}$ s. Initialize
   $(\bar q_0,\bar s_0,\bar r_0)=(0,0.25\ \mathrm{s},1)$. For later steps,
   $q_n$ is occupancy immediately before publication; $s_n$ is the minimum
   deadline-minus-completion slack among outputs due in the prior producer
   interval, with a missing output assigned $-0.25$ s; and $r_n=1$ exactly
   when every direct release scheduled in that interval occurred on time.
   If a producer slot is free, write the post-update record into that slot,
   mark it `pending-controller`, and count it immediately in occupancy and peak
   bytes. If none is free, producer state still advances and the decision job
   is still charged, but sequence $n$ has no record and must abstain when the
   job completes. One decision costs exactly 128 logical operations on a
   dedicated 4096-operation-per-second FCFS controller worker. If
   $A_n^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is that worker's availability time, its completion is

   $$
   t_{\mathrm{decision},n}
   =
   \max\{c_n,A_n^{\mathrm{ctrl}}\}
   +\frac{128}{4096}\ \mathrm{s}.
   $$

   No client can acquire a pending record before this time. For client $j$,
   predicted snapshot completion is
   $t^{\mathrm{pred}}_{j,n}=t_{\mathrm{decision},n}+(W^{\mathrm{ahead}}_{j,n}+40)/4096$ s, where
   $W^{\mathrm{ahead}}_{j,n}$ is unfinished
   allocation/copy work already queued on that client's staging worker; future
   arrivals are not used. At $t_{\mathrm{decision},n}$, clients acquire the
   pending direct record only
   when $\bar q_n\le q_0$, $\bar s_n\ge s_0$, and $\bar r_n\ge r_0$; otherwise
   it enqueues immutable copies from that record only if storage stays within
   the cap, every $t^{\mathrm{pred}}_{j,n}\le t_n+0.25$ s, and every predicted
   completion precedes
   $t_{\mathrm{decision},n}+T_{\mathrm{TTL}}$. Each copy holds a reference to
   the pending producer slot until it completes or is cancelled; the slot is
   released only after the last such reference. If no route qualifies, release
   the pending slot and record an abstention. Controller queue residence and
   copy time count from the original due timestamp $t_n$; future arrivals never
   revise an issued route. Public tuning
   first maximizes accepted service, then minimizes $D_U$, then operations,
   peak bytes, and written bytes lexicographically. Missing state or a
   non-finite update invokes B-SNAPSHOT with its 20 s TTL and full charges.
7. **B-REPLICA.** Client $j$ owns one producer with the common frozen initial
   state, input sequence, 192-operation update, and 4096-operation-per-second
   worker. Its output is delivered through an immutable 96 B snapshot and no
   producer state or queue is shared between clients. A crash, restart,
   version change, checkpoint, or warm-up is executed and charged separately
   for every replica. The arm therefore uses $N$ producer workers, $N$ state
   records, and $N$ times the logical update work; idle worker-seconds and all
   duplicated bytes remain explicit. It is a high-capacity ordinary null, not
   an insulation claim.

## Equal information, authority, and budgets

1. All non-oracle arms receive byte-identical inputs, timestamps, client
   subscriptions, hold-time draws, service draws, crash events, and deadlines.
2. Arms have the same producer equations and numerical precision. A different
   producer state is an outcome, not a hidden architecture change.
3. Every arm is capped at one simulated producer worker, 1 MiB interface
   storage, and the same 300 s logical horizon. Each registered comparison
   receives the same count of consumer servers at 4096 logical operations per
   second per server; adding a server is a charged capacity intervention.
   B-REPLICA is the sole declared producer-worker exception and reports all
   $N$ workers and duplicated work rather than entering equal-capacity claims.
   Staging, controller, transform, filter, reporter, feedback, replica, and
   reserved workers are never free parallelism. For worker $k$ provisioned at
   rate $\nu_k$ logical operations per second over duration $T_k$, report
   $C_{\mathrm{prov}}=\sum_k(\nu_k/4096)T_k$ reference-worker seconds, plus
   peak concurrent worker count, active reference-worker seconds, and idle
   reference-worker seconds. Every dominance comparison includes
   $C_{\mathrm{prov}}$; a rate multiplier changes it even when operation count
   is unchanged.
4. A reserved-resource arm receives the same peak memory, worker-class
   reservation vector, worker rates, $C_{\mathrm{prov}}$, and executed-
   operation allowance observed for the compared candidate in public
   development. Idle matched workers remain charged.
5. Copy, allocation, queue, admission, expiry, acknowledgement, tracing,
   restart, recovery, and artifact work are charged to the arm that uses them.
6. Dropped and stale outputs remain task outcomes. They cannot be removed from
   denominators.
7. No confirmation label, future hold time, future input, or evaluator state

8. The same tuning-trial count and public worlds apply to every tunable arm.
9. A support gate can abstain only before a deadline or integrity violation

10. Energy remains unavailable. No operation-to-joule conversion is allowed.

## Measurements, units, and decision margins

Primary trajectory endpoints are $D_U$ and $D_C$, defined in the
mathematical contract. The frozen output scales are the interquartile ranges
of the isolated public-development trajectory for the same input family. A
zero scale invalidates the family rather than adding an epsilon.

All artificial trajectory and transition-time endpoints use the exogenous due
grid $t_n=n\Delta t$. At each $t_n$, the observed producer value is the latest
state whose update service completed no later than $t_n$, held constant from
its completion; before publication sequence zero completes it is the
non-published initial scalar
$y_{\mathrm{init}}=(h_y/8)\sum_i x_{0,i}$. Delayed, blocked, or queued
updates are therefore not sequence-realigned. $D_U$ compares these
sample-and-hold values on the common due grid, and $t_{20}$/$t_{50}$ are the
first due-grid samples crossing their threshold, without interpolation.
Client latency and acceptance retain the same original due timestamp. A
logical-sequence-aligned trace may be reported only as a labeled diagnostic.

For $D_C$, evaluate client one for every sequence $n$ at its fixed deadline

## Common statistical analysis plan

1. Deterministic biological-source cells enumerate every registered parameter
   and heterogeneous-client offset exactly once. They use exhaustive numerical
   tolerance, sign, support, and relevance gates; they receive no bootstrap,
   p-value, seed replication, or Holm decision.
2. Stochastic artificial development uses 30 paired seeds per registered grid
   cell. These runs tune arms and estimate a sampling distribution but remain
   **NO_RESULT**.
3. Every stochastic draw uses the PCG64-DXSM stream grammar and draw transforms
   frozen in the common generator. The literal confirmation seed list and its
   SHA-256 digest must be committed before any confirmation run.
4. A separately generated confirmation seed pack is encrypted or committed by
   digest before reveal. Its replicate count is derived from public paired
   variance and a frozen minimum relevant effect; this prose does not invent
   power.
5. Use seed-level paired aggregates for stochastic artificial tracks. Events within a seed are not independent
   confirmation replicates.
6. The primary location estimator is the symmetric 20% trimmed mean of
   seed-level paired differences: sort the $R_s$ differences, remove
   $q=\lfloor0.2R_s\rfloor$ values from each tail, and average the remainder.
   The median is a secondary robustness report and cannot replace a failed
   primary estimator.
7. A stochastic protocol may contain multiple primary scalar components; it is not
   collapsed to one physical-unit mixture. Before confirmation, its component
   registry freezes endpoint, arm contrast, direction, relevance margin, and
   every required stratum. From exactly 20,000 synchronous paired-seed
   bootstrap resamples, compute the maximum absolute component deviation after
   dividing each component by its own nonzero relevance margin. The 0.9975
   quantile supplies one simultaneous two-sided critical value for that
   protocol; the ten protocol families are therefore Bonferroni-protected at
   familywise 95%. Labeled secondary strata receive ordinary 0.025/0.975
   intervals. Orient every paired difference so positive supports the
   alternative and subtract its superiority or noninferiority boundary before
   analysis. If $b_r$ is that boundary-centred value and $\widehat\theta$ its
   trimmed mean, create the primary null distribution by resampling
   $b_r-\widehat\theta$ with replacement exactly 20,000 times; the one-sided
   add-one p-value is
   $(1+\#\{\widehat\theta_b^{0}\ge\widehat\theta\})/20001$. A paired sign-flip
   result is only a labeled sensitivity analysis when the generator and
   preregistered symmetry diagnostic justify boundary exchangeability; it
   never substitutes for the primary bootstrap. Combine components by the
   frozen rules below, producing at most nine stochastic protocol-level
   p-values, then apply Holm step-down across the p-values actually registered
   before seed reveal at familywise $\alpha=0.05$.
8. Task and integrity noninferiority are tested before distortion or resource
   superiority.
9. For an insulation arm, the preregistered practical $D_U$ margin is 0.05
   dimensionless units and the accepted-service noninferiority margin is one
   percentage point. They are decision thresholds, not natural constants.
10. Promotion also requires non-domination on $D_U$, $D_C$, accepted
   service, p99 latency, operations, peak bytes, written bytes,
   $C_{\mathrm{prov}}$, and peak concurrent workers.
11. Every invalid, abstained, and failed transfer world is reported. No
    post-hoc stratum deletion is permitted.

For component p-values $p_1,\ldots,p_m$, `all` is the intersection--union
value $\max_k p_k$ and `any` is the Bonferroni union value
$\min\{1,m\min_k p_k\}$. Gates are evaluated first but remain registered
components; a failed gate forces protocol failure. The protocol combiners are:

## Ablations, interventions, and global kill rules

The package is rejected as an artificial mechanism or retired to ordinary
systems terminology if any of these occurs:

1. connection distortion vanishes under exclusive resources and valid clock
   alignment;
2. disconnected work-matched load reproduces the complete effect;
3. the finite pathway-specific transform arm changes producer logical state
   despite immutable snapshots and exclusive placement;
4. private transform servers fail to remove a claimed substrate-service effect
   under identical snapshot demand;
5. ideal immutable snapshots still change producer logical state without a
   declared path;
6. the result depends on granting one arm more input, hidden state, tuning,
   authority, memory, or uncharged work;
7. ordinary ring buffers, queues, process isolation, reservation, or
   overprovisioning match or dominate C-ADAPT;
8. lower producer distortion is purchased with a protected integrity failure
   or unacceptable client service;
9. the benefit exists only at one client count, one input frequency, or one
   hold-time interpolation cell;
10. a claimed sign-sensitive effect is ordinary symmetric delay;
11. intended gradients or acknowledgements explain an effect assigned to
    direct sequestration;
12. an intentionally useful coupling is promoted without target and protected
    service noninferiority to the explicit filter plus strict improvement on
    at least one fully charged resource axis;
13. a logical-operation result is reported as energy; or
14. raw events cannot deterministically reconstruct ownership, ordering,
    state, work, and scoring.

## RIN-T08 — Weak-coupling counterexample and service boundary

- **Evidence target:** RIN-E08 / C-1557
- **Result:** **NO_RESULT**
- **Question:** can reduced coupling lower distortion and work, and where do
  bandwidth or leak failures reverse that advantage?
- **Generator:** direct-hold coupling multiplier
  $\alpha\in\{1/16,1/8,1/4,1/2,1,2,4\}$, sine frequency
  $f\in\{0.01,0.05,0.5,2\}$ Hz, unreleased-reference probability per
  scheduled direct-reference release
  $\ell\in\{0,10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$, and burst input. Run the complete
  $N,S,r_h\in\{1,4,16\}^3$ grid for every sine cell and every burst cell; burst

- **Units:** $\alpha$ and $\ell$ are dimensionless; $f$ is in hertz.
- **Coupling and leak law:** for A-DIRECT-HOLD only, apply
  $h_{\alpha,j,n}=\min\{20\tau_p,\max(\Delta t,\alpha h_{j,n})\}$.
  Independently for every scheduled direct-reference release, the `fault`

  the charged watchdog fires exactly 20 s after scheduled release.
  B-SNAPSHOT is the bounded-copy reference control and is evaluated once per
  paired cell. It never receives $\ell$: its temporary source references obey
  deterministic copy completion/cancellation, while the leak intervention
  targets scheduled A-DIRECT-HOLD releases only. A-ISO remains the no-client
  trajectory reference.
- **Frozen decision strata:** weak coupling means
  $\alpha\in\{1/16,1/8,1/4,1/2\}$. A slow-clean cell has sine
  $f\in\{0.01,0.05\}$ Hz and $\ell=0$. A primary fast boundary has sine
  $f\in\{0.5,2\}$ Hz and $\ell=0$; a primary leak boundary has
  $\ell\in\{10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ under any registered sine or burst input.

  cell. `boundary-exposed` is true only when at least one registered fast or
  leak cell makes that same tuple either dominated, worse than both
  $\alpha=1$ and B-SNAPSHOT by more than 0.01 in tracking-error fraction, worse
  by more than one percentage point in accepted service, or above 0.01 in
  $U_{\mathrm{slot}}$. Simultaneous uncertainty and the common relevance
  margins apply to every Boolean; point estimates alone cannot set it.
- **Tracking-error fraction:** for each ordinary client $j$ and every one of
  its due sequences with $10\le t_n<300$ s, score zero only when the exact
  sequence is integrity-valid, completes within 0.25 s, and after offset
  subtraction is within 0.05 NOU of the corresponding isolated logical-step
  output $y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}$. An unavailable, inaccurate, late, stale,
  dropped, duplicated, or invalid output scores one. Divide by that client's
  complete due-sequence count and take the maximum across clients; no
  completion-only denominator or client pooling may replace this primary
  fraction.
- **Unrecovered-slot endpoint:** after common event-order release processing at
  each post-warm-up due time, let $L_n^{\mathrm{leak}}$ be the number of
  distinct producer slots with at least one withheld reference that has not yet
  been released by the watchdog. The primary fraction is

  $$
U_{\mathrm{slot}}
=
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n\in\mathcal I}\frac{L_n^{\mathrm{leak}}}{S},
  $$

  over $\mathcal I=\{n:10\le t_n<300\ \mathrm{s}\}$, so $M=5800$ for the

  clients on one slot count once.

- **Primary endpoints:** $D_U$, client tracking error, accepted service,
  unrecovered-slot fraction, and operations.
- **Secondary endpoints:** $\omega_B$, bytes, p99 latency, stale fraction,
  recovery work, and later measured joules, currently **NO_RESULT**.
- **Interventions:** inject fast and burst signals; inject unreleased
  references; and replay due service rather than deleting low-rate misses. In
  the no-cleanup diagnostic, a leaked reference remains live through the 300 s
  horizon, all occupied bytes remain charged, and no watchdog work is invented;
  that diagnostic cannot qualify an arm. In the restore-cleanup intervention,
  watchdog processing is disabled on $[0,150)$, then at 150 s scans and releases
  every live leaked reference in slot/client order at the common 64-operation
  plus 32 B telemetry charge per reference; later leaks again use the normal
  20 s watchdog. Neither intervention changes due outputs or input clocks.
- **Pass condition:** at least one preregistered
  $(\alpha,N,S,r_h)$ tuple is both `slow-clean-nondominated` and
  `boundary-exposed` under the frozen predicates above.
- **Kill condition:** a universal energy conclusion is drawn, missing outputs
  are removed from denominators, steady state replaces dynamic tracking, or
  leak recovery is free.

## RIN-T10 — Suppress, preserve, or use back-action

- **Evidence target:** RIN-E10 / C-1559
- **Result:** **NO_RESULT**
- **Question:** for a preregistered temporal objective, should coupling be
  suppressed, preserved, or deliberately used, and how does deliberate use
  compare with an explicit filter?
- **Episode registry:** one cell is one uninterrupted 300 s episode for each
  $(\text{input family},\tau_*,N)$ combination, with frequency added only for
  sine. The input families are upward step, downward step, pulse, sine, and
  burst; $\tau_*\in\{0.25,1,4\}$ s; $N\in\{1,4,16\}$; $S=4$; and
  $r_h=1$. No state, queue, reference, filter, counter, or random stream resets
  at either lifecycle intervention. RIN-T10 replaces the common sine grid with
  public-development frequencies $\{0.02,0.1,0.5\}$ Hz, sealed-confirmation
  frequencies $\{0.05,0.2,1\}$ Hz, and hostile-transfer frequencies
  $\{0.01,2\}$ Hz. The transfer cells are reported separately and cannot enter
  the primary confirmation decision.
- **Lifecycle:** at 150 s the synthetic shaping client detaches. Ordinary
  clients with IDs $2,\ldots,N$ detach at the same timestamp, while ordinary
  client 1 remains subscribed so service and recovery remain observable. When
  $N=1$, only the shaping client detaches. The common detachment law releases
  live references, cancels queued work, charges partial service, and retains
  already-due failures. Because lifecycle events precede same-time input, no
  output is due to a detached client at 150 s. At 225 s all detached ordinary
  clients rejoin before the same-time input, so their first new due output is
  sequence $n=225/\Delta t$. They receive no backlog. The same active-client
  denominator and cleanup law apply to every arm.
- **Target:** the causal exponential response, in NOU:

  $$
  y^*_{n+1}
  =
  e^{-\Delta t/\tau_*}y^*_n
  +
  (1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau_*})y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}.
  $$

  Here $y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}$ is the isolated post-update publication for due
  sequence $n$. Initialize $y^*_0=y^{\mathrm{iso}}_0$ exactly once and apply
  the displayed recurrence after sequence $n$ to create the target state for
  sequence $n+1$; sequence $n$ is scored against the pre-recurrence $y_n^*$.
  Continue the recurrence across both lifecycle interventions. The reference is
  $r_n=y^*_n$ for $t_n<225$ s and $r_n=y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}$ for
  $t_n\ge225$ s. The latter switch is preregistered; it does not reset
  $y^*$, producer state, or filter state.
- **Arms:** suppress with B-SNAPSHOT and C-ADAPT; preserve with
  A-DIRECT-HOLD; use with C-USE; B-EXPLICIT-FILTER; B-RING; B-PRIVATE; and
  B-RESERVE.
- **C-USE law:** a synthetic shaping client holds the one slot containing each
  new publication for $h_{\mathrm{use}}=\tau_*$, capped at 4 s, regardless of
  future input. Other slots keep their own publication identities and release
  times; if none is available, the registered hold-state rule applies. The law
  is causal, fixed before confirmation, and charges every missed or stale
  output. At 150 s it immediately releases every reference it still owns,
  paying the common direct-reference release charge once per live reference;
  all queued and future shaping holds are cancelled. It remains detached after
  the ordinary clients rejoin at 225 s. It performs no ordinary client work,
  and it deliberately uses pinning rather than insulation.
- **Explicit-filter route:** B-EXPLICIT-FILTER receives each isolated 96 B
  producer record through one B-SNAPSHOT ingress with fixed 20 s TTL and one
  dedicated 4096-operation-per-second staging worker. Its dedicated
  4096-operation-per-second FCFS filter worker processes ingress records in due-
  sequence order. Each successful pre-switch emission first creates one
  filter-owned 96 B source record with fixed 20 s TTL. Creating it on the
  filter worker pays the common 16-operation allocation and 12-operation
  initialization charges plus one operation and 8 B read to transfer the
  emitted transient scalar. The source is an explicitly typed
  `filtered-scalar` record with an 8 B NOU scalar, 56 B reserved zeros, and 32 B
  due-sequence/version/timestamp/payload-kind metadata. In ascending client-ID
  order, the filter worker pays one common direct-reference acquisition and
  enqueues one independent B-SNAPSHOT copy for every active ordinary client.
  Each descriptor pins the source until that client's staging copy completes
  or is cancelled; its release uses the common direct-reference charge. The
  filter-owned source pays the common 96 B release immediately after its last
  pin is gone. If its TTL arrives first, cancel every outstanding descriptor in
  client-ID order, pay each reference release, and then release the source.
  Every per-client
  destination still pays ordinary allocation, copy, TTL, cancellation, and
  release charges. Before 225 s only,
  ordinary client work validates that type, consumes the leading scalar
  directly, and then adds its fixed client offset; it does not average the
  seven padding values. The common $O_{j,n}$ work and offset-subtraction rule
  remain unchanged. Ordinary isolated-state records after the bypass use the
  common eight-component mean. Ordinary client work begins only after the
  relevant copy. Ingress and every output allocation, copy, TTL, cancellation,
  release, worker, and client-service charge use the common laws.
- **Explicit-filter state and timing:** at $t=0$, compute
  $a=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_*}$. When ingress sequence zero arrives, initialize the
  state to $y_0^{\mathrm{iso}}$ and emit that current state as target sequence
  zero. For every later pre-switch sequence, emit the already-computed current
  state $y_n^*$; after emitting sequence $n$, apply
  $y^*_{n+1}=y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}+a(y_n^*-y_n^{\mathrm{iso}})$ only when
  $t_{n+1}<225$ s. Thus no output receives the newly computed next state. A
  missing or out-of-order ingress cannot advance the recurrence and is never
  imputed; dependent target outputs remain unavailable until the missing
  sequence arrives, and a TTL-expired gap makes the remaining pre-switch chain
  unavailable. Within one FCFS filter job the order is derive or initialize,
  emit the transient scalar, allocate and initialize the typed source, acquire
  and enqueue client-copy descriptors, then execute the recurrence core. The
  coefficient setup and every derivation, scalar emission, source creation,
  acquisition, and recurrence operation consumes service on the dedicated
  filter worker; these operations therefore determine completion times rather
  than acting as untimed counters. At 225 s, cancel and charge remaining
  ingress/filter work, typed sources, source pins, and queued or in-service
  pre-switch output copies,
  bypass the recurrence, and deliver isolated records directly through the same
  per-client B-SNAPSHOT output route. Coefficient and state remain allocated
  until episode end; there is no reset.
- **Explicit-filter scalar ledger:** for this arm only, allocating or releasing
  one 8 B scalar costs 4 logical operations and writes one 8 B allocator record;
  one scalar read or write costs one logical operation and 8 B read or written.
  Coefficient setup therefore costs 37 operations and 16 B written: 32 for the
  exponent, 4 for allocation, and 1 for the value write. The physical ingress
  has no stored $y_n^{\mathrm{iso}}$: deriving it reads all 64 B of state and
  costs seven additions plus one multiplication by $h_y/8$. Sequence-zero
  first-state initialization therefore costs 13 operations, reads 64 B, and
  writes 16 B: eight derivation operations plus allocation and state write.
  Each emitted pre-switch scalar costs 2 operations, reads 8 B, and writes 8 B.
  Each executed recurrence core costs 3 operations, reads 24 B, and writes 8 B;
  sequences 1 through 4498 additionally pay the 8-operation, 64 B derivation
  before that core. Releasing
  coefficient and state at episode end costs 8 operations and writes 16 B.
  Filter-specific peak live storage is 24 B for coefficient, state, and
  transient output, in addition to every charged filter-source record,
  per-client immutable record, reference, worker queue, and descriptor.
- **Primary endpoint:** for ordinary client $j$, let $\mathcal A_j$ contain
  exactly the due sequences at which that client was active. Use its delivered
  scalar $\widetilde y_{j,n}$ and the common timeliness, sequence, and integrity
  gate. An unavailable, stale, late, duplicate, or invalid sample contributes
  fixed error 10 NOU instead of leaving $\mathcal A_j$; sensitivity uses 2 and
  100 NOU. Compute one RMSE per client and use the maximum across clients as the
  primary target error; report client 1 and the pooled active-client RMSE
  separately. The endpoint is gated by accepted-service noninferiority within
  one percentage point and zero excess integrity failures.
- **Secondary endpoints:** $D_U$, $D_C$, p99 latency, operations, bytes,
  $C_{\mathrm{prov}}$, peak workers, detach recovery in seconds, and error after
  the objective reverts. Detach recovery compares with a paired no-shaping
  counterfactual having the same ordinary active set and is the first time after
  150 s when 20 consecutive client-1 due samples have producer difference at
  most 0.05, accepted-service disadvantage at most 0.01, no live shaping
  reference, and zero excess integrity failures; right-censor at 225 s.
- **Interventions:** detach clients; change $\tau_*$; switch to the isolated
  objective; replace direct holds with immutable explicit filtering; and replay
  identical work disconnected.
- **Pass condition:** each suppress/preserve/use decision is reported by target
  stratum. C-USE may qualify only if its target error is noninferior to
  B-EXPLICIT-FILTER within 0.01 NOU, it loses no protected service, it is no

- **Kill condition:** C-USE wins only by missing due outputs, depends on
