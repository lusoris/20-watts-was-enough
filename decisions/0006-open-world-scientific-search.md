# 0006 — Search all sciences through shared problem classes

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-05

## Context

Useful computational mechanisms need not resemble neurons or originate in a
field already associated with AI. Different sciences repeatedly encounter
allocation, coordination, memory, repair, search, control, and robustness under
hard constraints. Restricting discovery to neuroscience would reproduce the
brain's historical substrate limits and miss independently useful solutions.

## Decision

Treat every empirical, formal, and engineering science as in scope for
discovery. Search breadth-first by constrained problem rather than by visual or
terminological resemblance to AI. Apply the extraction, deduplication, and
promotion gates in
[`research/discovery-policy.md`](../research/discovery-policy.md).

## Consequences

- The domain inventory is an open, growing atlas rather than an exhaustive
  boundary around “relevant” fields.
- Apparently unrelated fields may generate leads, but no field receives a
  lower evidence bar.
- Audits preserve domain-specific mechanisms before normalizing them into
  recurring `P-` bundles.
- Every transfer competes with its strongest conventional engineering analogue.
- Breadth is valuable only when it produces a new causal invariant, a sharper
  failure mode, a measurement, or a discriminating experiment.
