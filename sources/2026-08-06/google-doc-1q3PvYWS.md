That is the exact paradox of scientific discovery: If your rules engine strictly enforces known physics, you can never discover new physics. If an AI is rigidly constrained by Newton's laws, it will never hallucinate Einstein's General Relativity because the system will reject it as an "error."

So, how do researchers use AI to discover new fundamental laws?

The answer is brilliant but simple: You shift the constraint layer. You stop constraining the AI with current human theories, and you start constraining it with raw reality or pure logic.

Here is how this is actively being done at the bleeding edge of AI research right now.

## 1. Discovering New Physics: The "Empirical Data" Constraint

When scientists want an AI to discover new physical laws, they use a technique called Symbolic Regression.

Instead of telling the AI, "Generate an idea that obeys classical gravity," they feed the AI millions of raw data points from an experiment and tell it, "Generate a mathematical equation that perfectly predicts this data." The constraint is no longer the old physics textbook; the constraint is the raw data itself.

### A Real-World Example:

In April 2026, researchers at Emory University used AI to discover entirely new physics regarding how particles interact in a "dusty plasma" (a complex state of matter). They didn't force the AI to obey existing assumptions. Instead, they fed a custom neural network high-precision 3D tracking data of the particles. The AI was free to creatively generate wild mathematical models, but it was strictly constrained by the requirement to match the observed data. As a result, the AI uncovered hidden "non-reciprocal forces" (where one particle influences another differently than it is influenced in return), successfully overturning long-held assumptions in the field.

## 2. Discovering New Math: The "Formal Logic" Constraint

In pure mathematics, you don't have physical data to measure. Instead, the ultimate constraint is logical self-consistency.

Researchers build systems where a large language model (the creative engine) generates wild, combinatorial guesses for a mathematical proof. But before a human ever sees it, that guess is fed into an automated "Evaluator" (the rules engine)—a strict computer program that tests if the math actually works.

### A Real-World Example:

Google DeepMind built a system called FunSearch to do exactly this. They paired a highly creative LLM with an automated evaluator that strictly guarded against hallucinations and incorrect ideas. The LLM was asked to generate computer code that could solve the "Cap Set problem"—a notoriously difficult, decades-old open challenge in mathematics.

The LLM hallucinated thousands of creative, combinatorial ideas. Most of them were wrong and were instantly killed by the evaluator. But because the LLM was forced to keep trying against this unbreakable wall of logic, it eventually synthesized a piece of code that passed the test. FunSearch ultimately discovered the largest cap sets ever found, marking the first time an LLM made a genuine new discovery for an open problem in mathematical sciences.

## The Ultimate Scientific Method

When you look at systems like FunSearch or Symbolic Regression, you realize that AI isn't changing the scientific method—it is just running it at gigahertz speeds.

In both human research and AI architectures:

The Brain / LLM generates the wild, combinatorial hypothesis (Creativity).

The Experiment / Evaluator brutally rejects anything that doesn't fit the logic or the data (The Rules).

If the rules are the subject of research, you just rip out the old rules and let pure mathematics and raw observation be the cage that shapes the creativity.
