## Executive finding

This field contributes useful failure cases precisely because many religious
practices are not optimized as transparent means to a measurable external end.
A sequence can be retained because it is conventional, identity-bearing,
authorized, commemorative, expressive, or constitutive. Treating every step as
an inefficient approximation to an instrumental goal destroys the object being
modeled; treating every inherited step as causally necessary preserves error and
blocks repair.

The transferable contract is therefore a **typed practice record** that keeps
four questions separate:

1. what physical or informational effect is claimed and testable;
2. what conventional form identifies participation or preserves continuity;
3. which community, authority, role, and version govern the practice;
4. which values, rights, meanings, and objections may not be collapsed into one
   reward score.

No new architecture principle survives deduplication. Workflow engines,
protocol schemas, synchronization, provenance, constrained optimization,
institutional governance, cultural transmission, and source-reliability models
are mature nulls. Religious studies supplies adversarial tests: opaque actions
can transmit with high fidelity, synchrony can change affiliation, cost can be
mistaken for credibility, non-compensatory commitments resist prices, and the
same text or practice can support several authorized or disputed readings.

## Boundary: what this audit does not adjudicate

The audit does not rank religions, infer belief from behavior, declare a ritual
true or false in a theological sense, reduce worship to an optimization
technique, or collect community-specific sacred knowledge. Empirical studies
can test copying, coordination, stress, memory, or social inference under their
designs. They cannot establish or refute transcendence, revelation, sacramental
effect, divine agency, spiritual value, or the legitimacy of a community's
authority.

The workstation package uses synthetic procedures and fictional communities.
No participant's religion, philosophical belief, worship record, or community
practice is needed.

## Terms that must remain distinct

| Term | Operational meaning here | Not equivalent to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| instrumental action | sequence selected for a declared causal or functional effect | every useful convention |
| ritual/conventional action | socially stipulated form whose correct performance can be partly constitutive of the act | habit, compulsion, algorithm, superstition, or inefficiency by definition |
| causal opacity | observer cannot recover a physical means–end relation from the sequence and context | proof that no participant has reasons, meanings, or an instrumental model |
| fidelity | similarity under a declared feature representation across transmissions | truth, benefit, identical meaning, or independent origin |
| synchrony | temporal alignment under a stated tolerance and clock | cooperation, agreement, causation, or neural synchrony |
| costly display | action whose cost can affect an observer's inference about commitment under a model | honest evidence, moral worth, correctness, or social benefit |
| sacred/protected value | commitment treated as non-compensatory or inappropriate to price in a context | infinite numeric utility or freedom from conflict with another protected value |
| canon | corpus authorized for a role by a named tradition or institution | complete history, one interpretation, empirical truth, or universal authority |
| doctrine | formulated teaching within a tradition and authority structure | observation, law, or executable policy |
| hermeneutic policy | versioned norms and practices for interpretation | deterministic parsing or historical recovery |
| testimony | claim received from a speaker, witness, or tradition | independent verification or automatically weak evidence |
| conscience/refusal | protected stance or objection under applicable authority and law | a low-confidence preference to optimize away |

## Evidence synthesis

### Causal opacity can preserve form and preserve error

Kapitány and Nielsen experimentally varied opacity and ritual context in action
interpretation. Their work supports a distinction between ritual and everyday
instrumental stances rather than the rule that any unexplained step is a ritual
([doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2015.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.08.002)).
Legare and Nielsen's interdisciplinary synthesis argues that social stipulation
and opacity can increase high-fidelity imitation while inhibiting individual
innovation. It is a review and research framework, not a calibrated universal
effect
([doi:10.1098/rstb.2019.0419](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0419)).

Liberman, Kinzler, and Woodward showed 16-month-old infants two actors using the
same or different unnecessary action to turn on a light. Infants expected actors
who shared the ritual-like action to affiliate more than actors using different
ritual-like actions; a situational-constraint control changed the inference.
This is narrow evidence that conventional action can carry social information,
not that shared procedure proves shared values or trustworthiness
([doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2017.10.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.10.018)).

For transmitted procedure $q$ with step set $J$, declare a feature map
$\phi_v(q)$ and report fidelity only relative to it:

$$
F_v(q,q')=1-d_v\!\left(\phi_v(q),\phi_v(q')\right),
$$

where $d_v$ is a versioned normalized distance. A high $F_v$ may preserve an
unnecessary, harmful, or semantically reinterpreted step. Endpoint correctness,
causal necessity, community acceptance, and resource cost remain separate.

### Synchrony is a scoped intervention, not a cooperation primitive

Wiltermuth and Heath manipulated synchronous versus asynchronous walking,
singing, or cup movement in small laboratory groups and observed increased
cooperation in subsequent tasks in the synchronous conditions
([doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02253.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02253.x)).
Later naturalistic and laboratory work varies in task, arousal, population,
outcome, and mechanism. The safe repository statement is only that shared timing
can affect immediate social behavior under some designs.

For agents $i$ and events $k$, define timing dispersion

$$
D_k=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i(t_{ik}-\bar t_k)^2}
$$

in seconds. Lower $D_k$ is not task success. Tests must also report useful
service, message and waiting cost, fault correlation, exclusion, repair under
clock drift, and out-group or minority impact. Consensus, barrier
synchronization, scheduled execution, and asynchronous acknowledgement are
mandatory technical nulls.

### Cost can signal commitment without validating content

Henrich formalized credibility-enhancing displays as a cultural-learning
hypothesis: learners may attend to actions that would be costly if a model did
not hold the expressed commitment. The paper explicitly presents a theory and
evolutionary model rather than a conclusive empirical test
([doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2009.03.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2009.03.005)).

The engineering danger is immediate. Training cost, compute expenditure,
personal sacrifice, public confidence, organizational size, or repeated use can
be taken as credibility signals even when they are unrelated to evidence or
competence. An auditable evaluator therefore separates

$$
P(h\mid a,c,\ell) \quad\text{from}\quad
P(y\mid h,D,O_v,A_v),
$$

where $h$ is a claim or commitment, $a$ an observed action, $c$ its estimated
cost, $\ell$ the actor/source lineage, $y$ a tested outcome, $D$ evidence, $O_v$
the observation process, and $A_v$ auxiliary assumptions. A display may update
an observer's model of commitment while contributing no direct evidence about
$y$.

### Protected values are not large reward coefficients

Tetlock et al. experimentally studied reactions to taboo trade-offs, prohibited
base rates, and counterfactuals. Participants often responded to monetization of
protected values with moral outrage and moral-cleansing behavior
([doi:10.1037/0022-3514.78.5.853](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.5.853)).
The work demonstrates a class of human response; it does not settle which value
is legitimate, eliminate scarcity, or show that all asserted sacred values are
absolute.

A protected condition is therefore represented as a typed constraint or
authority rule, not as $w\to\infty$ in one utility function. If constraints
conflict, the system exposes the conflict, applicable authority, available
remedies, and unresolved state. Candidate 020 owns that governance problem.
Ordinary constraint programming, lexicographic optimization, deontic logic,
policy-as-code, rights impact assessment, and human adjudication are the nulls.

### Claimed ritual benefits require active nulls

A preregistered experiment with 180 undergraduates compared stress/control
conditions and ritualistic, repetitive-movement, cognitive-load, combined, and
control interventions. It found no direct support for the proposed cognitive
suppression effect on anxiety
([doi:10.1007/s12110-018-9325-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12110-018-9325-3)).
Other field and laboratory studies report effects in different practices and
populations. This disagreement is exactly why a named ritual, repetition,
expectation, social support, attention, movement, rest, and cultural-meaning arm
must be separated before claiming a mechanism.

The project must apply the same discipline to recurring AI procedures. A
repeated prompt, ceremony-like review, meeting cadence, or multi-agent debate
does not receive benefit credit without an active comparator that equalizes
time, attention, calls, information, authority, and expectation.

## European normative and data boundary

The [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, Article 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/treaty/char_2016/art_10/oj/eng)
protects freedom of thought, conscience, and religion, including change and
manifestation alone or in community, subject to the applicable legal framework.
[GDPR Article 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) treats data
revealing religious or philosophical beliefs as a special category whose
processing is prohibited by default unless a stated exception applies.

Consequences for this repository:

1. do not infer or classify a real person's belief for the workstation fixtures;
2. do not treat public manifestation, clothing, name, location, language,
   calendar, diet, or association as permission to infer belief;
3. keep freedom of belief and freedom from compelled belief compatible with
   synthetic governance tests;
4. preserve purpose, lawful basis, minimization, access, retention, correction,
   objection, and deletion/invalidation duties for any later personal-data use;
5. do not let a model decide which doctrine, authority, or practice is authentic;
   and
6. keep scientific evidence, legal status, community authorization, and
   theological meaning as separate fields.

## Proposed typed practice record

For procedure or practice $r$, use

$$
R_r=(\mathrm{id},v,g,A,\mathbf f,J,\rho,E,X,M),
$$

where:

- $\mathrm{id},v$ are identity and version;
- $g$ is the named governing community or system boundary;
- $A$ is the applicable authority record, including dispute state;
- $\mathbf f$ labels declared functions such as instrumental, conventional,
  commemorative, identity-bearing, expressive, constitutive, or unresolved;
- $J$ is the ordered/partially ordered step graph;
- $\rho$ gives applicability, role, timing, and participation conditions;
- $E$ records required evidence and acceptance;
- $X$ gives permitted variation, exception, repair, and retirement;
- $M$ records meanings or interpretations as attributed claims with source and
  scope, never as one hidden true label.

One step may have several functions. An `unresolved` label blocks automatic
deletion but does not prove necessity. Community-specific authority and meaning
remain external inputs; the model can preserve and enforce a supplied record,
not create legitimate standing.

## Workstation falsification package

All fixtures use fictional procedures, communities, protected values, and
authorities. Freeze generator, rule versions, observation paths, model/source
lineage, budgets, outcome definitions, and splits before confirmation.

### WS-REL-01 — instrumental versus conventional sequence transmission

Generate tasks with known necessary steps, irrelevant inherited steps,
identity-bearing but endpoint-neutral steps, context-dependent steps, and
harmful obsolete steps. Vary whether demonstrations are framed as instrumental,
conventional, ambiguous, or mixed.

**Arms:** behavioral cloning; exact imitation; goal-conditioned program
induction; typed workflow with dependency analysis; Candidate 015; Candidate 015
plus the practice record; and oracle step-function labels as an ineligible
ceiling.

**Outcomes:** endpoint success, necessary-step recall, obsolete-step retention,
constitutive-form violation, repair after context change, unauthorized
innovation, transmission fidelity by feature map, calls, bytes, time, and
joules.

**Falsifier:** reject if the typed workflow matches the practice record, if
benefit comes from oracle function labels, or if fidelity improves only by
preserving known harmful steps.

### WS-REL-02 — costly display versus source reliability

Create synthetic sources with independently controlled accuracy, commitment,
competence, action cost, ability to pay, strategic signaling, shared lineage,
and adversarial mimicry.

Compare verbal-only trust, cost-weighted trust, reputation, calibrated Bayesian
source reliability, cryptographic/provenance evidence, independent replication,
Candidate 014 ancestry, and the proposed record. Report belief calibration,
false trust, false distrust, inequality by resource endowment, mimicry success,
evidence cost, human work, and energy.

**Falsifier:** cost receives no epistemic credit when an evidence-qualified
reliability model matches or beats it. A display may remain useful for predicting
commitment only if that target is separately validated.

### WS-REL-03 — synchrony, coordination, and correlated failure

Generate multi-agent tasks with clock drift, latency, partial participation,
role turnover, fault injection, and minority dissent.

**Arms:** barrier-synchronous execution; fixed scheduled ritual; asynchronous
messages with acknowledgement; consensus/quorum; event-time coordination;
Candidate 011; and Candidate 015 convention repair.

Report useful task service, timing dispersion $D_k$, waiting, messages,
deadlocks, common-mode failure, dissent preservation, exclusion, repair latency,
compute, and joules. Reject any synchrony claim that does not outperform ordinary
clock/protocol mechanisms or that gains average coordination by hiding dissent
or amplifying correlated failure.

### WS-REL-04 — protected and conflicting values

Plant routine trade-offs, protected constraint versus ordinary objective, two
conflicting protected constraints, uncertain applicability, authority conflict,
and emergency/review paths.

Compare scalar reward with large weights, lexicographic optimization, hard
constraint solving, policy-as-code, deontic rules with conflict detection,
Candidate 020, and Candidate 020 plus explicit protected-value typing. Measure
violations, hidden infeasibility, legitimate abstention, unauthorized override,
conflict exposure, remedy latency, human burden, and useful service.

**Falsifier:** reject if ordinary constraint/governance composition matches the
frontier or if the candidate invents the protected value, authority, or priority.

### WS-REL-05 — canon, interpretation, and policy change

Use synthetic corpora with ambiguous clauses, variant witnesses, later
amendments, several interpretive communities, conflicting authorities, and
known task-specific application policies.

Compare one canonical string, fixed parser, versioned rule engine, retrieval over
all witnesses, Candidate 017 critical apparatus, Candidate 015 interpretive
policy, and Candidate 020 authority/dispute handling. Report source recovery,
policy-correct action, disagreement preservation, invented consensus, version
confusion, authority shopping, review latency, bytes, and energy.

No arm is scored against one unqualified `true interpretation`. The fixture has
versioned community-policy answers for particular applications and separately
known documentary history.

## Confirmatory and cost contract

The confirmatory unit is a complete transmission or governance episode through
performance, interpretation, decision, repair, and follow-up. Hold out entire
procedure families, communities, authority graphs, framing rules, clock regimes,
source populations, and value conflicts. Cluster by source lineage, procedure,
community, model version, and evaluator.

Use the gate order:

1. no excess protected-value, authority, or constitutive-form violation;
2. noninferior useful service and causal-step correctness;
3. lower harmful rigidity, false trust, invented consensus, or conflict hiding;
4. better repair/transfer under held-out procedure and authority change; then
5. lower human, communication, storage, compute, and energy cost.

Report raw outcome vectors and simultaneous intervals. Count instruction and
training time, participation, waiting, copying, review, interpretation,
governance, appeals, retained sensitive state, communication, compute, and wall
energy. Do not credit a lower machine cost that shifts uncompensated work or
risk to participants or communities.

## Kill conditions

Reject the residue as distinct if:

1. ordinary typed workflow plus versioned policy matches repair and transfer;
2. high fidelity preserves irrelevant or harmful steps without compensating
   constitutive value under the fixture contract;
3. synchrony improves a proxy while useful service, dissent, inclusion, or fault
   independence worsens;
4. costly action predicts neither separately measured commitment nor outcome
   beyond source lineage and evidence;
5. protected-value typing reduces to ordinary hard constraints and appeal;
6. the system infers real belief, authentic doctrine, community authority, or
   sacred meaning from behavior or content;
7. a single canonical parse erases registered witnesses or authorized dispute;
8. performance uses hidden community labor, participant burden, future labels,
   or uncharged review; or
9. results fail in a second materially different procedure and authority family.

## Audit decision

1. **Add no principle.** Ritual and religious-practice findings refine existing
   protocol, timing, memory, provenance, and governance bundles.
2. **Add step-function typing to Candidate 015 tests.** Instrumental,
   conventional, constitutive, identity-bearing, and unresolved functions must
   not be silently interconverted.
3. **Treat fidelity as representation-qualified.** Copying the same form is not
   correctness, truth, or unchanged meaning.
4. **Treat synchrony as a technical comparator.** It must beat clocks,
   acknowledgement, quorum, and event-time coordination on useful service.
5. **Separate commitment evidence from world evidence.** Costly display can at
   most inform a scoped source model.
6. **Represent protected values through authority and conflict.** Do not emulate
   them with arbitrarily large reward weights.
7. **Use active nulls for every ritual-benefit claim.** Repetition, movement,
   attention, expectation, social support, rest, and meaning are separate arms.
8. **Keep real religious data out of workstation fixtures.** European rights and
   special-category data rules remain explicit before any later human study.
