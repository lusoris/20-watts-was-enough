# Primary-source audit: cultural evolution and archaeological inference

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->

**Audit date:** 2026-08-05  
**Scope:** cumulative culture, imitation and teaching, model and content biases,
distributed cognition, material scaffolds and external memory, institutions and
sanctions, innovation and recombination, demographic and network effects, path
dependence, and archaeological observation limits  
**Purpose:** candidate evidence for later claim-ledger and principle-registry
review; this file does not promote claims or create a new `P-` identifier

## Executive finding

Cultural evolution contributes a useful systems view, but no new stable
architectural principle. Reliable accumulation across turnover requires at
least four separately measurable operations:

1. **generation:** someone produces a variant, refinement, or recombination;
2. **transmission:** another learner obtains enough process, outcome, or
   instruction information to reconstruct it;
3. **evaluation and selection:** variants are tested under a declared payoff,
   risk, and resource model; and
4. **retention and governance:** accepted changes survive personnel turnover
   through memory, practice, artifacts, institutions, and maintenance.

That “cultural ratchet” is a lifecycle composition of
[`P-003`](../principle-registry.md#p-003--temporary-trace-before-commitment),
[`P-004`](../principle-registry.md#p-004--diversity-selection-and-protection),
[`P-005`](../principle-registry.md#p-005--use-dependent-topology),
[`P-009`](../principle-registry.md#p-009--maintenance-plane),
[`P-012`](../principle-registry.md#p-012--memory-matched-to-information-lifetime),
and [`P-013`](../principle-registry.md#p-013--externalized-shared-state). Its
distinctive question is whether a population can preserve and recombine
validated capability better than a centralized learner with ordinary
versioning, retrieval, search, and governance. That remains an experiment, not
a principle.

Three negative findings are as important as the positive synthesis:

- high-fidelity action imitation is neither necessary in every cumulative-
  culture task nor sufficient for adaptive accumulation;
- population size alone does not determine cultural complexity—accessible
  model diversity, network topology, learning cost, ecology, and measurement
  all matter; and
- an archaeological artifact is a material residue transformed by use,
  discard, preservation, recovery, and classification, not a direct recording
  of the maker's cognition, social-learning rule, or institution.

## Evidence and inference boundary

| Evidence design | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| replacement microsociety | accumulation, loss, or path dependence under a bounded task and turnover protocol | prehistoric history, species uniqueness, or open-ended technological progress |
| transmission-chain experiment | transformations across specified learners, stimuli, and channel conditions | natural population prevalence or long-run adaptive value |
| social-learning choice experiment | copying choices under visible models, payoffs, and uncertainty | an evolved adaptation, stable real-world prestige, or immunity to manipulation |
| public-goods/institution experiment | behavior under stated payoffs, sanctions, identities, and repetition | the historical origin or legitimacy of institutions outside the game |
| ethnography/cognitive field study | organization of work and artifacts in a sampled activity system | a unique algorithm or universal optimal architecture |
| comparative ethnographic dataset | associations under the coding, societies, ecology, and contact model | one-way demographic causation or prehistoric temporal sequence |
| archaeological excavation | provenience, association, material modification, and chronology within uncertainty | unobserved speech, intention, teaching, symbolic meaning, or transmission mechanism by itself |
| archaeological frequency model | compatibility of observed counts with a declared generative and formation model | identification when multiple behavioral and taphonomic processes are observationally equivalent |
| formal cultural-evolution model | consequences of its transmission, payoff, mutation, topology, and population assumptions | that people use the rule or that the variables are observable in deep time |

“Established” below always means established within the stated design. A
mechanism observed in people does not transfer its effect size or validity to
AI merely because agents exchange artifacts.

## Terms that must remain distinct

| Term | Operational meaning here | Not equivalent to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| social learning | behavior changes using information generated by another individual | faithful copying, teaching, conformity, or improvement |
| imitation | reproducing demonstrated actions or action organization | emulation of an outcome, instruction following, or cumulative culture |
| emulation | learning about environmental results or end states without necessarily copying actions | low fidelity in every task |
| teaching | behavior modified in the presence of a naive learner, with cost or no immediate benefit to the teacher, that facilitates learning | any documentation, demonstration, or reward signal |
| cumulative culture | socially transmitted change that preserves and builds on prior achievements across learner turnover | diffusion, increasing popularity, complexity alone, or one person's repeated learning |
| prestige bias | preferential learning from a model cued by freely conferred attention or deference | demonstrated competence, authenticated authority, dominance, or truth |
| conformist bias | disproportionate preference for a locally common variant | majority vote, mere frequency matching, consensus correctness, or norm legitimacy |
| payoff bias | preferential learning from a model or variant with observed success | causal understanding, unbiased evaluation, or resistance to metric gaming |
| content bias | differential retention or transmission due to properties of the material | objective usefulness or factuality |
| convention | a regularity sustained through recurrent coordination and expectations | moral norm, enforceable institution, optimal protocol, or shared belief |
| institution | persistent rules, roles, monitoring, and consequences organizing interaction | one learned policy or a static prompt |
| external memory | recoverable state placed outside one individual's biological memory | correct, current, discoverable, or safely interpretable information |
| distributed cognition | analysis in which coordinated people, representations, and artifacts form the task system | a claim that individuals have no internal cognition |
| path dependence | earlier events alter later reachable states or transition probabilities | any temporal autocorrelation or proof the current state is suboptimal |
| archaeological assemblage | recovered material accumulated and transformed over an interval | a synchronous behavioral population sample |

## Current function is not historical origin

| Observation | Defensible current-function claim | Unsupported origin claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| meerkat helpers provision prey by pup age | the behavior facilitates prey-handling learning in the observed population | teaching evolved specifically to transmit this skill |
| participants prefer or benefit from a prestigious/successful model | the cue changes learning under the task | prestige psychology evolved for the tested domain or is generally adaptive |
| punishment increases cooperation in a public-goods treatment | sanction availability changes behavior under those payoffs | the institution historically originated to maximize group welfare |
| an artifact stores a mark or procedure | the material can constrain or cue later action when a user can interpret it | its maker intended external memory, notation, ritual, or pedagogy |
| an established tradition persists | present switching costs or uncertainty stabilize it | the same mechanism caused its initial appearance |

## Mathematical boundary conditions

### Accumulation is retention plus change, not a monotonic law

An audit-friendly bookkeeping model is

$$
K_{g+1}=q_g K_g + I_g + R_g - D_g,
$$

where $g$ is a learner generation, $K_g$ is validated capability in declared
task-score units, $q_g\in[0,1]$ is retained fraction per generation, $I_g$ is
net individually generated improvement, $R_g$ is net recombination gain, and
$D_g$ is degradation or compatibility loss in the same task-score units. This
is not a biological law: capability need not be additive, a higher score can
hide lost functions, and $q_g K_g$ is meaningful only under a versioned test
suite. Lewis and Laland's transmission model demonstrates how fidelity can
affect build-up under explicit assumptions; it does not prove imitation is the
unique route to fidelity ([Lewis and Laland 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0119)).

### Social-learning biases are transformations, not truth guarantees

For a binary local frequency $p$, an illustrative frequency-bias transform is

$$
P(\text{copy }A)=\frac{p^\alpha}{p^\alpha+(1-p)^\alpha},
$$

with $\alpha>1$ conformist, $\alpha=1$ frequency matching, and
$0<\alpha<1$ anti-conformist. This family does not identify the rule from one
endpoint distribution, and “majority” is relative to the sampled neighborhood.

A model-choice policy can instead be written

$$
P(i)=\frac{\exp(\beta_u\hat u_i+\beta_p h_i+\beta_d d_i)}
{\sum_j\exp(\beta_u\hat u_j+\beta_p h_j+\beta_d d_j)},
$$

where $\hat u_i$ is observed payoff in task units, $h_i$ is a prestige cue,
$d_i$ is domain relevance, and the $\beta$ coefficients have inverse units
needed to make the exponent dimensionless. This is a diagnostic model, not a
recommendation: payoff can be gamed, prestige can be laundered, and domain
labels can be wrong.

If learner exposure weights to possible models are $\pi_i$ with
$\sum_i\pi_i=1$, then

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{\sum_i \pi_i^2}
$$

is the dimensionless effective number of equally exposed models. It measures
attention concentration, not competence diversity, independence, population
size, or cultural complexity.

### Sanctioning changes incentives and consumes resources

For a public-goods round, a minimal payoff is

$$
\Pi_i=E_i-c_i+\frac{r}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}c_j-k_i-f_i,
$$

where $\Pi_i$ and endowment $E_i$ are currency units per round, $c_i$ is the
player's contribution, $1<r<N$ is the dimensionless group multiplier, $N$ is
group size, $k_i$ is punishment or monitoring expenditure, and $f_i$ is fines
received. Cooperation can rise while net welfare falls if $k_i+f_i$ is large,
punishment is antisocial, or errors target cooperators.

### External memory has a lifecycle cost

For an external representation used $n_r$ times,

$$
C_{\mathrm{ext}}=C_{\mathrm{write}}+n_rC_{\mathrm{read}}+
C_{\mathrm{index}}+C_{\mathrm{verify}}+C_{\mathrm{migrate}}+C_{\mathrm{loss}},
$$

with all terms in joules over the declared lifecycle. $C_{\mathrm{loss}}$ is
expected recovery/rework energy under unavailability or misinterpretation.
Storage is efficient only relative to an internal-memory, recomputation, or
retrieval baseline at equal correctness and latency.

### An assemblage is a weighted temporal mixture

For artifact variant $v$ in archaeological bin $b=[t_0,t_1]$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[y_{v,b}]=\int_{t_0}^{t_1} a(t)p_v(t)s_v(t)\,dt,
$$

where $y_{v,b}$ is recovered count, $a(t)$ is deposition rate in
artifacts year$^{-1}$, $p_v(t)$ is latent behavioral production frequency
(dimensionless), and $s_v(t)$ is the dimensionless joint probability of
survival, discovery, collection, and classification. Even with perfect
counting, $y_{v,b}/\sum_v y_{v,b}$ is a weighted time average rather than a
synchronous population frequency unless $a$ and $s$ are stable and the bin is
short relative to behavioral change. Premo's simulations show that time
averaging can flatten evenness, inflate richness, and create false rejections
of neutral transmission models ([Premo 2014](https://doi.org/10.1086/674873)).

## 1. Cumulative culture and fidelity

**Primary evidence.** Replacement microsocieties building paper airplanes and
spaghetti towers produced later-generation designs that outperformed earlier
ones in those tasks ([Caldwell and Millen 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2007.12.001)).
However, a 70-microsociety paper-airplane experiment found cumulative
improvement when participants could imitate actions, observe only outcomes, or
receive teaching; each information condition was independently sufficient in
that task ([Caldwell and Millen 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02469.x)).
A combinatorial “totem” experiment found that human groups created artifacts
more complex than isolated participants given the same time, and separated
guided variation from random-search bots and full from partial social
information ([Derex and Boyd 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9398)).

**Mechanism.** Accumulation depends on preserving task-relevant constraints
while permitting improvement. Action imitation can transmit tacit process;
outcome emulation can avoid irrelevant steps; teaching can select information
for a learner; artifacts can stabilize state. Fidelity is therefore fidelity
of **reconstructable function under a test**, not literal token identity.

**Evidence status.** Established for the bounded laboratory tasks; theoretical
support for fidelity thresholds is model-dependent; open-ended and
archaeological generalization is plausible but not established.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Centralized optimization, replay,
self-distillation, version control, retrieval, and explicit search can produce
the same performance trend without a cultural population. Copying can preserve
obsolete steps, evaluation noise can ratchet errors, and average score can rise
while rare capabilities disappear. Later-generation improvement does not prove
that no individual, given equal total learning time and information, could
reach the result.

**Measurable prediction.** With cumulative compute, observations, and human
maintenance held equal, a turnover population should retain more validated
capabilities and combine improvements faster than a centralized lineage. The
result must survive shuffled-lineage, no-transmission, and full-history
baselines.

## 2. Imitation, emulation, and teaching

**Primary evidence.** In a transparent versus opaque puzzle box, chimpanzees
more often omitted visibly irrelevant demonstrated actions in the transparent
condition, while three- to four-year-old children copied irrelevant actions at
high rates in both conditions ([Horner and Whiten 2005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-004-0239-6)).
This separates action copying from outcome reconstruction without ranking one
strategy universally. Wild meerkat helpers changed prey provisioning with pup
age; experiments supported faster acquisition of prey-handling skill under
these teaching-like opportunities ([Thornton and McAuliffe 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128727)).

**Mechanism.** A learner needs different channels when causal structure is
hidden, visible, dangerous to explore, or cheap to test. Teaching allocates
examples and difficulty; imitation preserves process; emulation preserves
outcomes; individual learning supplies causal correction.

**Evidence status.** Established behavioral effects in the studied species and
tasks. The claim that one channel caused human cumulative culture, or is
species-unique, is disputed and unnecessary for engineering.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Supervised examples, traces,
behavioral cloning, state snapshots, manuals, causal models, and active
experimentation already expose different information. Teaching can suppress
exploration, imitation can copy ritualized or malicious steps, and outcome-only
learning can miss hidden safety constraints. A teaching behavior's current
learning effect does not reveal its evolutionary origin.

**Measurable prediction.** An uncertainty-aware channel selector should choose
process traces for causally opaque/high-risk tasks, outcome demonstrations for
transparent tasks, and active tests when cheap. It fails if one fixed channel
or a standard active learner matches quality at lower bandwidth and energy.

## 3. Prestige, conformity, payoff, and content biases

**Primary evidence.** In one frequency-learning experiment, participants were
heterogeneous: some used a conformist rule and others behaved as “mavericks,”
so population-level conformity was not a universal individual strategy
([Efferson et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2007.08.003)).
Across perceptual tasks, demonstrator number and agreement, participant
confidence, task difficulty, learning cost, and demonstrated performance all
changed social-information use ([Morgan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.1172)).
In a virtual-arrowhead task, participants used individual learning and payoff
bias far more than conformity or averaging, and payoff-biased use predicted
success particularly on a multimodal landscape
([Mesoudi 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.12.001)).

Three- and four-year-old children preferentially learned artifact behavior from
an adult whom bystanders had previously attended to; the effect showed domain
sensitivity rather than unrestricted deference
([Chudek et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2011.05.005)).
Transmission chains also retained social-interaction information better than
matched nonsocial material in two experiments, showing that content changes
what survives transmission ([Mesoudi, Whiten, and Dunbar 2006](https://doi.org/10.1348/000712605X85871)).

**Mechanism.** Model cues and content properties allocate scarce attention
when direct evaluation is expensive. They are lossy heuristics with conditional
value, not epistemic authorities.

**Evidence status.** Established task-specific biases and heterogeneity;
plausible adaptive value under bounded uncertainty; speculative as a default AI
router.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Calibrated direct evaluation,
randomized exploration, domain credentials, authenticated provenance, and
ordinary contextual bandits are stronger defaults. Popularity creates
information cascades; prestige can be manufactured; observed payoff can be
confounded or Goodharted; memorable content can be false. Candidate 008 already
owns strategic allocation and contestability.

**Measurable prediction.** A combined model-choice policy may reduce evaluation
cost only when direct evidence is sparse. It must abstain or explore when
majorities are correlated, prestige is off-domain, and payoff comes from a
gameable proxy. Hidden ground truth, adversarial model cues, and changing tasks
are mandatory tests.

## 4. Distributed cognition, material scaffolds, and external memory

**Primary evidence.** Hutchins' cognitive ethnography analyzed naval
navigation as coordinated transformations across people, charts, instruments,
procedures, and spoken reports rather than as a calculation located in one
head ([Hutchins 1995](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/)).
In Tetris, Kirsh and Maglio showed that some rotations and translations expose
information or reduce mental computation rather than merely move a piece
toward the game goal ([Kirsh and Maglio 1994](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1)).

**Mechanism.** Representations can transform a hard internal inference into a
cheap perception/action loop. Roles and handoffs can partition computation,
while public artifacts preserve intermediate state across individuals and
time.

**Evidence status.** Established in the observed navigation activity and
Tetris experiments; plausible systems abstraction; not evidence that every
tool is part of a cognitive system or that external storage is cheap.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Workflow engines, databases,
retrieval, scratchpads, compilers, and user interfaces already externalize
state. External records introduce search, schema, access-control, staleness,
verification, migration, and outage costs. A distributed team can add handoff
errors and diffuse responsibility. This deduplicates into `P-010`, `P-012`, and
`P-013` unless the full lifecycle frontier improves.

**Measurable prediction.** A manipulable external representation should reduce
online compute and error at equal end-to-end energy, including authoring,
indexing, verification, migration, and recovery. Remove the scaffold and test
whether capability degrades in the predicted subtask rather than merely causing
interface surprise.

## 5. Institutions, norms, monitoring, and sanctions

**Primary evidence.** In common-pool-resource experiments, communication and
participant-devised sanctioning arrangements enabled substantial self-
governance under the tested conditions ([Ostrom, Walker, and Gardner 1992](https://doi.org/10.2307/1964229)).
Public-goods experiments found that costly peer punishment could sustain
cooperation, while cross-society experiments found punishment in all 15 sampled
populations but large variation in magnitude
([Fehr and Gächter 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/415137a);
[Henrich et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1127333)).

When participants could choose between sanctioning and sanction-free
institutions, the sanctioning institution attracted membership and achieved
higher cooperation and profit in the original laboratory study
([Gürerk, Irlenbusch, and Rockenbach 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1123633)).
A preregistered multilab replication with 1,008 participants met all criteria
in five of seven European laboratories; the remaining two showed weaker effects
in the same direction ([Lo Iacono et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad091)).
Separately, decentralized naming-game experiments showed that network scale and
structure can generate shared conventions without a central institution
([Centola and Baronchelli 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418838112)).

**Mechanism.** Institutions alter incentives, information, monitoring,
membership, appeal, and persistence. Conventions coordinate expectations;
norms add evaluations of appropriate conduct; sanctions impose consequences.
These functions must not be collapsed.

**Evidence status.** Established treatment effects with meaningful
cross-population variation and replicated European sanction-choice effects;
origin, universality, and AI transfer remain open.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Typed contracts, IAM, static safety
policy, calibrated rewards, audits, and ordinary incident governance are the
strongest nulls. Punishment consumes resources, can be antisocial or erroneous,
and can entrench a coalition. Conformity can suppress whistleblowers. A stable
convention can be arbitrary, exclusionary, or unsafe. Candidate 011 already
tests the live-governance/longitudinal-learning interface.

**Measurable prediction.** Endogenous governance is warranted only if it
improves net welfare, tail safety, adaptation, and legitimacy proxies after
monitoring, sanction, appeal, evasion, and capture costs. Test norm violations,
false accusations, minority expertise, turnover, and institution exit.

## 6. Innovation, reconstruction, and recombination

**Primary evidence.** The combinatorial-totem experiments separated random
variation, individual reasoning, social information, group size, and partial
connectivity; groups combined discoveries beyond the bounded individual-time
baseline ([Derex and Boyd 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9398)). In a
complex landscape, partially connected groups maintained more diverse
solutions and reached complex solutions absent from fully connected groups
([Derex and Boyd 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1518798113)). In a
wheel-building transmission experiment, performance improved across
generations while participants' causal understanding did not improve
detectably, showing that functional refinement can outpace explicit causal
models in that task ([Derex et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0567-9)).

An experimental virtual-projectile task reproduced different attribute-
correlation patterns under guided individual variation and success-biased
social learning, but this compatibility does not identify the actual prehistoric
process that generated Great Basin artifacts
([Mesoudi and O'Brien 2008](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0002731600041263)).

**Mechanism.** Partial separation preserves incompatible local discoveries;
periodic exchange permits recombination; tests retain functional improvements.
Explicit causal understanding can improve transfer and safety but is not a
necessary description of every cumulative performance gain.

**Evidence status.** Established in bounded experimental landscapes;
plausible for modular innovation; speculative for open-domain AI systems.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Quality-diversity search, novelty
search, branch-and-merge development, ablation-guided composition, and candidate
004 cover the same search problem. Recombination can break epistatic
dependencies; opaque high-performing artifacts can be brittle and unsafe;
partial connectivity slows diffusion and duplicates work.

**Measurable prediction.** A branch/exchange schedule should discover more
independent validated components and useful combinations at matched evaluations
and energy than fully connected, isolated, and centralized search. Performance
must survive component ablation and out-of-distribution transfer.

## 7. Demography and network effects

**Primary evidence.** In a dual-task laboratory game, larger groups more often
maintained complex traits, improved a simpler trait, and retained diversity
([Derex et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12774)). Copying experiments
also found that access to more models, not only nominal group size, improved
reconstruction accuracy ([Muthukrishna et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.2511)).
Comparative data from ten Oceanic societies associated population size with
marine-foraging toolkit size and complexity
([Kline and Boyd 2010](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2010.0452)).

The broader empirical picture is mixed. A sample of nonindustrial food-
producing societies did not recover a general positive population-size effect
([Collard et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0072628)). A model
plus genetic demographic estimates linked density/contact to the timing of
Late Pleistocene behavioral-complexity proxies
([Powell, Shennan, and Thomas 2009](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170165)),
while a later reanalysis argued population size did not explain several major
past changes in cultural complexity
([Vaesen et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1520288113)).

**Mechanism.** More accessible and partially independent models can raise the
chance of retaining rare skills and encountering improvements. Fully connected
attention may instead collapse diversity around an early success. Ecology,
mobility, contact, skill difficulty, teaching investment, and preservation can
mediate or confound the relationship.

**Evidence status.** Established effects in the named laboratory tasks and
specific comparative sample; disputed as a universal population-size law or
single explanation of archaeological transitions.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Effective model diversity,
communication bandwidth, compute, total trials, and evaluator access can fully
explain a nominal group-size effect. Larger populations also incur search,
coordination, duplication, and misinformation costs. Never use raw agent count
as the causal treatment when message and evaluation budgets scale with it.

**Measurable prediction.** At fixed total samples, compute, and messages,
performance should track preregistered accessible diversity and topology more
closely than raw population count. Star, full, modular, dynamic, and partially
connected networks must be compared under the same attention budget.

## 8. Path dependence without mysticism

**Primary evidence.** Spaghetti-tower microsocieties exposed to delayed,
uncertain payoff feedback showed stronger group-specific traditions and weaker
successive improvement than groups with immediate measurement
([Caldwell and Millen 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2009.08.002)).
Networked naming experiments produced different local conventions and showed
scale-dependent convergence under specified interaction rules
([Centola and Baronchelli 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418838112)).

**Mechanism.** Early variants change later exposure, compatibility, switching
costs, and available recombinations. Positive feedback can preserve a useful
standard or an arbitrary lock-in. Path dependence is demonstrated by divergent
outcome distributions under controlled early perturbations, not merely by the
fact that history occurred.

**Evidence status.** Established laboratory path sensitivity; plausible in
institutions and technology; particular archaeological histories remain
underdetermined.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Stable environmental preference,
functional superiority, common priors, or independent convergence can generate
similar persistence. Random restarts and multiverse simulations may be cheaper
than maintaining live divergent cultures. Branching without compatibility
tests creates fragmentation.

**Measurable prediction.** Replaying the same task from controlled early
seeds should yield distinct durable lineages only where feedback or switching
cost is manipulated. A reversible migration or cross-lineage recombination
intervention should distinguish lock-in from enduring functional superiority.

## 9. Archaeological observation and equifinality

**Primary evidence.** Schiffer's artifact-life-history model distinguishes
items in systemic use from items transformed into archaeological context by
procurement, manufacture, use, maintenance, reuse, discard, and deposition
([Schiffer 1972](https://doi.org/10.2307/278203)). Premo's simulation shows that
time averaging can make neutral transmission data fail standard neutrality
tests ([Premo 2014](https://doi.org/10.1086/674873)).

Two deliberately engraved ochre pieces from stratified Middle Stone Age levels
at Blombos Cave were reported with a mean thermoluminescence age of about
77,000 years for the containing layers
([Henshilwood et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1067575)). The
material observation and chronology are evidence; “symbol,” “notation,”
“external memory,” “teaching aid,” and “modern cognition” are increasingly
strong behavioral interpretations requiring additional comparisons and models.

**Mechanism.** Behavior generates and uses objects; cultural and natural
formation processes transform their distribution; excavation and classification
sample the residue. Many different behavioral histories can therefore produce
similar artifact counts, shapes, or associations—equifinality.

**Evidence status.** Established formation and time-averaging problem;
artifact-specific behavioral inferences range from plausible to disputed.

**Strongest nulls and failure boundary.** Functional convergence, raw-material
constraints, copying error, individual reconstruction, exchange, migration,
curation, differential breakage, excavation strategy, and classification can
mimic cultural-lineage patterns. Absence is weak when preservation or recovery
probability is low; presence fixes neither use nor meaning. Candidate 014 must
carry chronology, spatial support, formation model, classification version,
selection, and alternative histories with every downstream claim.

**Measurable prediction.** On simulated assemblages with known behavior and
formation histories, a versioned observation model should return calibrated
intervals and abstain when transmission mechanisms are non-identifiable. On
real collections, conclusions should remain stable under plausible rebinning,
reclassification, preservation, and deposition-rate sensitivity analyses.

## Research disposition

**Promote now:** no new stable principle. Promote as guardrails the separations
between imitation and emulation, transmission and improvement, convention and
norm, current function and historical origin, nominal population size and
accessible model diversity, and artifact observation and behavior.

**Hold as an integration hypothesis:** **audited cumulative inheritance**—a
population can retain and recombine validated capability across turnover only
when generation, transmission channel, evaluation, lineage, external state,
and governance are independently observable and versioned. This is a candidate
composition of `P-003`, `P-004`, `P-005`, `P-009`, `P-012`, and `P-013`, not a
fourteenth principle. Candidate 004 owns endogenous generation; candidate 008
owns strategic allocation; candidate 011 owns operational-to-longitudinal
governance; candidate 014 owns observation dependencies.

**Reject as unsupported prescriptions:** copy prestigious agents by default;
make every population fully connected; increase headcount to obtain complexity;
use punishment as a generic alignment method; treat documentation as shared
understanding; infer teaching, symbolism, or cognition directly from artifact
form; or describe later-generation improvement as energy efficiency without a
matched cumulative budget.
