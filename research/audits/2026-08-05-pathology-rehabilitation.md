# Pathology and Rehabilitation: Failure, Selection, Reserve, and Recovery

**Audit date:** 2026-08-05  
**Scope:** cancer as somatic evolution and control failure; immune tolerance and autoimmunity; neurodegeneration and proteostasis; compensation, restoration, rehabilitation dose and timing, learned non-use, reserve, recurrence, and adverse adaptation  
**Evidence policy:** imported conversations and concept notes can motivate questions but are not evidence. This audit prioritizes primary experiments, trials, and clinical cohorts; conceptual reviews are used only to define terms.  
**Status:** breadth audit; temporary claim IDs in this file are not entries in the shared claim ledger.

## Purpose and executive conclusion

Pathology is primarily a **negative blueprint**. It reveals how otherwise useful processes fail when selection operates at the wrong level, tolerance loses context, maintenance capacity falls below load, or plasticity reinforces a harmful strategy. Rehabilitation adds a second warning: improved task performance need not mean restoration of the damaged mechanism. An organism can regain an outcome by changing its body, policy, environment, or resource use.

The transferable lessons are therefore constraints, measurements, and failure tests rather than direct recipes:

- local adaptation is not system alignment;
- a sampled response is not eradication of hidden heterogeneous state;
- anomaly evidence is not action authority;
- observed aggregates are not automatically the toxic species;
- functional compensation is not the same as mechanistic restoration;
- more adaptation, earlier intervention, and greater dose are not monotonic goods;
- reserve can preserve service while concealing accumulating damage; and
- recurrence and adverse adaptation must be measured after the apparent success state.

Most candidate mechanisms deduplicate into existing registry principles—especially [P-004 diversity, selection, and protection](../principle-registry.md#p-004--diversity-selection-and-protection), [P-005 use-dependent topology](../principle-registry.md#p-005--use-dependent-topology), [P-006 homeostatic negative feedback](../principle-registry.md#p-006--homeostatic-negative-feedback), [P-008 compartmentalized interaction](../principle-registry.md#p-008--compartmentalized-interaction), [P-009 maintenance plane](../principle-registry.md#p-009--maintenance-plane), and [P-012 memory matched to information lifetime](../principle-registry.md#p-012--memory-matched-to-information-lifetime). The one residual worth holding is **compensation-aware recovery accounting**: an evaluation discipline that reports restored native capability separately from compensating-path dependence, extra resources, transfer failure, and relapse. It is not yet a new design principle.

## Evidence hierarchy and terms that must not be collapsed

| Evidence class | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Randomized clinical trial with active or dose-equivalent control | Causal effect of a specified treatment bundle in the enrolled population under the trial protocol | A single biological mechanism, general benefit at other doses/times, or recovery of native control |
| Prospective cohort or stored longitudinal samples | Temporal association, prognosis, and candidate early markers | Benefit of acting on the marker or absence of confounding and ascertainment bias |
| Human pathology, imaging, or kinematics | Association between tissue/behavioral state and function | Whether the state is causal, compensatory, protective, or an epiphenomenon |
| Controlled animal or cellular perturbation | Mechanism within the experimental model and intervention | Human efficacy, safety, dose, timing, or cross-species generality |
| Mathematical model | Consequences of declared assumptions and parameter ranges | That the assumptions identify the real state or that the policy is clinically superior |
| Review or terminology paper | Coherent definitions and synthesis | Primary confirmation of a quantitative claim |

The following distinctions are mandatory throughout the project:

- **Response** is a measured change during or after intervention; **eradication** means no viable disease state remains at a declared detection limit.
- **Recurrence** is renewed detection after an interval; it can reflect residual state, reseeding, observation limits, or new emergence.
- **Tolerance** is an action policy under uncertainty; it is not a fixed whitelist of all benign state.
- **Aggregate burden** is a measurement; **toxic species** and **causal mechanism** require separate perturbation evidence.
- **Restitution/restoration** recovers the premorbid or reference organization; **compensation** reaches an outcome through a changed organization or environment.[^levin2009]
- **Improvement** is a change in an outcome; **recovery** is ambiguous unless the measured level—task, strategy, impairment, tissue, or participation—is named.
- **Reserve** is latent capacity to sustain a criterion under disturbance; **repair** reverses damage. Reserve can mask damage without repairing it.
- **Plasticity** means capacity for change; it carries no claim that the change is adaptive at the system level.

## Timescale, information, authority, and budget ladder

| Layer | Typical pathology/rehabilitation process | Information available | Safe authority boundary | Required budget account |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milliseconds–seconds | motor correction, local alarm, reflex-like compensation | local state and short history | bounded reversible action | latency, operations, energy |
| Minutes–hours | inflammatory signaling, treatment exposure, practice session, acute mobilization | sampled biomarkers and task feedback | rate-limited intervention with stop condition | dose, repetitions, duration, monitoring |
| Days–weeks | clonal selection, immune regulation, tissue remodeling, skill acquisition | partial longitudinal state | reviewed schedule adaptation | cumulative exposure, adverse events, clinician/operator time |
| Months–years | recurrence, chronic degeneration, reserve depletion, functional consolidation | sparse visits, censored outcomes, changing environment | governance over durable changes | lifetime energy, storage, maintenance, recurrence surveillance |
| Generations / deployment epochs | somatic evolution or inherited design selection | historical outcomes and survivor-biased records | architecture or policy revision | retraining, migration, failure externalities |

Fast detection should not silently inherit slow, irreversible authority. A marker can trigger observation or a reversible probe before it can trigger deletion, permanent isolation, or architectural consolidation. This repeats [local autonomy with exception escalation](../principle-registry.md#p-002--local-autonomy-with-exception-escalation), not a pathology-specific principle.

## PATH-01 — Cancer is somatic evolution, not a goal-alignment recipe

Nowell proposed that tumors progress through sequential selection among mutant cell populations.[^nowell1976] Modern sequencing supports branched rather than purely linear histories: multiregion renal-cancer samples contained spatially separated subclones and evidence that a single biopsy missed part of the mutational landscape.[^gerlinger2012] The rigorous transferable observation is that agents sharing an origin can develop heritable variation, compete under local constraints, and respond differently to intervention.

An illustrative eco-evolutionary state model is

$$
\frac{dN_i}{dt}
= r_iN_i\left(1-\frac{\sum_j \alpha_{ij}N_j}{K_i}\right)
-d_i(u,t)N_i
+\sum_{j\ne i}\left(\mu_{ji}N_j-\mu_{ij}N_i\right).
$$

Here $N_i$ is the number of cells in subpopulation $i$ (cells), $r_i$ is its net intrinsic growth rate ($\mathrm{time}^{-1}$), $K_i$ is a scale for effective carrying capacity (cells), $\alpha_{ij}$ is dimensionless competition coupling when $N_j/K_i$ is used, $d_i$ is intervention-dependent removal or death rate ($\mathrm{time}^{-1}$), $u$ is a declared treatment input such as concentration ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$) or administered dose ($\mathrm{mg}$), and $\mu_{ij}$ is transition rate from $i$ to $j$ ($\mathrm{time}^{-1}$). The equation is illustrative, not an identified universal tumor law; density dependence, spatial structure, immune interaction, and phenotype switching need model-specific terms.

Observations are also incomplete:

$$
y_k(t)=\sum_i h_{ki}(t)N_i(t)+\varepsilon_k(t),
$$

where $y_k$ is a measured biomarker or imaging feature with its own unit, $h_{ki}$ is the assay's sensitivity to subpopulation $i$ (measurement unit per cell), and $\varepsilon_k$ is measurement and sampling error. A falling $y_k$ does not identify every $N_i$.

**Failure boundary.** Cancer does not demonstrate that unconstrained internal evolution is useful. It demonstrates the opposite: optimization at a subunit's reproductive level can destroy the host. Any AI analogy must specify the system-level objective, who is allowed to reproduce or rewrite state, how resources are capped, how hidden lineages are observed, and how changes can be revoked.

**Strongest nulls.** Before invoking cancer biology, compare against ordinary online learning, mixture models, change-point detection, ensemble diversity, resource quotas, and model-predictive control under the same observations and compute. A pathology-inspired mechanism loses if a standard controller matches recurrence, robustness, and resource cost.

## PATH-02 — Heterogeneity, sampling, and recurrence

Relapsed acute myeloid leukemia genomes supported at least two recurrence patterns: expansion of a founding clone with additional mutations or expansion of a subclone from the primary leukemia.[^ding2012] Recurrent gliomas likewise showed treatment-associated evolutionary patterns; temozolomide exposure was linked to hypermutation and mutations in a growth-factor pathway in a subset of recurrences.[^johnson2014] These studies establish scoped historical relationships, not deterministic recurrence rules for every cancer.

A recurrence endpoint should distinguish disease process from detection process. Its cause-specific hazard can be written

$$
h(t\mid \mathcal H_t,u_t)
=\lim_{\Delta t\to0}
\frac{\Pr(t\le T<t+\Delta t\mid T\ge t,\mathcal H_t,u_t)}{\Delta t},
$$

where $T$ is recurrence time (for example, days), $\mathcal H_t$ is the measured history up to $t$, $u_t$ is intervention history, and $h$ has units $\mathrm{time}^{-1}$. Death, loss to follow-up, scheduled observation, and changing detection limits can create competing risks or interval censoring. Reporting “no recurrence” without follow-up duration and detection threshold is therefore incomplete.

For AI systems, the closest analogue is not merely “keep diverse experts.” It is a jointly hidden-state and observation problem: a subsystem can appear healthy under aggregate metrics while a rare failure mode survives, and aggressive optimization can select for modes not represented in the current metric. This deduplicates into [P-004](../principle-registry.md#p-004--diversity-selection-and-protection), [P-007 prediction-error allocation](../principle-registry.md#p-007--prediction-error-allocation), and established surveillance methods. A new biological label adds no value unless it changes the test design.

**Failure boundary.** Diversity is not intrinsically protective. Diverse harmful states can defeat a narrow intervention; maintaining diversity also consumes resources. The project must distinguish diversity of protected useful hypotheses from diversity of ungoverned replicators.

## PATH-03 — Treatment selection and adaptive therapy

Evolutionary models predict that simultaneous combinations can outperform sequential monotherapies when no single mutant is resistant to all agents, but conclusions depend on mutation, growth, and cross-resistance assumptions.[^bozic2013] Adaptive therapy instead attempts to retain treatment-sensitive cells that can suppress resistant cells, varying treatment according to observed burden rather than pursuing maximum immediate kill.

In metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, an early adaptive-abiraterone pilot reported that 10 of 11 patients remained radiographically stable at reporting while receiving an average cumulative dose of 47% of standard continuous dosing; the comparison was not randomized.[^zhang2017] A later report compared 17 adaptive-therapy patients with 16 contemporaneous continuous-therapy patients and reported median radiographic time to progression of 33.5 versus 14.3 months, median overall survival of 58.5 versus 31.3 months, and a mortality hazard ratio of 0.41 (95% CI 0.20–0.83), with abiraterone administered 54% of the time on average.[^zhang2022] These are promising but small, disease- and drug-specific, model-guided nonrandomized results. They do not establish adaptive dosing as a general clinical rule or prove the assumed competition mechanism.

For a system analogue, the candidate is a constrained partially observed controller:

$$
b_t(x)=p(x_t=x\mid y_{0:t},u_{0:t-1}),
\qquad
u_t=\pi(b_t,B_t),
$$

where $x_t$ is latent subsystem state, $b_t$ its posterior belief distribution, $y_{0:t}$ the observations, $B_t$ the remaining multidimensional budget, $\pi$ the intervention policy, and $u_t$ the intervention. The controller should be compared with fixed schedules, threshold rules, and model-predictive control. A policy that preserves a “sensitive competitor” is unsafe if the competitor itself causes unacceptable harm or if observation cannot distinguish the states.

**Failure boundaries.** Adaptive control can fail when the ecological interaction is wrong, resistant burden is underestimated, action effects are delayed, the safe burden ceiling is narrow, state transitions are irreversible, or exploration harms the host. A successful average response cannot substitute for tail-risk, recurrence, and time-to-detection analysis.

## PATH-04 — Immune tolerance is layered and cost-sensitive

The immune evidence rejects a single “self versus non-self classifier.” In mice, thymic clonal elimination removed T cells recognizing particular self-associated antigens.[^kappler1987] A transgenic B-cell model showed a different outcome: self-reactive cells could persist but become functionally silenced, or anergic.[^goodnow1988] Aire-dependent expression of tissue-associated antigens in thymic medullary epithelial cells broadened what developing T cells could be tested against.[^anderson2002] Peripheral CD25-expressing regulatory T cells and the transcription factor Foxp3 provided further control beyond central deletion.[^sakaguchi1995][^hori2003]

Thus, tolerance includes at least representation, deletion, silencing, suppression, context-sensitive activation, and ongoing peripheral control. These mechanisms carry different costs. Deleting a useful clone, failing to contain a harmful clone, suppressing a legitimate response, and delaying action are not interchangeable errors.

A minimal decision-theoretic statement is

$$
R(a\mid x)=\sum_s L(a,s)\Pr(s\mid x),
\qquad
a^*(x)=\arg\min_a R(a\mid x),
$$

where $x$ is observed evidence, $s$ a latent state such as benign/self, harmful, or uncertain, $a$ an action such as tolerate, monitor, suppress, isolate, or escalate, and $L(a,s)$ a declared loss. Loss may be measured in service-loss seconds, joules, recovery time, safety events, or another stakeholder-grounded unit; it must not be an unexplained dimensionless score. The posterior depends on prevalence and context, so an anomaly score alone cannot choose the action.

For AI, this mostly reproduces cost-sensitive classification, abstention, staged authorization, and [P-002 local autonomy with exception escalation](../principle-registry.md#p-002--local-autonomy-with-exception-escalation). It also intersects the [security audit](2026-08-05-security-cryptography.md): immune metaphors do not solve adversarial identification, provenance, authorization, or base-rate problems.

**Failure boundaries.** A learned tolerance policy can normalize gradual compromise, suppress novelty, inherit biased labels, or be manipulated through context signals. A permanent denylist can also turn temporary uncertainty into irreversible capacity loss. Safe transfer requires reversible quarantine, time-bounded decisions, multiple evidence channels, and an explicit escalation authority.

## PATH-05 — Preclinical autoimmunity is risk evidence, not diagnosis or authority

In stored serum from people who later developed systemic lupus erythematosus, 115 of 130 (88%) had at least one tested autoantibody before diagnosis; autoantibodies were detectable as far as 9.4 years before diagnosis, with a reported mean of 3.3 years.[^arbuckle2003] Sampling schedules limit the precision of onset estimates, and the cohort was selected through eventual disease. The result shows that molecular evidence can precede clinical threshold crossing; it does not say every marker-positive person will progress or that preemptive suppression is beneficial.

The engineering analogue is an early-warning state with uncertain conversion:

$$
\Pr(T\le t+\tau\mid T>t,x_{0:t}),
$$

where $T$ is time to declared failure, $\tau$ is a prediction horizon, and $x_{0:t}$ is the measured history. Calibration must be reported over horizons and subgroups. Positive predictive value depends on prevalence:

$$
\mathrm{PPV}
=\frac{\mathrm{Se}\,p}
{\mathrm{Se}\,p+(1-\mathrm{Sp})(1-p)},
$$

where $\mathrm{Se}$ is sensitivity, $\mathrm{Sp}$ specificity, and $p$ event prevalence. All are dimensionless probabilities. High sensitivity can still yield many false alarms when $p$ is low.

**Strongest nulls.** Compare with calibrated survival models, sequential probability-ratio tests, change-point detection, conformal risk control, and simple repeated-threshold policies. Prospective value requires an intervention trial or a decision-curve analysis with real false-action costs, not only earlier detection.

**Recurrence boundary.** Relapse after immunosuppression may reflect persistent drivers, re-expansion of surviving clones, altered regulation, or a new trigger. A generic “memory caused recurrence” story is underidentified without lineage and longitudinal state evidence. Memory-specific transfer belongs in the [memory, replay, and forgetting audit](2026-08-05-memory-replay-forgetting.md).

## PATH-06 — Proteostasis is a capacity–load problem

Alpha-synuclein was identified as a component of Lewy bodies in Parkinsonian pathology,[^spillantini1997] but inclusion membership does not by itself establish which molecular species causes dysfunction. Experiments in model organisms show that proteostasis capacity is mutable: increasing the proteasome subunit RPN-6 improved resistance to proteotoxic stress and influenced lifespan in *C. elegans*,[^vilchez2012] while the heat-shock response declined sharply near the onset of reproduction in the same organism through a chromatin-associated program.[^labbadia2015] Inducing autophagy by mTOR inhibition reduced polyglutamine toxicity in fly and mouse Huntington-disease models.[^ravikumar2004] These are mechanistic perturbations in scoped models, not proof of a universal human anti-neurodegeneration intervention.

An illustrative aggregate balance is

$$
\frac{dA}{dt}=k_nU^n+k_gUA-k_cA,
$$

where $U$ and $A$ are soluble precursor and measured aggregate concentrations ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$), $n$ is a dimensionless reaction order, $k_n$ has units $(\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}})^{1-n}\mathrm{time}^{-1}$, $k_g$ has units $(\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}})^{-1}\mathrm{time}^{-1}$, and $k_c$ has units $\mathrm{time}^{-1}$. The first term represents nucleation, the second templated or aggregate-dependent growth, and the last clearance. Real systems may require fragmentation, transport, compartment-specific clearance, saturation, and multiple measured species.

This equation makes two boundaries visible:

1. Lower measured $A$ can follow lower production, slower conversion, greater sequestration into an unmeasured form, transport, dilution, or faster clearance. It does not identify the mechanism.
2. Raising $k_c$ consumes resources and can damage useful components or alter other growth and signaling functions. Clearance is not free.

The transferable system question is whether maintenance throughput can keep a damaged-state distribution below an operational threshold while preserving useful state. That is already the domain of [P-009 maintenance plane](../principle-registry.md#p-009--maintenance-plane) and the [cellular quality-control audit](2026-08-05-cellular-quality-control.md), which owns staged detection, sequestration, repair, removal, replacement, and replenishment.

**Failure boundary.** Do not equate garbage collection, weight pruning, checkpoint deletion, or expert retirement with proteostasis unless production, diagnosis, containment, disposal, replacement, and resource cost are all represented. Removal without replacement can collapse capacity; sequestration can preserve a hazard; replenishment can reproduce a defect.

## PATH-07 — Pathology can propagate through susceptible compartments

Injection of brain extract containing aggregated tau into susceptible transgenic mice induced and spread tau pathology,[^clavaguera2009] and inoculation with preformed alpha-synuclein fibrils initiated progressive Lewy-body-like pathology and neurodegeneration in nontransgenic mice.[^luk2012] These experiments support seeded propagation in particular animal models. They do not establish a single route or rate in humans, simple interpersonal contagion, or that all observed spread is templated transmission rather than selective vulnerability plus network structure.

A generic compartment model is

$$
\frac{dA_i}{dt}
=g_i(A_i,U_i)-c_iA_i
+\sum_{j\ne i}\left(q_{ji}A_j-q_{ij}A_i\right),
$$

where $A_i$ is aggregate concentration in volume-normalized compartment $i$ ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$), $g_i$ local production or conversion ($\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,time^{-1}}$), $c_i$ clearance rate ($\mathrm{time}^{-1}$), and $q_{ij}$ effective transport rate from $i$ to $j$ ($\mathrm{time}^{-1}$). Unequal physical volumes require explicit volume-ratio factors. A fitted $q_{ij}$ is not evidence for a physical route unless interventions or tracing identify it.

For distributed AI systems, error propagation, poisoned shared state, copied checkpoints, or coupled expert updates are already studied by fault containment, provenance, access control, and [P-008 compartmentalized interaction](../principle-registry.md#p-008--compartmentalized-interaction). The biological evidence motivates topology-aware tracing and containment tests; it does not replace them.

**Strongest nulls.** Compare with standard dependency graphs, causal tracing, taint analysis, epidemic-on-network models, replica isolation, and rollback. A propagation-inspired system must improve fault localization or survival under a fixed observation and intervention budget.

## PATH-08 — Functional compensation is not mechanistic restoration

After stroke, a person can complete a reaching task through a different combination of arm, trunk, and environmental support. In a small three-dimensional kinematic study, nine patients used greater trunk recruitment than nine controls, allowing endpoint success despite altered joint coordination.[^cirstea2000] A proof-of-concept analysis after constraint-induced movement therapy found improvement consistent with task-specific compensation rather than normalization of motor control.[^kitago2013] Trunk restraint during task-specific training has also been tested in a randomized trial, illustrating that the strategy itself can be an intervention target rather than mere noise.[^michaelsen2006]

The observed task score is therefore underidentified:

$$
Y_{\mathrm{task}}=F(I,C,E),
$$

where $I$ represents recovered native or target capability, $C$ compensating strategy, and $E$ environmental support. $Y_{\mathrm{task}}$ inherits the task metric's unit; the decomposition is conceptual unless $I$, $C$, and $E$ are separately operationalized. One scalar outcome cannot recover three latent causes.

A compensation-aware report should retain at least two distinct objects:

$$
D_{\mathrm{native}}=d(z,z_{\mathrm{ref}}),
\qquad
\mathbf B_{\mathrm{comp}}=(E_{\mathrm{extra}},M_{\mathrm{extra}},L_{\mathrm{extra}},P_{\mathrm{fragile}}).
$$

Here $z$ is a declared internal or behavioral state vector and $z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ a justified reference; $D_{\mathrm{native}}$ has the metric's stated unit or is explicitly normalized. The compensation-burden vector records extra energy $E_{\mathrm{extra}}$ (joules), memory $M_{\mathrm{extra}}$ (bytes), latency $L_{\mathrm{extra}}$ (seconds), and the probability of failure under a declared removal or shift test $P_{\mathrm{fragile}}$ (dimensionless). These components must not be silently collapsed into a weighted score.

The engineering analogue is direct: a damaged module may appear “recovered” because a larger fallback model, human operator, cache, tool, or permissive benchmark compensates. Exact state restoration is already covered in the [fault tolerance and reconstruction audit](2026-08-05-fault-tolerance-and-reconstruction.md). This audit's residual is the accounting rule that functional success and mechanism restoration remain separate axes.

**Failure boundary.** Compensation is often desirable and can be the safest attainable goal. The problem is not compensation; it is claiming restoration, hiding recurring cost, or deploying beyond the compensating context. Conversely, insisting on native restoration can reject a robust, cheap workaround. The evaluation should expose the trade rather than predetermine it.

## PATH-09 — Learned non-use and forced-use interventions

Early work used forced use of a hemiplegic upper extremity to test reversal of learned non-use in chronic stroke and head-injury patients.[^wolf1989] The multisite EXCITE randomized trial later found that constraint-induced movement therapy improved upper-extremity outcomes in selected participants three to nine months after stroke who retained some movement.[^wolf2006] The intervention was a bundle—including restraint of the less-affected limb, intensive shaped practice, and procedures intended to transfer use into daily life—so the trial establishes the bundle's effect in its eligibility range, not a pure learned-non-use mechanism.

Animal mapping studies add mechanistic plausibility. Skilled use changed primary motor-cortex movement representations in adult squirrel monkeys,[^nudo1996maps] and rehabilitative training after focal ischemic injury was associated with reorganization and functional recovery.[^nudo1996stroke] These maps are dynamic correlates of use and training, not proof that a skill is stored in a single cortical patch or that map expansion is always beneficial.

For AI, the candidate problem is **use allocation under feedback**. If a router initially avoids a temporarily weak expert, the expert receives less data and may become permanently weak. But forced routing can also waste compute, corrupt predictions, or prevent useful specialization. Existing methods—load-balancing losses, exploration, rehearsal, expert dropout, uncertainty sampling, and [P-005 use-dependent topology](../principle-registry.md#p-005--use-dependent-topology)—are the correct nulls.

**Measurable prediction.** Under a reversible synthetic lesion, a bounded forced-use or exploration schedule should improve post-lesion expert competence and later autonomous selection relative to identical-budget entropy regularization or uniform exploration. It fails if task loss, calibration, expert diversity, or energy-normalized recovery is no better after the forced-use phase ends.

## PATH-10 — Rehabilitation dose is a schedule, and timing can reverse the result

“Dose” cannot be represented by minutes alone. A minimally explicit session schedule is

$$
\mathcal U=\{(t_s,\Delta t_s,n_s,I_s,c_s)\}_{s=1}^{S},
$$

where $s$ indexes the $S$ sessions, $t_s$ is session start time after injury (hours or days), $\Delta t_s$ duration (minutes), $n_s$ repetitions (count), $I_s$ a declared intensity such as mechanical work per repetition (joules), heart-rate reserve fraction (dimensionless), or task difficulty, and $c_s$ the task/content label. The repetition dose is $D_{\mathrm{rep}}=\sum_s n_s$ (count). If intensity is a power $P_s$ in watts, physical work is $D_{\mathrm{work}}=\sum_s P_s\Delta t_s$ (joules after converting time to seconds). Minutes, repetitions, and work are not interchangeable.

The AVERT randomized trial assigned 2,104 patients to very early, more frequent mobilization within 24 hours or usual care. A favorable modified Rankin Scale score at three months occurred in 46% versus 50%, with adjusted odds ratio 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90; $p=0.004$); mortality did not differ significantly.[^avert2015] This is strong evidence against the unrestricted rule “earlier and more is better,” although the intervention varied timing, frequency, and amount as a bundle.

The ICARE randomized trial enrolled 361 patients and found no significant 12-month superiority of a structured task-oriented upper-extremity program over dose-equivalent usual customary care or monitored usual care in a primarily moderately impaired population.[^winstein2016] This is an important active-control null: theory-rich content did not automatically beat comparable therapy exposure.

The phase-II CPASS study randomized 72 participants to an additional 20 hours of motor therapy initiated in acute (within 30 days), subacute (two to three months), chronic (six months or later), or control conditions. At one year, reported Action Research Arm Test differences versus control were $6.87\pm2.63$ points for subacute ($p=0.009$), $5.25\pm2.59$ for acute ($p=0.043$), and $2.41\pm2.25$ for chronic ($p=0.29$).[^dromerick2021] The small phase-II result motivates timing research; it does not establish one universal critical window or justify withholding usual care.

A comparative causal effect can be reported as

$$
\Delta=
\left(Y_1-Y_0\right)_{\mathrm{candidate}}
-\left(Y_1-Y_0\right)_{\mathrm{control}},
$$

where $Y_0$ and $Y_1$ are baseline and declared follow-up outcomes for the candidate and control groups. $\Delta$ has the same units as $Y$ and requires a confidence interval. It has a causal interpretation only under the design's randomization, adherence, missing-data, and interference assumptions. Natural recovery, baseline severity, co-interventions, and test familiarity must be controlled.

For AI, training schedule, repair timing, replay frequency, and update intensity may interact with system state. The strongest null is not “no adaptation”; it is the best equal-compute schedule found by ordinary hyperparameter optimization or adaptive control. Claims of a critical period require a state-by-time interaction that predicts out-of-sample outcomes, not a post hoc curve.

## PATH-11 — Reserve can preserve function while hiding fragility

An autopsy series described people with preserved mental status despite numerous neocortical plaques and proposed that larger brains or more neurons provided reserve before a clinical threshold was crossed.[^katzman1988] Cognitive-reserve theory subsequently distinguished passive capacity from active flexibility or efficiency in using networks.[^stern2002] These sources support a useful explanatory construct, but education, occupation, brain measures, and pathology are entangled with selection and measurement. They do not show that increasing a proxy will causally prevent disease.

Engineering reserve must be operational rather than metaphorical. For a quality measure $Q$, minimum acceptable quality $q_{\min}$, disturbance family $\delta_a$ of amplitude $a$ with declared unit, and risk tolerance $\alpha$, define

$$
H_q=\sup\left\{a\ge0:
\Pr\left[Q(x,\delta_a)\ge q_{\min}\right]\ge1-\alpha
\right\}.
$$

$H_q$ has the same unit as $a$—for example, missing experts, watts lost, milliseconds of added latency, or corruption rate. The disturbance distribution, horizon, and probability-estimation method must be reported.

Reserve overlaps strongly with slack, redundancy, graceful degradation, and capacity margins in the [high-reliability organizations and incident-learning audit](2026-08-05-high-reliability-organizations-incident-learning.md). It is not a new principle. The valuable pathology-derived warning is that stable output can coexist with falling $H_q$.

**Measurable prediction.** A health monitor that tests reserve through bounded perturbations should predict threshold-crossing failures earlier than output-only monitoring at the same observation and intervention budget. It loses if the probes cause more harm, or if ordinary uncertainty and utilization metrics predict equally well.

## PATH-12 — Plasticity can be adverse

After amputation, a small human study reported a strong association ($r=0.93$) between phantom-limb pain and cortical reorganization.[^flor1995] This correlation does not establish that all reorganization causes pain or that reversing the map alone will cure it. In an intentionally narrow primate model, two owl monkeys trained in highly repetitive stereotyped hand movements developed dedifferentiated somatosensory representations and motor-control abnormalities resembling focal dystonia.[^byl1996] In rats with sensorimotor-cortex lesions, early use pressure on the impaired forelimb exaggerated neuronal injury.[^kozlowski1996] Together with AVERT, these studies reject “more plasticity” as a safe objective.

For learning systems, adverse adaptation includes shortcut reinforcement, feedback-loop polarization, catastrophic specialization, overfitting to the rehabilitation curriculum, corruption propagation, and reliance on an expensive compensating path. A plasticity controller must optimize a vector of outcomes over a post-intervention horizon, including transfer, calibration, energy, recurrence, and reversibility.

**Failure boundaries.** A change in representation, routing, sparsity, or activation is not evidence of useful consolidation. Training-induced structure may be causal, compensatory, neutral, or harmful. The minimum mechanism test removes or perturbs the alleged adapted path and checks both target performance and off-target cost.

### Concepts that look similar but remain separate

- **Tumor competition** and **mixture-of-experts specialization** share selection mathematics, but only the latter is governed toward a declared task and resource objective.
- **Immune tolerance** and **security authorization** both gate action, but biological analogy cannot establish identity, provenance, or cryptographic authority.
- **Protein clearance** and **memory forgetting** both remove state, but their utility criteria, replacement processes, and harms are different.
- **Functional compensation** and **fault reconstruction** can both restore service, but rehabilitation asks whether the restored output depends on a fragile, costly, or context-bound new strategy.
- **Cognitive reserve** and **redundancy** both create margin, but reserve is often a latent explanatory construct; engineering redundancy can and must be measured under declared disturbances.
- **Adverse plasticity** and **catastrophic forgetting** both concern harmful learning, but adverse adaptation also includes stable performance through harmful strategies, feedback loops, and off-target costs without forgetting.

## Held residual: compensation-aware recovery accounting

**Provisional label:** PATH-R1, compensation-aware recovery accounting  
**Status:** held; methodological extension, not a registry principle  
**Why it may be useful:** existing task benchmarks frequently treat endpoint success as recovery. The rehabilitation evidence makes this inference unsafe when alternative strategies, tools, routing paths, human escalation, or environmental supports can produce the same score.

A recovery report should include:

1. **Target function:** task success, quality, and calibration on the recovery distribution.
2. **Native-path distance:** declared mechanistic, representational, routing, or behavioral deviation from a justified healthy reference; “native” must not mean “historically first” without justification.
3. **Compensation burden:** added joules, operations, bytes, latency, external calls, human minutes, and privileged permissions.
4. **Dependency test:** performance after removing or perturbing the suspected compensating path.
5. **Transfer test:** performance under task, environment, load, and sensor shifts not used in recovery training.
6. **Recurrence test:** failure and hazard over a declared post-intervention horizon with detection limits.
7. **Reserve test:** remaining performance margin under bounded disturbance.
8. **Adverse-effect test:** off-target regression, safety events, calibration loss, or damage caused by the recovery schedule.

This residual should be rejected as redundant if ordinary multi-objective evaluation plus fault-injection already captures these axes with equal clarity and lower overhead. It should be promoted only after it changes model selection or exposes false recovery in at least two distinct system families.

## Equal-budget falsification program

### Common protocol

Every comparison declares the same budget vector

$$
\mathbf B=(E,C,M,O,U,H),
$$

where $E$ is energy (joules), $C$ is arithmetic or accelerator work (operations, FLOPs, or accelerator-seconds), $M$ peak and persistent memory (bytes), $O$ observation volume (samples or bytes), $U$ intervention count and magnitude in a declared unit, and $H$ human oversight (seconds). Budgets remain a vector unless stakeholders publish a conversion rule before evaluation.

Outcomes also remain a vector: task loss, calibration error, native-path distance, compensation burden, severe failure count, recurrence hazard, false interventions, detection delay (seconds), recovery time (seconds), energy (joules), peak memory (bytes), and human time (seconds). Report distributions, confidence intervals, worst-group outcomes, and tail quantiles where safety matters. A candidate is not successful merely because one weighted average improves.

All tests require preregistered lesions or disturbances, held-out environments, ablation of the claimed mechanism, matched tuning opportunity, and at least one strong non-biological null.

### Test A — Somatic-evolution controller versus standard adaptive control

- **System:** population-based learner or mixture of specialists under a changing objective.
- **Candidate:** lineage-tracked variation and selection with host-level constraints.
- **Nulls:** model-predictive control, online ensemble weighting, and a fixed robust policy.
- **Disturbance:** rare resistant subpopulation or objective-local exploit seeded below initial detection.
- **Pass condition:** lower cumulative host loss and recurrence at equal $\mathbf B$, without increased catastrophic tail risk.
- **Falsifier:** the benefit disappears when observations and model capacity are matched, or internal variants exploit the evaluation metric.

### Test B — Heterogeneous-state surveillance and recurrence

- **System:** distributed service with multiple latent fault modes.
- **Candidate:** lineage-aware, spatially stratified sampling and post-response surveillance.
- **Nulls:** uniform sampling, risk-weighted sampling, and standard change-point detection.
- **Measurements:** detection delay (seconds), missed-mode rate, false-alarm rate, recurrence hazard, and observation bytes.
- **Pass condition:** candidate identifies rare surviving modes earlier at equal observation and intervention budgets.
- **Falsifier:** apparent benefit comes from more samples, privileged lineage labels, or a lower action threshold.

### Test C — Layered tolerance versus anomaly-threshold action

- **System:** safety monitor facing benign novelty and harmful events at realistic base rates.
- **Candidate:** context-conditioned action ladder—observe, reversible quarantine, limited suppression, and escalated removal.
- **Nulls:** calibrated binary classifier, selective classifier with abstention, and cost-sensitive thresholding.
- **Measurements:** expected declared loss, false suppression, missed harm, delay, operator time, and service loss.
- **Pass condition:** lower loss across prevalence shifts without hidden human labor.
- **Falsifier:** ordinary calibration plus abstention matches the result, or the layered system normalizes slow compromise.

### Test D — Proteostasis-inspired maintenance versus ordinary quality control

- **System:** long-lived model with accumulating corrupted cache, adapters, experts, or state.
- **Candidate:** load/capacity monitor with containment, repair, clearance, and replenishment.
- **Nulls:** garbage collection, least-recently-used eviction, checkpoint rollback, health scoring, and the existing cellular-quality-control pipeline.
- **Measurements:** corruption burden, useful-state loss, downtime, replacement quality, joules, and recurrence.
- **Pass condition:** better long-horizon integrity at equal energy, memory, and observations.
- **Falsifier:** measured “aggregate” falls while function or reserve worsens, or standard maintenance matches the result.

### Test E — Restoration versus compensation after a controlled lesion

- **System:** robot policy, modular model, or tool-using agent with a known reversible lesion.
- **Candidate:** recovery method selected using compensation-aware accounting.
- **Nulls:** task-score-only selection, exact checkpoint restoration, redundancy/failover, and constraint-guided reconstruction.
- **Measurements:** task score, $D_{\mathrm{native}}$, $\mathbf B_{\mathrm{comp}}$, removal-test loss, transfer, recurrence, and reserve.
- **Pass condition:** the accounting predicts which apparently recovered systems fail when support is removed or context shifts.
- **Falsifier:** internal-distance measures do not predict any relevant outcome beyond cost and robustness metrics.

### Test F — Bounded forced use after routing collapse

- **System:** sparse expert model with one expert temporarily degraded so the router avoids it.
- **Candidate:** time-bounded forced routing followed by autonomous selection.
- **Nulls:** uniform exploration, entropy regularization, load-balancing loss, rehearsal, and expert dropout.
- **Measurements:** expert competence, routing calibration, task loss, energy, and post-intervention autonomous use.
- **Pass condition:** durable competence and system performance improve after forced use ends at equal training tokens and operations.
- **Falsifier:** gains vanish outside trained tasks, healthy experts degrade, or standard exploration wins.

### Test G — State-dependent recovery timing and dose

- **System:** continual learner recovering from a timestamped synthetic lesion.
- **Candidate:** schedule conditioned on measured post-lesion state.
- **Nulls:** matched early, late, uniform, and hyperparameter-optimized schedules.
- **Factorization:** independently vary start time, repetitions, intensity, content, and spacing where feasible.
- **Measurements:** difference in changes with confidence intervals, adverse regressions, compute, and long-horizon recurrence.
- **Pass condition:** a preregistered state-by-timing or state-by-dose interaction replicates on held-out tasks.
- **Falsifier:** “critical window” disappears under active controls or equal tuning.

### Test H — Reserve depletion and adverse adaptation

- **System:** recovered model with stable headline performance.
- **Candidate:** reserve probes plus post-recovery adverse-adaptation monitoring.
- **Nulls:** utilization, uncertainty, calibration, and conventional stress testing.
- **Disturbances:** progressive capacity removal, sensor shift, compensator removal, feedback-loop exposure, and off-target tasks.
- **Measurements:** $H_q$, time to threshold crossing, pain-analogue/off-target loss only if operationalized, recurrence, and probe harm.
- **Pass condition:** reserve decline predicts failure earlier and adverse changes are detected without excessive probe cost.
- **Falsifier:** output-only or standard health metrics predict equally well, or the probes themselves dominate failure.

## Integration disposition

- **No new established cross-domain principle is recommended.** Selection, containment, maintenance, use dependence, reserve, replay, and reconstruction already have owners.
- **Hold PATH-R1 as an evaluation method.** It becomes valuable only if it predicts transfer failure, recurrence, or hidden resource reliance beyond ordinary robustness and cost accounting.
- **Add no fixed biological constants.** Clinical percentages, sample sizes, treatment effects, and time windows remain study-specific with their designs and uncertainty.
- **Do not use pathology as architecture authority.** A failure mechanism supplies an adversarial test before it supplies a component.
- **Prioritize experiments E, G, and H.** They directly test the unresolved restoration/compensation distinction, timing interaction, and hidden reserve without requiring a disease metaphor in implementation.
