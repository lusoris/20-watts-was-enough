## Executive finding

Tribology contributes a useful discipline for reasoning about costly
interfaces: the relevant state belongs to the interacting system, not to an
isolated component label. That discipline does not license a friction-themed
AI architecture. Nine bounded residues survive source review:

1. a friction coefficient is conditioned by the pair, interface, environment,
   motion, load, history, and measurement operator rather than being one
   portable material constant;
2. nominal area and mean load do not determine real contact, local pressure,
   heat, conductance, or damage tails;
3. lubrication is a regime-qualified balance among film formation, roughness,
   starvation, viscous loss, boundary interaction, temperature, and supply
   cost;
4. wear is path- and mechanism-dependent material change, and the Archard
   relation is a conditional model rather than a universal lifetime law;
5. mean friction does not identify stick--slip stability when rate, state,
   compliance, inertia, damping, and controller delay interact;
6. a third body is a dynamic interfacial inventory whose circulation can
   protect, abrade, transmit load, or escape; debris is not intrinsically waste
   or lubricant;
7. surface texture is an operating-envelope intervention, not an unconditional
   friction reducer;
8. adaptive or self-lubricating interfaces spend material inventory, activation
   opportunity, transition time, and substrate/coating life; and
9. lower contact friction or coupon wear does not establish lower energy,
   higher accepted service, or lower lifecycle burden at system scale.

The proposed AI translations are typed interface state, conditional routing,
regime gates, path-qualified memory, mediator ledgers, reversible local
adaptation, and lifecycle-normalized selection. Those mechanisms already have
owners in P-001, P-002, P-005--P-010, and P-012--P-013. The audit therefore
adds falsifiable boundaries and null comparisons, not a new principle.

## Evidence and normative firewalls

### Scientific-source roles

1. **Contact and wear theory:** Archard and Greenwood--Williamson support
   scoped asperity/contact and wear relations. Their simplifying assumptions
   remain visible and are not a claim that every interface follows the same
   law.
2. **Lubrication theory:** Hamrock--Dowson and Patir--Cheng support conditional
   film and rough-surface flow models. A fitted correlation or averaged
   Reynolds equation is not a universal regime classifier.
3. **Rate/state dynamics:** Dieterich and Ruina establish history- and
   state-dependent friction and stability in scoped rock/spring-slider
   systems. Their parameters are not imported as engineering constants.
4. **Third-body and tribofilm evidence:** Godet supplies a mechanical
   third-body framework; Gosvami and colleagues provide in-situ evidence for
   antiwear-film growth in a specified single-asperity experiment. Neither
   makes every debris layer beneficial.
5. **Surface and coating interventions:** Kovalchenko and colleagues test one
   laser-texture envelope; Voevodin and colleagues test adaptive composite
   coatings across specified environments. These studies do not establish
   universal texture or coating performance.
6. **System estimates:** Holmberg and colleagues estimate friction-related
   energy at vehicle/global scales from stated models and source data. Those
   estimates motivate complete boundaries but are not direct constants or
   measurements for this project.

## Construct and unit firewall

1. **Friction force** [N] is tangential resistance at a declared interface and
   direction. A dimensionless coefficient is a ratio under a declared normal
   force and measurement operator, not a material identity.
2. **Friction work** [J] is the path integral of tangential force through
   displacement. It is not automatically heat at one component, input
   electricity, fuel, or useful task energy.
3. **Nominal contact area** [m$^2$] is the apparent geometric support. **Real
   contact area** is the sum of load-bearing microscopic patches under a model
   or observation operator.
4. **Mean pressure** [Pa] cannot identify peak/local pressure, flash
   temperature, subsurface stress, contact conductance, or damage probability.
5. **Film thickness** [m], composite roughness [m], and film ratio
   [dimensionless] are distinct. A film ratio does not alone determine
   traction, starvation, scuffing, chemistry, or wear.
6. **Wear volume** [m$^3$], wear depth [m], lost mass [kg], transferred mass
   [kg], debris inventory [kg], and functional failure are different outputs.
7. **Wear coefficient** is conditional on a chosen wear law and test support;
   it is not a universal probability of failure or remaining-life fraction.
8. **Stick--slip** is a dynamical classification requiring a motion, force, or
   state trace and threshold. A high mean coefficient or noisy velocity alone
   does not establish it.
9. **Third body** names material mediating two first bodies in a contact. Its
   origin, mass, location, circulation, chemistry, load support, and exit route
   must be represented separately.
10. **Texture** requires geometry, direction, density, scale, manufacture, and
    measurement support. A scalar roughness value does not identify a dimple,
    groove, plateau, or multiscale pattern.
11. **Self-lubricating/adaptive** describes a bounded material or interface
    response. It does not imply no inputs, no wear, reversibility, unlimited
    inventory, safe products, or autonomous intelligence.
12. **Accepted service** is task output satisfying frozen quality, deadline,
    and protected constraints. Gross operations, sliding distance, or low
    friction are not accepted service.

## Shared mathematical skeleton

### Friction, work, and conditional coefficients

For normal force $W_N$ [N], signed tangential contact force $F_t$ [N], signed
sliding velocity $v$ [m/s], and displacement $x$ [m], resistance opposes
motion. Dissipated friction power and work are therefore

$$
\mu_{obs}=\frac{|F_t|}{W_N},\qquad
P_{f,diss}=-F_tv=|F_tv|\quad[\mathrm W],\qquad
E_{f,diss}=-\int F_t\,dx=\int |F_t|\,|dx|\quad[\mathrm J].
$$

The convenient Coulomb form $|F_t|=\mu W_N$ is a scoped constitutive relation.
In this audit $\mu$ may depend on the two bodies, topography, lubricant,
temperature, humidity, speed, dwell, load, debris, direction, and history.

### Rough elastic contact

For asperity $j$ with summit height $z_j$ [m], separation $d$ [m], effective
radius $R_j$ [m], and effective elastic modulus $E^*$ [Pa], the synthetic
Greenwood--Williamson/Hertz family uses

$$
\delta_j=(z_j-d)_+,
\qquad
f_j=\frac{4}{3}E^*\sqrt{R_j}\,\delta_j^{3/2},
\qquad
A_j=\pi R_j\delta_j.
$$

$d$ is selected so $\sum_j f_j=W_N$. Apparent area, $W_N/A_{nom}$, and the
distribution of $f_j/A_j$ answer different questions. The equations define a
benchmark family, not a complete rough-contact theory.

### Lubrication support and regime variables

For an incompressible isoviscous one-dimensional film with gap $h(x,t)$ [m],
pressure $p$ [Pa], viscosity $\eta$ [Pa s], and entrainment speed $U$ [m/s],

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
\left(\frac{h^3}{12\eta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right)
=\frac{U}{2}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}.
$$

With minimum film $h_{min}$ and RMS roughnesses $R_{q,1},R_{q,2}$,

$$
\lambda=\frac{h_{min}}
{\sqrt{R_{q,1}^2+R_{q,2}^2}}.
$$

Neither the Reynolds equation nor $\lambda$ alone closes thermal,
elastohydrodynamic, starvation, cavitation, chemistry, asperity-contact, or
pumping effects.

### Conditional sliding-wear model

The Archard form for wear volume $V_w$ [m$^3$], sliding distance $L$ [m],
hardness $H$ [Pa], and dimensionless coefficient $k_w$ is

$$
V_w=k_w\frac{W_NL}{H}.
$$

It is dimensionally closed but mechanism-conditional. If $k_w$, $H$, contact
state, counterface, debris, temperature, or regime changes along the path, one
constant fitted at the endpoint is not automatically predictive.

### Rate-and-state friction

A synthetic Dieterich--Ruina family uses

$$
\mu(v,\theta)=\mu_0+a\ln\frac{|v|+v_\epsilon}{v_0}
+b\ln\frac{\theta v_0}{D_c},
\qquad
\dot\theta=1-\frac{(|v|+v_\epsilon)\theta}{D_c},
$$

where $\theta$ [s] is state, $D_c$ [m] is characteristic slip distance, and
$a,b$ are dimensionless. In a spring--slider,

$$
m\ddot x=k_s(V_dt-x)-c\dot x
-W_N\mu(\dot x,\theta)\tanh(\dot x/v_\epsilon).
$$

Mean $\mu$ does not determine stability because the state law, stiffness,
inertia, damping, drive, and delay remain.

### Third-body balance

For third-body mass $M_3$ [kg], source-body generation rates $G_{1\to3}$ and
$G_{2\to3}$ [kg/s], external supply $G_{in}$ [kg/s], reincorporation rates
$G_{3\to1},G_{3\to2}$ [kg/s], and escape $G_{out}$ [kg/s],

$$
\frac{dM_3}{dt}=G_{1\to3}+G_{2\to3}+G_{in}
-G_{3\to1}-G_{3\to2}-G_{out}.
$$

A force, wear, or friction effect must be linked to this inventory and its
state; it cannot replace the balance.

### Mission and lifecycle boundary

For accepted service $S_{acc}>0$ [task units], operational input energy
$E_{op}$ [J], maintenance energy $E_{maint}$ [J], allocated manufacture
$E_{mfg}$ [J], and end-of-life/recovery term $E_{eol}$ [J],

$$
e_{life}=\frac{E_{op}+E_{maint}+E_{mfg}+E_{eol}}{S_{acc}}
\quad[\mathrm{J/task\ unit}].
$$

Every allocation, sign, uncertainty, reference period, and service criterion
must be declared. Friction-only work is one term, not the denominator or the
complete numerator.

## Claim-ledger proposals

### C-1497

- **Statement:** a measured coefficient of friction is a tribosystem- and
  operator-conditioned response rather than one portable material constant;
  interface pair, topography, normal load, motion, environment, lubricant,
  temperature, history, and measurement support qualify it.
- **Status:** established boundary; value of an analogous typed AI-interface
  state remains untested.
- **Scientific observation:** rough-contact and rate/state studies explain why
  equal material labels or equal mean load can produce different friction
  traces when real contact and state differ.
- **Primary sources:** `Archard1953Contact`, `GreenwoodWilliamson1966Contact`,
  `Dieterich1979RockFriction`, `Ruina1983SlipInstability`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** bind an interaction-cost estimate to both
  parties, mode, support, workload, environment, version, evidence age, and
  recent interface state instead of caching one scalar cost per module class.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** use a cheap scalar inside a verified stationary
  envelope and activate state estimation only after support or residual gates
  fail.
- **Failure modes:** coefficient treated intrinsic; static and kinetic values
  pooled; sign/direction lost; normal force inferred from actuator command;
  temperature or dwell omitted; same samples used to define and confirm the
  envelope; sensing cost ignored.
- **Measurable prediction:** in `TAI-T01`, a typed state model must reduce
  held-out friction-work and route-cost error under state changes while tying a
  scalar model in a scalar-sufficient family.
- **Open question:** what smallest reconstructible state is sufficient across
  task-relevant interface changes without becoming a full-history model?
- **Disposition:** map to P-006, P-007, P-012, P-013 and Candidate 014; no new
  principle.

### C-1498

- **Statement:** apparent contact area and aggregate normal load do not
  identify real contact area or the tail of local contact pressure. Any claim
  about flash temperature, conductance, plasticity, or damage additionally
  requires its own constitutive model and observation support.
- **Status:** established contact-mechanics boundary inside stated rough-
  contact assumptions; transfer value remains speculative.
- **Scientific observation:** multi-asperity contact models connect load to a
  distribution of microscopic contacts rather than uniform nominal pressure.
- **Primary sources:** `Archard1953Contact`,
  `GreenwoodWilliamson1966Contact`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** represent where an interaction actually
  concentrates compute, traffic, heat, writes, contention, or error rather
  than accepting global utilization as local exposure.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** inspect or reroute only interfaces whose supported
  local-tail estimate can change a protected decision.
- **Failure modes:** nominal mean substituted for local maximum; surface
  operator or cutoff missing; independence assumed for clustered asperities;
  topography measured after damage and treated prior; solver non-convergence
  dropped; oracle local loads exposed to one arm.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T02` requires rough-support inference to
  improve peak-pressure coverage and overload decisions over nominal averages,
  with no advantage in a uniform-support control.
- **Open question:** can a compact tail summary preserve protected local
  queries across changed load and topography support?
- **Disposition:** map to P-001, P-008, P-012, C-1305, and Candidate 014; no
  new principle.

### C-1499

- **Statement:** fully flooded film formation and rough-surface flow are
  qualified by geometry, composite roughness, viscosity, speed, load, and the
  assumptions of the selected model. Starvation, temperature, viscous
  traction, boundary contact, supply, pumping, and damage are additional terms
  that must be represented rather than inferred from that correlation alone.
- **Status:** established for the cited full-film and averaged rough-flow
  components; the integrated synthetic controller and artificial-system
  composition are plausible and untested.
- **Scientific observation:** elastohydrodynamic film correlations and rough-
  surface average-flow models depend on explicit dimensionless groups,
  geometry, and boundary assumptions rather than one lubricant label.
- **Primary sources:** `HamrockDowson1977PointContact`,
  `PatirCheng1978AverageFlow`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** treat a mediator such as cache, buffer,
  precision, redundancy, or communication reserve as a regime-dependent
  interface resource with supply, drag, leakage, starvation, and failure cost.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** a low-cost regime gate selects among verified
  policies and escalates to full optimisation near uncertain boundaries.
- **Failure modes:** lower friction scored despite boundary damage; viscosity
  at wrong temperature; fully flooded formula used under starvation;
  roughness direction dropped; supply/pump work free; film ratio called wear;
  regime labels fitted on confirmation.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T03` requires regime-aware optimisation to
  reduce mission loss versus one fixed lubricant/mediator rule and requires a
  cheap gate to remain non-inferior to the mechanistic optimiser.
- **Open question:** is a regime map still useful after a robust finite-horizon
  optimiser receives the same state and action set?
- **Disposition:** map to P-002, P-006, P-008, P-009, P-012 and Candidates
  001/006/012; no lubrication principle.

### C-1500

- **Statement:** wear is a mechanism- and path-dependent change of material
  and function; an Archard-type proportional law is conditional and a nominal
  cycle count or endpoint wear volume is not a universal remaining-life state.
- **Status:** established boundary; compact AI damage-state benefit remains
  speculative.
- **Scientific observation:** the Archard relation describes scoped simple
  wear behaviour, while wear-mechanism maps expose transitions with load,
  speed, temperature, and material conditions.
- **Primary sources:** `Archard1953Contact`, `LimAshby1987WearMaps`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** retain mechanism-qualified exposure, transition,
  repair, remap, and acceptance state for repeatedly used routes or memories
  rather than one operation counter.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** a tested sufficient damage state can avoid replay
  of the full event history while preventing premature replacement or late
  failure.
- **Failure modes:** fitted $k_w$ transported across regimes; lost volume
  equated with functional failure; transferred material double-counted as
  loss; load/order interaction omitted; survivor paths only; reset or repair
  silently resets history; future threshold used in state compression.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T04` requires path-aware inference to improve
  wear and remaining-service prediction on endpoint-matched histories while
  tying the Archard sum in a constant-regime control.
- **Open question:** which damage queries require full history, and which admit
  an auditable finite-dimensional state?
- **Disposition:** map to P-009, P-012, C-500, C-1045--C-1046, and Candidates
  005/009/014/018; no new principle.

### C-1501

- **Statement:** stick--slip is a state- and dynamics-dependent instability;
  mean friction does not identify it without rate dependence, interface state,
  compliance, inertia, damping, drive, observation bandwidth, and control
  delay.
- **Status:** established in the cited rate/state and spring-slider scope;
  general AI scheduling benefit is untested.
- **Scientific observation:** rate/state laws and spring-slider analyses
  predict stable or unstable sliding from coupled constitutive and system
  parameters rather than a coefficient alone.
- **Primary sources:** `Dieterich1979RockFriction`,
  `Ruina1983SlipInstability`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** diagnose burst/idle oscillation, retry storms,
  queue chatter, or actuator cycling with a joint local-state and system-delay
  model instead of attributing it to mean load.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** event-trigger high-rate estimation or damping only
  near a validated instability boundary; remain cheap in stable motion.
- **Failure modes:** numerical oscillation called physics; velocity sign and
  zero regularisation mishandled; future trace leaks into classifier; sample
  rate below event bandwidth; controller changes plant while baseline is open
  loop; false alarms uncharged; unstable run truncated and deleted.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T05` requires a rate/state null to improve
  onset and trace prediction over a mean-friction model, and an event-triggered
  composition to reduce solver evaluations without missed bursts.
- **Open question:** does any residual remain beyond delay-aware state-space
  identification and robust/event-triggered control?
- **Disposition:** map to P-002, P-006, P-007, P-012 and Candidates 003/012/014;
  no new principle.

### C-1502

- **Statement:** third-body material is a dynamic interfacial inventory with
  generation, transport, accommodation, reincorporation, and escape; its net
  effect can be protective, abrasive, load-bearing, lubricating, or obstructive
  depending on state and boundary.
- **Status:** established mechanical framework with scoped primary tribofilm
  evidence; universal sign and transfer benefit are unestablished.
- **Scientific observation:** the third-body approach enforces continuity and

  antiwear tribofilm can grow through contact-conditioned processes.
- **Primary/authoritative sources:** `Godet1984ThirdBody`,
  `GosvamiEtAl2015Tribofilm`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** treat caches, residuals, summaries, traces, and
  shared buffers as mediated interface inventories with origin, circulation,
  transformation, eviction, and externality rather than intrinsically useful
  or wasteful objects.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** retain or remove mediator state according to
  measured service and damage effect while closing its quantity and cost
  ledger.
- **Failure modes:** debris always flushed or always retained; mass creation;
  protective coverage inferred from inventory alone; chemistry hidden; escaped
  mass ignored; mediator supplied free; one arm receives hidden role labels;
  mass-balance residual forced to zero.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T06` requires a balance-qualified controller
  to beat both always-remove and always-retain rules across sign-changing
  mediator worlds without violating mass closure.
- **Open question:** can useful mediator state be identified from affordable
  observables before its sign changes?
- **Disposition:** map to P-008, P-009, P-012, P-013, C-1381 and Candidates
  006/014; no new principle.

### C-1503

- **Statement:** engineered surface texture can alter hydrodynamic pressure,
  lubricant storage, debris trapping, contact stress, leakage, and drag, but
  benefit is conditional on geometry, scale, direction, operating regime,
  counterface, manufacture, and metric.
- **Status:** established for scoped experiments and models; universal texture
  benefit is disputed/unsupported.
- **Scientific observation:** laser-textured contacts shifted lubrication
  transitions in a bounded pin-on-disk study; reviews document multiple
  mechanisms and application-specific envelopes rather than one optimum.
- **Primary/authoritative sources:** `KovalchenkoEtAl2005Texturing`,
  `Etsion2005Texturing`.
- **Proposed AI translation:** compile recurring interaction patterns into
  interface structure only when the future workload and failure envelope repay
  build, inflexibility, and reversal cost.
- **Efficiency mechanism:** choose smooth, textured, or reconfigurable support
  from a held-out mission distribution rather than optimizing one coupon
  point.
- **Failure modes:** texture always compared with a weak smooth baseline;
  geometry tuned on confirmation; roughness operator changed; manufacture and
  edge stress free; one speed/load point called a regime map; failed textures
  excluded; benefits transported across direction or scale.
- **Measurable prediction:** `TAI-T07` requires robust texture selection to
  choose nonzero texture only in beneficial envelopes and smooth support in
  hostile/tie envelopes, after build and lifecycle cost.
- **Open question:** can a compact workload-support certificate predict when
  structural compilation survives mission shift?
- **Disposition:** map to P-005, P-010, C-1052 and Candidates 001/006/009; no

## Source-to-claim and source-to-test ledger

### Contact, friction, and wear

1. `Archard1953Contact` supports the scoped multi-contact/wear-model origin for
   C-1497, C-1498, and C-1500. It motivates `TAI-T01`, `TAI-T02`, and
   `TAI-T04`; it does not validate their synthetic coefficients or AI arms.
2. `GreenwoodWilliamson1966Contact` supports rough multi-asperity contact for
   C-1497/C-1498 and the explicit control family in `TAI-T02`. The benchmark's
   height/radius distributions remain synthetic.
3. `LimAshby1987WearMaps` supports mechanism/regime qualification for C-1500
   and the hostile transitions in `TAI-T04`. Its maps are not copied or used as
   universal boundaries.

### Lubrication and interface mediation

1. `HamrockDowson1977PointContact` supports the fully flooded point-contact
   correlation used as one bounded generator component in C-1499/`TAI-T03`.
   Starvation, thermal state, and damage terms are explicit synthetic
   hypotheses.
2. `PatirCheng1978AverageFlow` supports the need to preserve roughness and
   directional flow factors in partial lubrication. It qualifies C-1499 and
   the roughness failure modes in `TAI-T03`; the protocol does not claim to
   implement the full Patir--Cheng model.

### Dynamics

1. `Dieterich1979RockFriction` supports time-, velocity-, displacement-, and
   stiffness-qualified friction for C-1497/C-1501 and the rate/state family in
   `TAI-T01`/`TAI-T05`.
2. `Ruina1983SlipInstability` supports state-variable spring-slider stability
   for C-1501/`TAI-T05`. Rock-friction parameters are not transported to other
   materials or AI systems.

### Third bodies and surfaces

1. `Godet1984ThirdBody` supports continuity and accommodation as the core
   mechanical third-body boundary in C-1502/`TAI-T06`.
2. `GosvamiEtAl2015Tribofilm` supplies direct in-situ evidence that one
   antiwear tribofilm can grow under specified single-asperity sliding. It
   bounds, but does not fix the sign of, the mediator in C-1502/`TAI-T06`.
3. `KovalchenkoEtAl2005Texturing` supplies a primary operating-envelope example
   for C-1503/`TAI-T07`; its reported speeds, loads, oils, and effects are not
   protocol constants.

   texturing mechanisms and application boundaries, not an independent
   replication or universal benefit claim.
5. `VoevodinZabinski2000Chameleon` and
   `VoevodinEtAl2002Nanocomposite` support bounded environment-conditioned
   surface adaptation for C-1504/`TAI-T08`. The synthetic phases and inventory
   laws are not material-performance predictions.

4. `TAI-T04` covers C-1500: constant Archard, thermal, transition, and

exercise constrained mission policies; `T09` comes last because it composes
transfer, acquisition, service, and lifecycle ledgers. A smoke implementation
may reduce worlds/steps but cannot change equations, arms, missing-run loss,
gates, or private-seed semantics, and is never confirmation evidence.

## Failure and interpretation firewall

1. A successful synthetic protocol establishes performance only for its frozen
   generator, observations, actions, nulls, resources, and held-out support.
2. A failed protocol can reject an AI composition; it cannot refute a physical
   source result outside the benchmark.
3. An equation copied correctly can still be outside its physical support. The
   protocol manifest must name correlation/model/version and validity checks.
4. Synthetic mechanical energy is not measured workstation energy. Both are
   logged separately; neither is converted without a measured conversion
   boundary.
5. A lower coefficient can reduce required traction, braking, grip, damping,
   or stability. Accepted service and protected outcomes have priority over
   friction minimisation.
6. Surface texture or an adaptive coating is not an AI model and does not gain
   novelty from biological or "smart material" language.
7. A material inventory, third body, trace, cache, or buffer can have
   sign-changing value. The sign must come from an intervention and task
   outcome, not from its persistence.
8. Resource savings caused by early failure, fewer accepted tasks, narrower
   support, hidden preprocessing, omitted probes, or uncharged manufacture do
   not count.
9. No result, benchmark status, or workstation readiness exists until code,
   manifests, sealed seeds, independent evaluator, hostile artifact tests,
   and rerunnable outputs are present in the repository.

## Open blockers before central integration

1. The `TAI-T03`, `TAI-T06`, `TAI-T07`, and `TAI-T08` response laws are
   deliberately synthetic falsifiers. Their implementations require
   dimensional/unit/property tests and sensitivity plots before any private
   pack is revealed; they must never be described as validated tribological
   simulators.
2. The 60 s/seed resource caps are design targets, not measured runtimes. A
   smoke benchmark must confirm feasibility without changing confirmation
   semantics.
3. `TAI-T05` requires an independently implemented ODE evaluator and a tighter-
   tolerance convergence artifact before any scientific use.
4. `TAI-T06` requires integer-unit property tests for every inflow, outflow,
   mixing, clipping, and failure path; a reported zero residual computed after
   overwriting state is invalid.
5. `TAI-T09` requires implementation-level property tests showing that the
   frozen incumbent, publication-selection mechanism, accepted-service rule,
   allocation policy, and query support exactly match this audit before the
   first private seed is revealed.
6. Exact current DIN adoption of ISO 4378-2/-3:2024 and any applicable licensed
   test text must be verified before a real German test programme. Public
   catalogue metadata is not the standard's full method.
7. Any real lubricant/coating/material work needs a fresh REACH/CLP, workplace,
   waste, machinery, product, safety, and competent-authority assessment; the
   audit does none.

## Audit verdict

The field is now deep enough to route as dedicated ANZSRC tribology coverage,
dedicated DFG machine-element/product-design coverage, and the exact EuroSciVoc
tribology and lubrication routes recorded above, with mechanics, surface
engineering, and numerical characterisation adjacent. The durable addition is
nine typed boundaries and nine frozen preimplementation falsification
contracts. None has been implemented or executed. No principle, candidate,
empirical AI advantage, hardware recommendation, workstation result, or legal
conformity claim is promoted.
