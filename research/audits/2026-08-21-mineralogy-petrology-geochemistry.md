## Executive finding

Mineralogy, petrology, and geochemistry add a stringent history-and-observation
test to the repository, but not a new nature-derived optimizer. The same
present assemblage, texture, bulk composition, isotope ratio, or fluid analysis
can be consistent with multiple pressure–temperature paths, reaction
sequences, transport histories, open-system exchanges, preservation filters,
and instrument models. Conversely, phases excluded by an equilibrium
calculation can persist because nucleation, diffusion, reactive surface area,
fluid access, or replacement-front propagation is too slow.

The durable residue is a **path-, support-, and preservation-qualified material
record**. A defensible record keeps together:

1. system components, bulk and local composition, pressure, temperature,
   activity/fugacity convention, phases admitted, thermodynamic dataset, and
   solution models;
2. kinetic network, nucleation and growth law, reactive area, diffusion length,
   transport field, boundary exchange, fluid access, and quench or overprint;
3. specimen geometry, texture, zoning, inclusion relations, sampling support,
   preparation, rejected grains, and preservation/selection operator;
4. instrument, operator, calibration, blank, interference, drift, reference
   material, uncertainty covariance, and interlaboratory state;
5. forward model, inverse family, prior or regularizer, alternative histories,
   identifiable functionals, and unsupported directions; and
6. every downstream claim, validity interval, supersession relation, and
   invalidation edge.

Each ingredient is established practice in thermodynamics, kinetics, reactive
transport, petrochronology, inverse theory, and metrology. The project may claim
a systems contribution only if the connected record prevents false historical
or mechanism claims, or improves a declared quality–resource frontier, beyond
the complete mature stack at equal data, compute, storage, and human effort.

## Terminology firewall

1. A **phase** is a physically and chemically homogeneous part under a declared
   thermodynamic description. It is not a software module or arbitrary cluster.
2. An **equilibrium assemblage** minimizes an appropriate thermodynamic
   potential subject to declared components, constraints, and models. It does
   not specify the path or time required to reach it.
3. A **pseudosection** is a phase-diagram section for a specified bulk
   composition and model system. It is not a universal map for every rock with
   a similar name.
4. A **metastable phase** is not the global equilibrium state under the declared
   conditions but can persist because transition pathways have finite barriers
   or rates. It is not synonymous with measurement error.
5. **Nucleation**, **growth**, **dissolution**, **diffusion**, and **ripening**
   are distinct kinetic processes. One fitted rate cannot silently represent
   all five.
6. A **polymorph** shares chemical composition with another crystalline form
   but differs in structure. It is not an interchangeable label when physical
   properties and stability differ.
7. A **reaction path** is a state sequence conditional on initial state,
   boundaries, kinetics, and transport. End-member mass balance does not make
   the path unique.
8. A **pseudomorph** preserves external form after replacement. Morphological
   continuity is not proof of solid-state material continuity.
9. A mineral **assemblage**, **texture**, **zoning profile**, **inclusion**, and
   **isotopic domain** are different observations with different supports and
   reset mechanisms.
10. A **P–T–t path** is an inferred pressure–temperature history through time.
    A peak pressure, peak temperature, mineral date, and closure temperature
    need not refer to one simultaneous event.
11. A **closure temperature** is model- and history-dependent; it is not an
    immutable thermometer value attached to a mineral name.
12. An isotope **ratio**, **delta value**, **fractionation factor**, **model
    age**, **isochron age**, and **event age** are not interchangeable.
13. A **tracer** constrains transport or sources only through a declared forward
    model, endmember set, fractionation law, and observation operator.
14. **Mass closure** is a conservation check over a boundary and interval. It
    is not proof that the represented sources, sinks, or causal mechanism are
    complete.
15. **Eh**, **pe**, oxidation state, redox-couple potential, electrode
    indication, and equilibrium speciation are distinct quantities.
16. **Reactive transport** couples conservation, flow, mixing, reaction, and
    evolving material state. It is not reaction kinetics evaluated at a mean
    concentration.
17. An **effective** or **upscaled** property is defined for a target response,
    support, boundary condition, and regime. It is not a context-free material
    constant.
18. Sediment **provenance** is an inference about source and routing history.
    It is not identical to the observed mineral-age histogram.
19. **Concordance** is agreement among declared chronometer relations under a
    model. It is not proof that the inferred date names the desired geological
    event.
20. **Absence from the record** can mean non-occurrence, non-preservation,
    non-exposure, non-sampling, rejection, non-detection, or zero support.

## Evidence synthesis

In each subsection, the cited field result and its scoped equation or
measurement are the **source facts**. Text introduced as **translation** is a
repository design consequence, and text introduced as **speculation** or
**speculative residue** remains unvalidated until its registered track beats
the stated mature null. A source fact never establishes its translation by
itself.

### Phase equilibria constrain states, not histories

For a closed system at fixed pressure $P$ [Pa], temperature $T$ [K], and total
component amounts $b_j$ [mol], a standard equilibrium calculation minimizes
total Gibbs energy

$$
G(\mathbf n,P,T)=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_i
n_i^{\alpha}\mu_i^{\alpha}(P,T,\mathbf x^{\alpha})
$$

subject to

$$
\sum_{\alpha}\sum_i A_{ji}n_i^{\alpha}=b_j,
\qquad n_i^{\alpha}\ge0.
$$

$n_i^{\alpha}$ is amount [mol] of endmember $i$ in phase $\alpha$;
$\mu_i^{\alpha}$ is chemical potential [J/mol]; $\mathbf x^{\alpha}$ is a
dimensionless phase-composition vector; and $A_{ji}$ is component-$j$
stoichiometry [mol component/mol endmember]. The solution is conditional on the
admitted phase list, component basis, activity-composition models, equation of
state, bulk composition, and database.

Holland and Powell constructed internally consistent petrological datasets with
uncertainties and correlations
([1998](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1525-1314.1998.00140.x);
[2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1525-1314.2010.00923.x)). Powell, Holland, and
Worley describe nonlinear phase-diagram calculations involving solid solutions
([1998](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1525-1314.1998.00157.x)); Connolly describes a
linear-programming discretization for multicomponent phase equilibria
([2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2005.04.033)). These sources establish
model-based equilibrium calculations, not natural equilibration.

**AI translation:** constraints, admissible states, and versioned model data
should travel with a state estimate. **Speculative residue:** Candidate 014 may
gain by invalidating every dependent result when composition, phase list,
thermodynamic datum, or solution model changes. **Mature null:** constrained
optimization, phase-equilibrium solvers, ensemble thermodynamic datasets,
sensitivity analysis, and ordinary dependency graphs.

### Nucleation and kinetic trapping make end state non-identifying

In an idealized spherical classical-nucleation model, the reversible work for a
nucleus of radius $r$ [m] is

$$
\Delta G(r)=4\pi r^2\gamma+
\frac{4\pi r^3}{3}\Delta g_v,
$$

where interfacial energy $\gamma$ is in J/m² and bulk driving force
$\Delta g_v<0$ is in J/m³. The critical barrier is

$$
\Delta G^*=\frac{16\pi\gamma^3}{3\Delta g_v^2},
\qquad
J=J_0\exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta G^*}{k_BT}\right),
$$

where $J$ is nucleation rate [m⁻³ s⁻¹], $J_0$ has the same unit, and $k_B$ is
Boltzmann's constant [J/K]. The expression is a comparator, not a universal
mineral law: shape, heterogeneous substrates, nonclassical precursors,
elasticity, solution chemistry, and finite clusters alter it.

Sun et al. combined solution chemistry, bulk solubility, and surface energies
to explain preferential nucleation of metastable aragonite over calcite in the
studied seawater system
([2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1423898112)). Steefel and Van Cappellen
modeled nucleation, reactive-surface generation, dissolution, precipitation,
and ripening rather than imposing partial equilibrium
([1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0016-7037(90)90003-4)). Putnis and Putnis showed
that interface-coupled dissolution–reprecipitation can preserve morphology and
crystallographic information while replacing material through a porous product
([2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jssc.2007.03.023)).

**Source fact:** barriers and interfaces can change phase order and replacement
mechanism. **Translation:** systems need explicit transition graphs, barrier or
rate support, and a record of rejected/latent paths rather than assuming direct
movement to a global optimum. **Speculation:** preserving a small equivalence
set of kinetically possible histories may improve diagnosis. **Mature null:**
chemical master-equation models, kinetic Monte Carlo, hidden Markov/state-space
models, reaction-path solvers, and ordinary event sourcing.

### Assemblages, textures, zoning, and dates form a constrained record

Spear et al. used garnet zoning and coexisting mineral chemistry to calculate
P–T paths in scoped metamorphic systems
([1984](https://doi.org/10.1130/0091-7613(1984)12%3C87:PPFGZA%3E2.0.CO;2)).
Petrochronology explicitly links dates to textural and petrological context
rather than treating a number as an event name
([Vance et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1144/GSL.SP.2003.220.01.01);
[Engi et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.2138/rmg.2017.83.1)).

A simple compositional profile obeying Fickian diffusion is

$$
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}
=\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(D(T,P,c)\nabla c\right),
\qquad
D=D_0\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a+P\Delta V}{RT}\right),
$$

where $c$ is concentration [mol/m³], $D$ is diffusivity [m²/s], $D_0$ is a
pre-exponential factor [m²/s], $E_a$ is activation energy [J/mol], activation
volume $\Delta V$ is [m³/mol], and $R$ is [J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹]. Because $D$ changes
strongly with the path, observed smoothing constrains a time–temperature
integral rather than time or temperature alone.

Dodson's closure-temperature result explicitly depends on cooling rate, grain
dimension, geometry, diffusion parameters, and decay timescale
([1973](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00373790)). Later dissolution–reprecipitation,
fluid ingress, deformation, or partial melting can reset one domain without
resetting another. The record is therefore a collection of overlapping kernels,
not a lossless event log.

**Translation:** Candidate 014 should type each domain by spatial support,
formation/reset model, and retained alternatives. Candidate 017 may compress
only registered historical queries that remain reconstructible. **Mature null:**
petrographic context, diffusion chronometry, phase-equilibrium modeling,
Bayesian P–T–t inversion, petrochronology, and full specimen/data provenance.

### Isotope tracers and reservoir balances are conditional inverses

Urey derived the thermodynamic basis for equilibrium isotope fractionation
([1947](https://doi.org/10.1039/JR9470000562)). For isotope ratios $R_A$ and
$R_B$ in phases $A$ and $B$, the dimensionless fractionation factor is

$$
\alpha_{A-B}=\frac{R_A}{R_B},
\qquad
\delta=10^3\left(\frac{R}{R_{\mathrm{std}}}-1\right).
$$

Here $\delta$ is reported in per mille (‰). The equations define
representations, not causes. Temperature-dependent
equilibrium, kinetic fractionation, diffusion, mixing, exchange, and analytical
normalization can produce different $\delta$ values.

For mixing streams with denominator-isotope amounts $n_k$ [mol] and ratios
$R_k$, the exact two-isotope mixture is

$$
R_{\mathrm{mix}}=\frac{\sum_k n_kR_k}{\sum_k n_k}.
$$

Weights are denominator-isotope amounts, not arbitrary bulk fractions. If
concentrations differ, a straight line in an isotope-ratio plot need not mean a
simple mass mixture. DePaolo and Wasserburg showed how Sm–Nd parent/daughter
fractionation and radiogenic growth support long-lived reservoir inference
([1976](https://doi.org/10.1029/GL003i005p00249)); their result does not make
reservoir number or history unique.

For reservoir inventory $M_i$ [mol] of component $i$,

$$
\frac{dM_i}{dt}
=\sum_a F_{a,\mathrm{in}}C_{i,a}
-\sum_b F_{b,\mathrm{out}}C_{i,b}
+\sum_r\nu_{ir}r_rV,
$$

where volumetric flux $F$ is [m³/s], concentration $C$ is [mol/m³], reaction
rate $r_r$ is [mol m⁻³ s⁻¹], volume $V$ is [m³], and stoichiometric coefficient
$\nu_{ir}$ is dimensionless. Closure can reveal that the declared boundary is
incomplete; it does not identify which omitted source, sink, storage term, or
measurement bias caused the residual.

**Translation:** keep inventory, gross flux, transformation, tracer response,
and observation covariance separate. This refines Candidates 007, 013, and 014.
**Mature null:** compartment models, isotope-enabled mixing, Bayesian source
apportionment, state-space mass balance, and sensitivity/identifiability
analysis. No “natural provenance token” survives.

### Weathering, redox, and speciation resist scalar summaries

Maher's reactive-transport analysis shows that chemical-weathering rates in
the studied systems can depend strongly on fluid residence time and approach to
equilibrium rather than surface kinetics alone
([2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2010.03.010)). A useful dimensionless
diagnostic is

$$
\mathrm{Da}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{transport}}}
{\tau_{\mathrm{reaction}}},
$$

but its interpretation depends on how both characteristic times are defined.
It does not collapse mineralogy, reactive area, saturation state, flow-path
distribution, temperature, and biological mediation into one constant.

Lindberg and Runnells evaluated 611 groundwater analyses and found multiple
redox couples far from mutual equilibrium; their calculated Nernstian potentials
and field electrode indications could disagree strongly
([1984](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.225.4665.925)). For a specific reversible
half-reaction under equilibrium support,

$$
E=E^{\circ}-\frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q,
$$

where $E$ and $E^{\circ}$ are [V], $n$ is electron stoichiometry, Faraday
constant $F$ is [C/mol], and reaction quotient $Q$ is dimensionless. A system
containing kinetically isolated couples need not possess one operational master
$E$.

**Translation:** chemical state must remain a vector of species, couples,
methods, and validity intervals; a scalar “resource” or “environment quality”
signal is insufficient. This sharpens Candidate 013's typed-resource gate and
Candidate 014's operator-qualified observation. **Mature null:** equilibrium
speciation, kinetic reaction networks, multi-electrode/analytical observation,
and coupled state estimation.

### Reactive transport and upscaling are target-specific

A single aqueous component in a saturated porous medium can be represented by

$$
\frac{\partial(\phi c_i)}{\partial t}
+\nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\mathbf q c_i
-\phi\mathbf D\nabla c_i\right)
=\sum_r\nu_{ir}r_r+s_i,
$$

where porosity $\phi$ is dimensionless, $c_i$ is [mol/m³ fluid], Darcy flux
$\mathbf q$ is [m/s], dispersion/diffusion tensor $\mathbf D$ is [m²/s], and
source $s_i$ is [mol m⁻³ bulk s⁻¹]. Mineral volume fractions, permeability,
porosity, and reactive surface can evolve with $r_r$; charge balance, component
conservation, speciation, and phase relations add coupled constraints.

Steefel, DePaolo, and Lichtner describe reactive-transport modeling as a means
to connect coupled processes across scales
([2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2005.09.017)). A later code comparison
documents common formulations and benchmark needs
([Steefel et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10596-014-9443-x)). Dagan makes
observation/computation scale and heterogeneity correlation length explicit in
stochastic flow theory
([1986](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR022i09Sp0120S)). Renard and de Marsily show
that equivalent permeability depends on the equivalence criterion and
distinguish effective from block properties
([1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0309-1708(96)00050-4)).

A coarse field that preserves total discharge can still fail to preserve local
residence times, reaction-front geometry, breakthrough tails, mineral
conversion, or effluent speciation. Therefore every reduction must name the
observables and boundary family it preserves.

**Translation:** this is a direct stress test for
[Fixture F-005](../../experiments/fixtures/005-regime-qualified-flow-inference-control.md),
P-008, P-010, and Candidate 014. **Speculation:** a versioned target-specific
upscaling contract may reduce silent model reuse. **Mature null:** homogenization,
multicontinuum/multirate transport, pore-network and Darcy-scale models,
multifidelity surrogates, closure tests, and held-out boundary/regime validation.

### Provenance is a selection-and-routing inverse

Detrital records are created by sequential filters: source-rock mineral
fertility, liberation, weathering, transport, hydraulic sorting, recycling,
deposition, diagenesis, exposure, sampling, mineral separation, grain picking,
instrument acceptance, discordance filtering, and statistical display.
Malusà et al. quantified several such biases in zircon U–Pb and fission-track
workflows
([2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemgeo.2013.09.016)). Vermeesch gives a
sample-size calculation for the probability of missing a population fraction
under random representative sampling
([2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2004.05.037)); that calculation does not
repair non-random zero-support sources.

For source fraction $w_k$, fertility $f_k$, survival/routing probability $s_k$,
and analytical acceptance $a_k$, an idealized observed fraction is

$$
p_k^{\mathrm{obs}}
=\frac{w_kf_ks_ka_k}{\sum_jw_jf_js_ja_j}.
$$

All factors are dimensionless and may vary with grain size, mineral, age,
chemistry, route, and laboratory. Without external constraints, $w_k$ is not
identifiable from $p_k^{\mathrm{obs}}$ alone. If $f_ks_ka_k=0$, no sample size
recovers source $k$.

**Translation:** provenance graphs need selection support and alternative
routing histories, not only lineage. This refines Candidate 014 and Candidate
017. **Mature null:** compositional mixture models, bias-aware detrital
provenance, multi-mineral tracers, source-to-sink mass balance, and explicit
sampling designs.

### Geochronology estimates model-qualified dates

For a closed U–Pb domain with radiogenic daughter lead marked by an asterisk,

$$
\frac{^{206}\mathrm{Pb}^{*}}{^{238}\mathrm U}
=e^{\lambda_{238}t}-1,
\qquad
\frac{^{207}\mathrm{Pb}^{*}}{^{235}\mathrm U}
=e^{\lambda_{235}t}-1.
$$

$t$ is [s or yr consistently] and each decay constant $\lambda$ has reciprocal
time units. Wetherill's concordia construction makes the two decay relations
joint and shows how open-system fractionation can produce discordance
([1956](https://doi.org/10.1029/TR037i003p00320)). Regression with uncertainty
in both axes and correlated errors requires an errors-in-variables method; York
et al. give unified line-fit equations
([2004](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1632486)).

Common lead, uranium or lead loss, inheritance, mixed growth domains,
disequilibrium, decay-constant uncertainty, blank and mass bias, reference-
material anchoring, and data rejection can change the inferred age. Even a
well-estimated mineral-domain date needs petrological and textural evidence to
name crystallization, metamorphism, fluid ingress, cooling, or disturbance.

**Translation:** the date, domain, event interpretation, calibration root, and
alternative open-system histories are separate typed records. **Mature null:**
concordia/discordia, isochron and hierarchical chronometer models, robust
mixtures, petrochronology, and metrological dependency graphs.

### Deep-time and rare-event records are preservation-filtered

Sadler's compilation shows that apparent sediment accumulation rates decrease
with the time span over which they are averaged because net stratigraphic
records are discontinuous
([1981](https://doi.org/10.1086/628623)). Signor and Lipps showed how incomplete
sampling can make abrupt extinction appear gradual
([1982](https://doi.org/10.1130/SPE190-p291)). The transfer is broader than
paleontology: absence, duration, onset, recurrence, and simultaneity claims are
conditional on a preservation-and-detection operator and an age model.

Let latent event process $N(t)$ be observed through preservation probability
$p(t,m,z)$, where $m$ is event magnitude and $z$ is environment. Under
independent thinning only as an idealized null,

$$
\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}(t,m,z)
=p(t,m,z)\lambda_{\mathrm{latent}}(t,m,z).
$$

Both intensities have units events per time per declared magnitude/support
measure. If $p=0$ in an interval, latent rate is not identifiable there. If
preservation depends on event magnitude or environment, ordinary missing-at-
random correction is invalid.

**Translation:** zero-support intervals and age uncertainty must survive every
summary. This refines Candidate 014 and Candidate 018. **Mature null:** censored
point processes, age–depth models, capture/occupancy methods, preservation
models, sensitivity bounds, and explicit `unknown` results.

### Reference materials and interlaboratory results bound measurement claims

The current German/European adoption
**DIN EN ISO/IEC 17025:2018-03** addresses competence, impartiality, and
consistent operation of testing and calibration laboratories
([DIN record](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/standard/din-en-iso-iec-17025/278030106)).
**DIN EN ISO 17034:2017-04** addresses competence of reference-material
producers
([DIN record](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/standard/din-en-iso-17034/256379952)).
ISO 33403:2024 covers use of reference materials; ISO 33405:2024 covers
characterization, homogeneity, stability, certified-value uncertainty, and
traceability
([ISO 33403](https://www.iso.org/standard/84224.html),
[ISO 33405](https://www.iso.org/standard/84226.html)). ISO 13528:2022 plus
Amendment 1:2026 covers statistical methods for proficiency testing by
interlaboratory comparison
([ISO 13528](https://www.iso.org/standard/78879.html),
[Amendment](https://www.iso.org/standard/90057.html)).

These documents are mature comparators, not scientific evidence that a
geochemical model is correct. A reference value can share method roots with
participant results; a powdered reference material may not reproduce in-situ
matrix, grain-scale heterogeneity, ablation behavior, digestion recovery, or
speciation; and interlaboratory consensus can preserve common bias.

**Translation:** every derived claim carries calibration root, reference-
material purpose/support, covariance, matrix and commutability limits, operator
and preparation state, comparison scheme, and dependency invalidation. This is
already Candidate 014 composed with Candidate 009.

## Proposed path-qualified material record

For specimen, cell, or synthetic episode $d$, retain

$$
R_d=(I,B,S,K,T,O,C,F,A,P,H,X),
$$

where:

- $I$ is immutable specimen/episode identity, version, and custody lineage;
- $B$ is boundary, bulk/local composition, components, reservoirs, and flux
  support;
- $S$ is admitted phase/species state, thermodynamic data, solution models,
  uncertainty, and equilibrium support;
- $K$ is reaction graph, nucleation/growth/dissolution/diffusion law, reactive
  area, and kinetic-parameter support;
- $T$ is pressure, temperature, time, transport, deformation, and fluid-access
  path or represented equivalence set;
- $O$ is observed assemblage, texture, zone, inclusion, isotope domain, spatial
  and temporal support, censoring, and preservation operator;
- $C$ is instrument, operator, preparation, calibration, blank, interference,
  drift, reference material, uncertainty covariance, and interlaboratory state;
- $F$ is forward observation/transport model, discretization, boundary family,
  solver, and numerical checks;
- $A$ is inverse family, prior/regularizer, alternatives, identifiable
  functionals, null space, and abstention region;
- $P$ is provenance, sampling, rejected items, software, data, and model roots;
- $H$ is hypothesis/claim target, intended use, action, and loss boundary; and
- $X$ is downstream dependency, validity interval, supersession, expiry,
  invalidation, and reconstructability state.

Unknown fields remain `unknown`; they are not imputed to make the record look
complete. This schema is a Candidate-014 domain fixture, not a new candidate.

### WS-MPG-01 — equilibrium state versus kinetically reachable path

**Generator.** Per seed create 4,096 systems, each with 5 conserved components,
8 candidate phases, 12 endmember compositions, and a known smooth Gibbs-energy
surface over 24 pressure–temperature points. Draw one of 16 directed reaction
graphs with phase-specific nucleation barriers, growth/dissolution rates, and
three reactive-area histories. Execute 64-step heating, cooling, compression,
and decompression schedules. In 25% of episodes the equilibrium phase has a
large barrier; in 25% a metastable phase later transforms; in 25% the same final
assemblage is reached by at least three histories; and in 25% all arms receive
an equilibrium-friendly control.

**Comparators.** Global Gibbs minimization; local greedy descent; equilibrium
plus fixed lag; continuous-time Markov/reaction network with known graph;
particle state-space inference with uncertain rates; Candidate-014 record with
an explicit path-equivalence set. The known-parameter simulator is an ineligible
ceiling. Equalize 128 fit/tuning calls per arm.

**Splits and hostile cases.** Hold out four reaction-graph topologies, one
polymorph family, high-barrier/low-driving-force combinations, reversed
schedules, quench timing, and one phase omitted from the development phase list.

**Metrics.** Final phase-abundance $L_1$ error [mol], trajectory log score,
metastable-phase recall, transformation-time interval coverage, false unique-
path rate, true-history set coverage, unsupported-phase abstention, CPU, bytes,
and review time.

**Success.** At least 92.5% simultaneous coverage of registered phase-amount
and transformation-time functionals; zero ordinary path claim when multiple
histories remain observationally equivalent; noninferior final-state error and
at least 25% lower false unique-path rate than the best kinetic null; and a
strict compute, state, or review Pareto gain after record overhead.

**Kill.** Reject the residue if a conventional particle/Bayesian reaction-path
model plus dependency graph matches it; if confirmation truth selects the phase
list or rate prior; if equilibrium is credited with path recovery; or if an
omitted phase receives posterior mass without external evidence.

## Confirmatory analysis and decision contract

The independent unit is a complete latent-history-to-claim episode, not a
mineral, grid cell, isotope ratio, time point, or laboratory replicate. Pair
arms on identical latent systems and cluster units sharing a thermodynamic
dataset, kinetic family, calibration root, source population, laboratory, or
preservation operator.

Hold out entire:

- reaction graphs, polymorph families, and phase-list omissions;
- P–T–t, diffusion, geometry, inheritance, and reset families;
- reservoir topologies, tracer contrasts, and missing boundaries;
- redox couples, mediators, matrices, and handling faults;
- porous-field orientations, correlations, boundary schedules, and target
  observables;
- source, fertility, recycling, sorting, and acceptance regimes;
- chronometer open-system, matrix, reference, and laboratory roots; and
- preservation, hiatus, sampling, and age–depth families.

Use a frozen decision order:

1. no excess conservation violation, unsupported source/history, false absence,

   valid null;
2. registered interval coverage and error control within tolerance;
3. noninferior target utility and rare-event/source sensitivity;
4. lower false unique-history, false mechanism, transfer, or invalidation
   failure; then
5. lower CPU, wall time, bytes, human work, or measured energy.

## Cross-track failure modes

Reject any apparent gain that depends on:

1. treating equilibrium as a realized path or elapsed-time estimate;
2. treating the observed metastable phase as an optimizer choice;
3. inferring material continuity from preserved morphology;
4. tuning kinetics or phase lists on confirmation truth;
5. assigning one complete P–T–t path where equivalent histories remain;
6. naming a geological event from a decontextualized date;
7. treating closure temperature as a mineral constant;
8. using isotope-ratio fractions as bulk source-mass fractions;
9. treating mass closure as unique causal attribution;
10. forcing mutually disequilibrated redox couples into one master Eh;
11. calibrating an upscaled model on total flow and claiming reaction-front or
    effluent accuracy;
12. reconstructing a source or interval with zero observation support;
13. discarding rejected grains, failed measurements, blanks, or unsuitable
    reference materials from denominators;
14. multiplying laboratories or tracers that share one calibration/model root
    as independent evidence;
15. treating interlaboratory consensus as truth;
16. treating a concordant date as proof of a unique event;
17. treating first/last observed occurrence as the true event boundary;
18. assuming missing-at-random preservation when magnitude or environment
    controls survival;
19. retaining one best history while deleting alternatives needed for future
    queries; or
20. excluding generator, calibration, fine-grid simulation, invalidation,
    review, storage, or rerun cost from lifecycle accounting.

## Proposed central claims

The root integrator may assign the next available central IDs. These twelve
claims are the maximum justified promotion set; most local propositions should
remain audit-local.

1. **Phase-equilibrium output is model- and composition-qualified.** A computed
   petrological assemblage depends on bulk composition, components, pressure,
   temperature, admitted phases, thermodynamic data, and solution models; it
   does not establish natural equilibration or a reaction path. Sources:
   `HollandPowell1998ThermodynamicData`,
   `HollandPowell2011ThermodynamicData`, `Connolly2005PhaseEquilibria`.
2. **Metastable polymorph selection can be nucleation-controlled.** Interfacial
   and bulk driving terms can make a metastable phase nucleate faster than the
   equilibrium bulk phase under scoped solution conditions. Source:
   `SunEtAl2015AragoniteNucleation`.
3. **Preserved morphology does not prove material continuity.** Fluid-mediated
   interface-coupled dissolution–reprecipitation can replace a solid while
   preserving external form and some crystallographic relation. Source:
   `PutnisPutnis2007Reequilibration`.
4. **Mineral records constrain only supported portions of P–T–t history.**
   Assemblage, zoning, texture, diffusion, and dated domains can constrain
   histories, while overprint, reset, fractionation, and non-identifiability
   prevent a lossless path claim. Sources: `SpearEtAl1984PTPaths`,
   `Dodson1973ClosureTemperature`, `VanceEtAl2003Petrochronology`.
5. **Isotope tracers are fractionation-, mixing-, and reservoir-qualified.**
   Stable and radiogenic ratios constrain sources and histories only under
   declared fractionation, concentration, endmember, exchange, and open-system
   models. Sources: `Urey1947IsotopicSubstances`,
   `DePaoloWasserburg1976NdIsotopes`.
6. **Mass balance is necessary but not uniquely causal.** Inventory closure can
   expose an incomplete declared boundary, but it cannot by itself identify
   which omitted flux, storage, reaction, or measurement term caused the
   residual. Evidence test: WS-MPG-03; this is a conservation consequence, not
   a new external empirical claim.
7. **Weathering flux can be transport- and residence-time-limited.** Field-scale
   chemical weathering need not inherit a laboratory surface-rate law when flow
   and approach to equilibrium control the realized flux. Source:
   `Maher2010WeatheringResidence`.
8. **One master Eh can be invalid for disequilibrated waters.** Multiple redox
   couples in natural groundwater can be mutually inconsistent and disagree
   with a single electrode indication. Source:
   `LindbergRunnells1984GroundwaterRedox`.
9. **Reactive upscaling is target- and boundary-qualified.** A coarse property
   that preserves one flow response need not preserve residence-time tails,
   local reaction, breakthrough, or effluent speciation. Sources:
   `Dagan1986GroundwaterScales`, `RenardDeMarsily1997Permeability`,
   `SteefelEtAl2015ReactiveTransportCodes`.
10. **Detrital provenance is selection-qualified.** Mineral fertility,
    survival, sorting, recycling, preparation, acceptance, and sample size
    separate an observed age fraction from source mass fraction. Sources:
    `MalusaEtAl2013DetritalBias`, `Vermeesch2004GrainCount`.
11. **Geochronological dates are inverse- and event-qualified.** Concordia,
    discordia, isochron, common-component, open-system, and correlated-error
    models estimate mineral-domain dates; petrological context is separately
    required to interpret events. Sources: `Wetherill1956DiscordantUPb`,
    `YorkEtAl2004LineFit`, `EngiEtAl2017Petrochronology`.
12. **Deep-time absence and event shape are preservation-qualified.** Hiatus,
    time averaging, exposure, sampling, detection, and age-model uncertainty can
    smear or remove rare events; zero preservation support forbids an absence
    claim. Sources: `Sadler1981StratigraphicCompleteness`,
    `SignorLipps1982SamplingBias`.

The proposed path-qualified material record remains a **speculative composition
hypothesis**, tested by WS-MPG-01 through WS-MPG-08. No cited source establishes
that this repository's composition beats the complete null stack.
