# Comparative cognition, tool use, and flexible action: primary-source audit

- **Audit date:** 2026-08-05
- **Scope:** tool use and manufacture, causal intervention, future preparation,
  episodic-like memory, caching and re-caching, uncertainty monitoring,
  imitation and emulation, teaching, innovation and recombination, play and
  exploration, social-learning strategies, inhibitory control, flexible
  problem solving, and cephalopod peripheral control
- **Taxonomic coverage:** corvids, parrots, cephalopods, cetaceans, elephants,
  great apes and monkeys, rodents, meerkats, ants, and bumblebees where the
  task and controls are informative
- **Evidence rule:** primary experiments support task-bounded behavioral
  observations. Behavioral success does not by itself identify subjective
  experience, a unique cognitive representation, a species-wide capacity, or
  a transferable algorithm.
- **Promotion state:** audit-local `CCOG-` claims only. This file changes no
  stable claim, principle, candidate, or central bibliography entry.

## Executive finding

Comparative cognition does not supply a ladder of species intelligence. It
supplies a set of controlled demonstrations in which different bodies,
perceptual systems, ecological histories, and training histories expose
different parts of adaptive action. The recurrent mechanisms are narrower
than labels such as “reasoning,” “foresight,” “metacognition,” or “creativity”:

- action success depends on an animal having the sensory and motor opportunity
  to discover and execute a solution;
- manufacture and multi-step tool use can preserve a functional relation while
  changing material, geometry, or action sequence, but learned affordances and
  search remain strong explanations;
- transfer after interventions is more diagnostic of causal structure than
  terminal success, yet trap-tube and Aesop-style results show that arbitrary
  cues and trial-by-trial reinforcement can mimic broad causal claims;
- future tool selection and saving can survive delays and contextual
  separation, but prospective behavior does not identify conscious mental time
  travel and must be separated from learned delayed choice;
- what–where–when performance and cache protection require bound event state,
  decay rules, observer-conditioned action, and later use, while leaving
  subjective recollection unmeasured;
- uncertainty responses and information seeking can improve decisions, but
  reward gradients, response strength, public difficulty cues, and learned
  escape policies are mandatory nulls;
- social learning may transmit actions, results, locations, affordances, or
  attention. “Copying” is not one update rule;
- teaching-like behavior can be defined operationally by a knowledgeable
  actor changing behavior at a cost, contingent on a learner state, so complex
  mind-reading is not required;
- innovation normally recombines available actions, materials, and learned
  relations under selection. A novel terminal sequence does not show that a
  solution appeared without history;
- exploration can provide later-useful property information without being
  strategically targeted at information gain;
- detour and cylinder tasks measure a task-specific interaction among learned
  approach, transparency, persistence, motor affordance, and inhibition, not a
  unitary cross-species “self-control” scalar; and
- octopus studies demonstrate a particularly clear division between local
  sensorimotor programs, body mechanics, peripheral feedback, and centrally
  selected goals. “Distributed” does not mean independent arms or absent
  central control.

The strongest residual is not a new comparative-cognition primitive. It is a
**task-, opportunity-, and history-qualified adaptive action contract**: retain
what was observable, manipulable, practiced, rewarded, and socially available;
measure the intervention and the transfer; then compare the proposed internal
state with ordinary learning, search, retrieval, and control at equal cost.
That contract refines [P-003](../principle-registry.md#p-003--temporary-trace-before-commitment),
[P-007](../principle-registry.md#p-007--prediction-error-allocation),
[P-008](../principle-registry.md#p-008--compartmentalized-interaction),
[P-012](../principle-registry.md#p-012--memory-matched-to-information-lifetime),
and Candidates [004](../../experiments/candidates/004-closed-endogenous-curriculum.md),
[007](../../experiments/candidates/007-endogenous-observation-surveillance.md),
[014](../../experiments/candidates/014-versioned-observation-contract.md), and
[019](../../experiments/candidates/019-audited-cumulative-inheritance.md). It
does not justify a new principle or candidate.

## Outcome firewall

The following outcomes must remain separate.

| Outcome | Operational question | Minimum measurement | Invalid substitution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tool use | does an external object mediate a measurable action–outcome relation? | success, latency in seconds, attempts, material and geometry | calling any object contact a tool |
| manufacture | does the subject alter an object into a functional form? | transformation sequence, dimensions, discarded material, function test | a pre-shaped object merely selected |
| sequential use | is one action or tool required to obtain another before the terminal outcome? | step accuracy, order, recovery after step deletion or reorder | one practiced chain without transfer |
| causal sensitivity | does action change appropriately under an intervention on the relevant relation? | intervention-by-choice effect and novel transfer | terminal success or preference alone |
| future preparation | is an item selected or retained for a later state not currently actionable? | delay in seconds/hours, distractors, context change, baseline choice | persistence near the apparatus |
| episodic-like memory | is event-specific what–where–when information used flexibly? | item, location, elapsed time, unique episode, content update | familiarity or semantic rule alone |
| uncertainty control | is information bought, a test declined, or checking invoked selectively? | difficulty/calibration curve, cost, accuracy conditional on choice | slowing or low confidence label alone |
| social learning | does exposure to another actor causally change acquisition or expression? | demonstrator assignment, exposure, diffusion or transfer, asocial control | group similarity alone |
| imitation | is the demonstrator's action form reproduced beyond result or location learning? | action coding, novel action, ghost/result control | matching the outcome |
| teaching | does a knowledgeable actor modify behavior contingent on learner state, at a cost, improving learning? | teacher cost, contingency, learner rate/retention | provisioning or co-presence alone |
| innovation | is a behavior or sequence new relative to a declared observation history? | action inventory, exposure history, latency, independent recurrence | researcher surprise |
| exploration/play | is unrewarded manipulation measured and does it alter later choice? | contact duration, action diversity, property coverage, delayed transfer | persistence or neophilia alone |
| inhibitory control | is a prepotent response withheld under a specific task demand? | first-choice and trial course, training, transparency and motor controls | one cross-species rank |
| distributed control | which state, sensing, and update are local, central, or mechanical? | perturbation site, latency, kinematics, denervation or occlusion control | neuron count or anatomical location alone |

## Observation contract

Every comparative result used for system design must declare the tuple

$$
\mathcal O=(X,S,A,H,R,C,\tau,U),
$$

where:

- $X$ is task and apparatus state;
- $S$ is the sensory information physically available;
- $A$ is the feasible motor action set;
- $H$ is training, rearing, prior task, and social-exposure history;
- $R$ is reward, punishment, deprivation, and stopping rule;
- $C$ is the control and intervention set;
- $\tau$ is the relevant timescale in seconds, hours, days, or years; and
- $U$ is the sampling unit: trial, episode, subject, dyad, group, site, or
  population.

A capability comparison is not interpretable if any of $S$, $A$, $H$, or $R$
differs without measurement. For method or condition $m$, define opportunity-
qualified performance

$$
Q_m=\Pr(Y=1\mid do(m),S,A,H,R,C),
$$

and the transfer contrast

$$
\Delta_{m,m_0}=Q_m-Q_{m_0}.
$$

$Y$ is a preregistered success event, $Q$ and $\Delta$ are dimensionless, and
the intervention must be explicit. A difference in raw success that changes
opportunity or history is not a cognitive-mechanism estimate.

## Conventional null stack

Before attributing a result to a new architecture, compare against:

- model-free reinforcement learning with state features available to the
  subject;
- model-based reinforcement learning, POMDP planning, and receding-horizon
  control;
- nearest-neighbor and episodic retrieval over action–outcome trajectories;
- learned affordance classifiers plus beam search or Monte Carlo tree search;
- behavior cloning, goal-conditioned imitation, inverse reinforcement
  learning, and result-only/ghost controls;
- novelty bonuses, count-based exploration, curiosity/information-gain
  objectives, and random action with matched attempts;
- abstention, selective prediction, value-of-information acquisition, and
  calibrated confidence;
- hierarchical policies, options, finite-state controllers, and task graphs;
- local feedback controllers, compliant-body dynamics, and centralized
  controllers with matched communication; and
- explicit logs, caches, versioned memory, curricula, and population-level
  replay.

Species names and cognitive labels supply no baseline credit.

## Quantitative contract

### Action, success, and intervention

For episode $e$ with states $x_t$, observations $o_t$, actions $a_t$, and
outcome $y_e$, record the trajectory

$$
\zeta_e=(x_0,o_0,a_0,\ldots,x_T,o_T,a_T,y_e).
$$

Report action latency and duration in seconds, distances in metres, object
dimensions in metres, force in newtons when measured, mass in kilograms,
number of contacts and attempts as counts, and reward in its literal unit. The
causal transfer score for intervention family $G$ is

$$
T_G=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}
\left[s_g-s^{\mathrm{matched}}_g\right],
$$

where each $s$ is a success proportion. $T_G$ is dimensionless. The matched
condition must preserve perceptual salience and action cost while changing the
claimed functional relation.

### Event memory and cache value

Represent cache or encounter $i$ as

$$
e_i=(w_i,\ell_i,t_i,q_i,c_i),
$$

with item/content $w_i$, location $\ell_i$, time $t_i$ in seconds from a
declared origin, quality or perishability state $q_i$, and social context
$c_i$. At retrieval time $t$, a minimal expected value is

$$
V_i(t)=p_i(t\mid e_i)r_i(t)-k_i(t),
$$

where $p_i$ is a dimensionless availability probability, $r_i$ is reward
value, and $k_i$ is search/recovery cost in the same reward unit. A learned
semantic decay schedule plus location memory is a required null for an
episodic-like claim.

### Information seeking and abstention

For belief $b$ and optional observation $z$ costing $c_z$, the ordinary
value-of-information null is

$$
\operatorname{VOI}(z\mid b)=
\mathbb E_z\!\left[\max_a\mathbb E[U(a,\theta)\mid b,z]\right]
-\max_a\mathbb E[U(a,\theta)\mid b]-c_z.
$$

Acquire $z$ when this is positive. Utility and $c_z$ use the same declared
reward unit. An uncertainty response is not evidence for introspection if a
learned stimulus-difficulty or response-strength policy predicts the same
choices. Report selective risk

$$
R(\kappa)=\Pr(\hat y\ne y\mid \text{accepted at coverage }\kappa)
$$

across the complete risk–coverage curve, not accuracy on accepted trials alone.

### Social transmission

Let $Z_i(t)=1$ after individual $i$ acquires behavior $Z$. A minimal diffusion
hazard is

$$
\lambda_i(t)=\lambda_0(t)\exp(\beta_E E_i(t)+\beta_S S_i(t)+\beta_H H_i),
$$

where $E_i$ is individual/asocial exposure, $S_i$ is network-weighted exposure
to performers, and $H_i$ is measured history. Hazard has units $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$
or $\mathrm{day}^{-1}$ according to the declared clock; covariates and
coefficients must be scaled explicitly. A positive $\beta_S$ supports social
transmission under the model, not action imitation, teaching, or a mental-state
representation.

### Local and central control

For a limb or module $j$,

$$
u_j(t)=\pi_j\bigl(s_j(t),g(t),m_j(t)\bigr),
\qquad
\dot s_j=f_j(s_j,u_j,d_j),
$$

where $s_j$ is local sensorimotor state, $g$ is a global goal or command,
$m_j$ is local memory, $d_j$ is disturbance, and $u_j$ is actuation. Compare
this with a centralized controller receiving the same observations and a
hierarchical controller with the same bandwidth and latency. A local program
earns architectural credit only for a better accuracy–latency–energy–recovery
frontier after local sensing, communication, duplicated state, and failure
handling are charged.

### Equal-budget boundary

For system $m$, report

$$
\mathbf K_m=(N_{\mathrm{episodes}},N_{\mathrm{actions}},N_{\mathrm{queries}},
T_{\mathrm{train}},T_{\mathrm{test}},M_{\mathrm{peak}},B_{\mathrm{moved}},
E_{\mathrm{life}},H_{\mathrm{human}}),
$$

where episode, action, and query entries are counts; times are seconds; memory
and data movement are bytes; lifecycle energy is joules; and human effort is
person-hours. Enforce preregistered componentwise caps
$\mathbf K_m\preceq\mathbf K_{m_0}$ or estimate a Pareto frontier. Animal
metabolic energy, keeper/trainer effort, apparatus energy, and compute
electricity remain separate rows unless one explicit lifecycle boundary
justifies their combination.

## Mechanism cards

## 1. Affordance-qualified tool manufacture

Weir, Chappell, and Kacelnik reported wire bending into a hook by a captive New
Caledonian crow in a retrieval task
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1073433)). Goffin's cockatoos made a
similar elongated functional tool from different materials
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2016.0689)); one initial report concerned a
single innovative subject
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.09.002)). Asian elephants modified
branches for fly switching, while performance depended on whether the offered
action preserved trunk sensing
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/anbe.2001.1815);
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023251)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | alter wire, plant, wood, cardboard, twig, branch, or movable platform to reach food or repel flies |
| observed state | object dimensions, manipulation sequence, functional success, discarded attempts |
| sensory/motor opportunity | bill–foot and trunk affordances, binocular view, haptics, material fracture and bend properties |
| training history | wild tool ecology, captivity, prior probing, object handling, standing-on-object history, and shaping exposure must be separate |
| controls | pre-shaped objects, wrong length/rigidity, novel materials, no-reward manipulation, unmodified object, motor-feasible alternative |
| inferential limit | functional shaping does not identify a mental template, insight, or mechanism shared across taxa |
| timescale/units | manufacture and use latency in seconds; contacts/count; length in metres; mass in kilograms; success proportion |
| possible system translation | learn material- and actuator-qualified affordances, then synthesize the cheapest object transform meeting a functional constraint |
| strongest ordinary null | affordance classifier plus model-based search over known object operations and reinforcement from failed contacts |
| exact dedup | P-004 diversity/selection; P-007 error allocation; P-010 structural offloading; Candidate 004 curriculum. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match object observations, action primitives, trials, search expansions, parameters, and joules; reject if search plus affordance learning matches novel-material transfer |

**Failure and negative transfer.** A body-incompatible apparatus can turn a
motor or sensory mismatch into an apparent cognitive failure. One successful
individual cannot establish a species-wide rate. Manufacture should lose
credit when the test material fractures along a line that supplies the target
shape without controlled shaping.

## 2. Sequential and meta-tool action

New Caledonian crows used a short tool to retrieve a longer tool needed for a
terminal reward
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2007.07.057)). Great apes showed graded
performance as sequential-tool demands and costs increased
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0052074)). Kea produced ordered
object relations in a second-order tool task
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2010.08.007)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | select or obtain tool $a_1$ to make tool/state $a_2$ accessible before the terminal action $a_3$ |
| observed state | selected tool, step order, reversals, omissions, latency, terminal success |
| sensory/motor opportunity | all intermediate relations visible or separately hidden; reach and manipulation costs measured |
| training history | prior single-tool competence and exposure to every component action documented |
| controls | useless long tool, directly reachable reward, reversed order, deleted step, visually distinct but causally matched transfer |
| inferential limit | an action chain can be cached, shaped, or searched; success does not establish hierarchical conscious planning |
| timescale/units | seconds/step, action count, errors/episode, success proportion, delayed transfer in hours |
| possible system translation | options or task-graph policies whose subgoals are admitted only when they preserve terminal reachability |
| strongest ordinary null | learned finite-state controller, hierarchical RL/options, retrieval of successful chains, and beam search |
| exact dedup | P-001 selective allocation; P-003 trace-before-commitment; P-008 compartments; Candidate 004. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | equalize demonstrations, episodes, planning expansions, memory, retries, and energy; reject if finite-state/task-graph control matches intervention transfer |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Increased step count simultaneously changes
working-memory load, motor opportunity, reward delay, and extinction exposure.
Only interventions that selectively reorder or disable a relation distinguish
structured subgoals from a practiced chain.

## 3. Causal intervention versus surface cue learning

In two-trap tasks, three of six New Caledonian crows solved the initial problem
and transferred to a visually different trap-table, but performance across
controls was mixed
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2008.1107)). Rooks solved an initial
trap-tube but not novel transfer controls
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-006-0061-4)). Capuchins' behavior in the
classic trap tube was consistent with learned surface relations rather than a
broad cause–effect representation
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7036.108.1.15)). Reanalysis and new
experiments on Aesop-style displacement tasks found trial-and-error learning
could explain results presented as causal understanding
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2016.10.029)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | choose an action that avoids a functional trap or changes liquid displacement under controlled alternatives |
| observed state | first trial, trial course, side choice, arbitrary feature, transfer after functional inversion |
| sensory/motor opportunity | visibility of trap, hole, connection, water, reward, object density and reachable openings |
| training history | reinforced trials on apparatus variants and prior tool pushing/dropping reported exactly |
| controls | inverted/nonfunctional trap, arbitrary-cue removal, visually novel causal analogue, impossible connector, trial-one analysis |
| inferential limit | transfer excludes some cue rules but rarely identifies a unique causal model; repeated success can be model-free learning |
| timescale/units | trial index/count, choice probability, learning slope/trial, object mass and displaced volume when measured |
| possible system translation | intervention-indexed world models that retain which relation changed and predict counterfactual action outcomes |
| strongest ordinary null | feature-based RL, model-based RL learned from interventions, object-centric search, and memorized side/geometry rules |
| exact dedup | P-003 traces; P-007 prediction error; P-012 memory lifetimes; Candidate 007 intervention-aware observation and Candidate 014 observation contracts. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match trials, state features, interventions, search, and reward; require transfer to held-out causal graphs whose surface cues are counterbalanced |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Pooling late trials with first exposure can
make acquisition look like prior understanding. Passing one inverted control
does not exclude all associative features, while failing a transfer may reflect
novel motor geometry rather than absence of causal sensitivity.

## 4. Delayed future preparation

Bonobos and orangutans selected, transported, and retained a functional tool
for later use after delays reported up to 14 hours
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1125456)). Ravens selected tools or
barter tokens for later tasks with delays up to 17 hours and showed task-specific
self-control
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam8138)). Western scrub-jays cached in
a compartment where they had previously lacked breakfast, a result framed as
planning for a future motivational state
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05575)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | choose or store an item now that is useful only in a later context |
| observed state | selected item, distractors, retention, transport, later use, current motivation |
| sensory/motor opportunity | later apparatus may be absent at choice; carrying/storage opportunity and item discriminability declared |
| training history | tool/barter training and prior delay schedule separated from transfer tests |
| controls | currently useful item, never useful distractor, apparatus absent, context change, satiation or changed future need |
| inferential limit | delayed choice supports prospective control but does not measure autonoetic consciousness or prove episodic simulation |
| timescale/units | delay in seconds/hours; retention proportion; selection/use accuracy; carrying cost |
| possible system translation | prospective eligibility state linking a latent future task to an expiring resource reservation |
| strongest ordinary null | learned delayed discrimination, successor representation, model-predictive planning, and cached context–item policy |
| exact dedup | P-003 temporary trace; P-012 lifetime-matched memory; P-013 external state; Candidate 018 tiering and Candidate 004 curriculum. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | equalize episodes, future-state cues, model rollouts, memory and energy; reject if a learned context–item value table transfers equally |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Delay alone is not future planning. The
decisive tests change current and future motivational states, detach choice
from apparatus cues, and introduce item–task recombinations not reinforced
during acquisition.

## 5. What–where–when event binding

Scrub-jays recovered preferred perishable and nonperishable foods according to
what had been cached, where, and whether 4 or 124 hours had elapsed
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/26216)). Rats learned replenishment contingencies
in a radial-maze task while retaining item, place, and elapsed-time information
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2006.05.025)). “Episodic-like” correctly
marks the inferential boundary: the tasks do not assay subjective recollection.

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | use trial-unique item, location, and elapsed-time information to choose a recovery action |
| observed state | food identity, location, encoding time, retention interval, perishability/replenishment, recovery choice |
| sensory/motor opportunity | odor and visible-food guidance removed; locations accessible and counterbalanced |
| training history | semantic perishability or replenishment rule learned before event-specific test and modeled separately |
| controls | time-of-day, interval, odor, familiarity, item-only/place-only rules, unique episodes, post-encoding content update |
| inferential limit | integrated event-guided choice does not establish conscious re-experiencing or a human-identical memory system |
| timescale/units | hours or days; location error in metres; choice proportion; reward count |
| possible system translation | versioned event records whose content, place, time, decay law, and later outcome remain queryable together |
| strongest ordinary null | semantic decay/replenishment rule plus spatial memory, recurrent state, and episodic nearest-neighbor retrieval |
| exact dedup | P-003 temporary trace; P-012 memory lifetime; P-013 external state; Candidates 017 and 018. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match storage bytes, events, queries and compute; require advantage on trial-unique recombination and post-encoding updates, not familiar intervals |

**Failure and negative transfer.** A stable interval rule can solve a nominal
what–where–when task. The system claim survives only if it predicts event-
specific exceptions and content updates beyond a factorized semantic-plus-
spatial baseline.

## 6. Observer-qualified caching and re-caching

Western scrub-jays with prior pilfering experience re-cached food after being
observed, whereas birds without that experience did not show the same pattern
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/35106560)). Jays also altered cache placement
and used shaded sites when watched
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2004.0190)). These are prospective,
experience- and observation-qualified actions; they do not uniquely identify
attribution of another individual's visual knowledge.

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | cache under private or observed conditions, then recover or re-cache in private |
| observed state | observer presence/view, cache location, light, distance, prior pilfering, later move/check/eat action |
| sensory/motor opportunity | barriers and illumination define information available to observer and cacher |
| training history | personal theft and pilferage experience, social housing, and cache-recovery history measured |
| controls | observer occluded, private cache, different observer, nonpilfering history, equal site access |
| inferential limit | state-conditioned cache protection need not represent another mind; learned theft risk and attention cues remain nulls |
| timescale/units | cache/recovery interval in minutes/hours; locations/count; re-cache proportion; distance/metres; illuminance if measured |
| possible system translation | provenance- and audience-qualified secrets/caches whose relocation policy depends on exposure history and threat model |
| strongest ordinary null | contextual risk model, Bayesian theft probability, access-control policy, and randomized cache relocation |
| exact dedup | P-007 predictive allocation; P-012 memory lifetime; P-013 shared external state; Candidates 009, 014, and 018. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match event memory, observer features, relocation operations, and cost; reject if a calibrated theft-risk policy matches novel-observer transfer |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Relocating every observed cache can waste
effort and reveal new locations. Any digital translation must score secrecy,
availability, relocation cost, false alarms, and adversarial observer behavior
separately.

## 7. Uncertainty monitoring and active information purchase

A bottlenose dolphin selectively used an uncertainty response around an
auditory discrimination boundary; the task required extensive threshold
training
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.124.4.391)). Rhesus macaques
generalized an uncertain response to familiar tasks
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.32.2.185)), while broader test
batteries found individual differences and only provisional evidence for a
domain-general private cue
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2016.12.004)). Rats declined difficult duration
discriminations when that option was available
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2007.01.061)). Baboons requested selective
re-presentation of poorly seen targets
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-015-0907-8)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | classify, decline, check, brighten, or repeat information under graded perceptual or memory difficulty |
| observed state | stimulus difficulty, choice, information cost, accuracy, response time, repeated acquisition |
| sensory/motor opportunity | optional response physically available before or after test display; public cues counterbalanced |
| training history | uncertainty/decline response shaping, reward schedule, familiar-task exposure, and transfer order declared |
| controls | forced trials, no-decline baseline, prospective choice before test display, novel modality/task, response-strength model |
| inferential limit | adaptive use supports uncertainty-sensitive control; it does not by itself establish reflective awareness or one domain-general monitor |
| timescale/units | milliseconds/seconds, stimulus distance from threshold, choice and error proportions, reward/query cost |
| possible system translation | calibrated selective prediction with iterative value-of-information sensing and explicit query cost |
| strongest ordinary null | confidence from logits or value margin, learned difficulty cue, risk–coverage calibration, and POMDP value of information |
| exact dedup | P-001 selective allocation; P-002 exception escalation; P-007 prediction-error allocation; Candidates 007, 010, and 014. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | equalize labels, queries, observations, calibration data, latency, and joules; require cross-task transfer after public difficulty cues are removed |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Accuracy conditional on accepted trials can
rise merely because coverage falls. A system must publish the full risk–
coverage–cost surface and behavior on confidently wrong out-of-distribution
inputs. Transfer from one perceptual task to another is weaker than prospective
information seeking in a memory task where the test display cannot cue choice.

## 8. Imitation, emulation, and result copying

Chimpanzees copied more causally irrelevant actions when an apparatus was
opaque and tended to omit them when its mechanism was visible; children copied
more of the demonstrated action sequence in both conditions
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-004-0239-6)). Other work found
chimpanzees recognized previously successful actions yet did not reproduce the
human actor's bodily movements
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2013.07.015)). Goffin's cockatoos
acquired aspects of tool use socially but did not simply reproduce every
manufacturing action
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2014.0972)). A trained bottlenose dolphin
copied familiar motor and vocal behaviors while sighted or blindfolded, using
auditory or echolocation information when vision was unavailable
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.46867/IJCP.2010.23.04.08)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | observe a demonstrator produce an action and outcome, then act on the same or transferred apparatus |
| observed state | action form, object trajectory, location, result, demonstrator identity, learner reproduction |
| sensory/motor opportunity | demonstrator and learner morphologies, viewpoint, modality, and executable action repertoire documented |
| training history | copy command, enculturation, action familiarity, apparatus experience, and social exposure separated |
| controls | ghost/result-only, end-state, no-demonstrator, novel action, opaque/transparent mechanism, alternative efficient action |
| inferential limit | social acquisition does not identify action imitation unless result, location, affordance, and attention learning are excluded |

**Failure and negative transfer.** High-fidelity action copying can preserve

when a hidden technique matters. The useful system needs a declared copying
target, not maximal behavioral resemblance.

## 9. Teaching as learner-state-contingent control

Wild meerkat helpers changed whether they supplied dead, disabled, or live
scorpions according to pup age and begging calls, and experimental provisioning
altered prey-handling acquisition
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128727)). During tandem runs,
*Temnothorax* ants used bidirectional contact to regulate leader speed and
enabled followers to learn a route, at a measurable cost to the leader
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/439153a)). These satisfy an operational teaching
definition without requiring that the teacher represents the learner's beliefs.

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | knowledgeable actor modifies provisioning or movement contingent on learner behavior, changing learner acquisition |
| observed state | learner age/signal/location, teacher action, teacher delay or risk, learner retention and later performance |
| sensory/motor opportunity | feedback channel, route visibility, prey danger and practice opportunity physically defined |
| training history | learner prior handling/navigation and teacher experience measured; no human shaping assumed |
| controls | playback or age manipulation, noncontingent provisioning, solo route, yoked exposure, equal practice without teacher feedback |
| inferential limit | operational teaching need not entail pedagogy, intention attribution, or explicit learner model |
| timescale/units | seconds/minutes per event, days of development, teacher delay, learner error and retention proportions |
| possible system translation | a teacher policy that chooses example difficulty or guidance from observable learner state while charging teacher cost |
| strongest ordinary null | curriculum learning, adaptive tutoring, active learning, scheduled scaffolding, and behavior shaping |
| exact dedup | P-001 allocation; P-002 escalation; P-003 traces; Candidate 004 curriculum and Candidate 019 inheritance. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | equalize examples, learner actions, feedback bits, teacher/evaluator time, and energy; require better delayed transfer than tuned adaptive curriculum |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Assistance that accelerates immediate
success can suppress independent recovery. Score teacher dependence, retention,
novel transfer, teacher cost, and learner harm separately.

## 10. Selective social-learning strategies

In captive groups, chimpanzees preferentially adopted a method demonstrated by
an older, high-ranking, previously successful model when exposure and payoff
were controlled
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0010625)). A group-level foraging
study identified simultaneous biases toward dominant and knowledgeable models,
with greater copying under uncertainty and for lower-ranking learners
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2014.09.002)). Longitudinal Shark
Bay data and network-based diffusion analyses support predominantly vertical
social transmission of sponge tool use in dolphin matrilines while accounting
for ecology and genetic association
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0500232102);
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2019.0227)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | choose whether and from whom to acquire a demonstrated foraging or tool method |
| observed state | model experience, rank, payoff, network exposure, learner uncertainty, ecology, acquisition time |
| sensory/motor opportunity | who can observe whom, proximity, demonstrator rate, habitat access and tool availability measured |
| training history | model expertise, learner baseline, maternal exposure, prior technique and payoff history declared |
| controls | equal demonstrations/payoffs, asocial exposure, multiple models, ecology/genetics/network covariates, novel model |
| inferential limit | a transmission bias can arise from attention, access, association, or payoff history; “prestige” is not directly observed in nonhuman subjects |
| timescale/units | demonstrations/count, acquisition days/years, association/network weight, hazard per day, success proportion |
| possible system translation | route demonstrations by domain-specific reliability, relevance, access, uncertainty, and audit history rather than undifferentiated majority vote |
| strongest ordinary null | contextual bandit over teachers, confidence-weighted ensembling, nearest expert routing, and network exposure model |
| exact dedup | P-001 allocation; P-004 diversity/selection; P-013 external state; Candidates 008, 015, 019, and 020. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match access to teachers, examples, metadata, evaluator labels, network messages, and energy; require gain after rank and exposure cues are counterbalanced |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Copying a salient or dominant actor can
stabilize a locally common but inferior technique and suppress innovations
from peripheral actors. Reliability must be domain-, time-, and task-qualified;
social status is not a transferable truth score.

## 11. Social acquisition beyond the individual innovation frontier

Two chimpanzee groups received three months of access to a sequential puzzle
without a spontaneous solution; after one trained model per group was
introduced, 14 naive group members acquired the sequence, with diffusion
related to social exposure
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01836-5)). Bumblebees generally did
not spontaneously solve an out-of-reach string-pulling task but acquired it
after staged training or observation, and transmission chains remained
consistent with associative mechanisms
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002564)). Bees also copied and
improved a demonstrated ball-rolling route
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aag2360)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | acquire a multi-action solution absent during a substantial asocial baseline after social exposure |
| observed state | baseline attempts, demonstrator exposure, network contact, acquisition order, action sequence and retention |
| sensory/motor opportunity | all required materials/actions available in baseline; demonstrator visibility and access quantified |
| training history | model training and learner's partial-action history retained; baseline duration alone is not infinite impossibility |
| controls | unseeded group, ghost/end-state, staged asocial shaping, alternative demonstrator, network-based diffusion |
| inferential limit | failure to innovate during a finite sample is not proof of inability; social acquisition may combine attention, local enhancement and re-innovation |
| timescale/units | attempts/count, observation hours, acquisition days/months, action edit distance, network hazard |
| possible system translation | population learner that preserves validated demonstrations for skills whose estimated independent discovery time exceeds the sharing cost |
| strongest ordinary null | centralized replay buffer, curriculum/shaping, demonstration-augmented RL, scripted workflow, and retrieval |
| exact dedup | P-004 diversity/selection; P-013 external state; Candidate 004 curriculum and Candidate 019 inheritance. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | charge demonstrator training, failed baseline attempts, network exposure, storage, and evaluation; compare with centralized shared replay at equal cumulative effort |

**Failure and negative transfer.** A social system can spread a behavior faster
while increasing total population effort or locking in a fragile sequence.
Require held-out task benefit, independent execution after model removal, and
robustness to a deliberately suboptimal demonstrator.

## 12. Innovation as recombination plus functional transfer

One Goffin's cockatoo spontaneously manufactured a probing tool in the original
report, and multiple subjects later transferred the target tool type across
materials
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.09.002);
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2016.0689)). In a five-lock apparatus, one
naive cockatoo completed all steps within the first five sessions and other
birds succeeded after demonstration or incremental experience; transfer tests
showed sensitivity to changed lock function and order
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068979)). An Asian elephant moved
a cube, stood on it, generalized to other supports, and attempted stacking,
but the key evidence came from one of three subjects and some component actions
had prior history
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023251)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | produce a new-to-subject action sequence or object transformation that satisfies a functional constraint |
| observed state | prior action inventory, first solution latency, failed attempts, component reuse, transfer and efficiency |
| sensory/motor opportunity | manipulable materials, action primitives, support surfaces, reach and feedback all declared |
| training history | every known component action, husbandry practice, demonstration and prior apparatus task retained |
| controls | no demonstration, alternative materials, disabled/reordered parts, matched random exploration, naive subjects/sites |
| inferential limit | novelty relative to observed history is not creation without antecedents; sudden terminal success can follow covert or unrecorded exploration |
| timescale/units | sessions/count, seconds/minutes to solution, manipulation count, sequence length, material dimensions |
| possible system translation | retrieve partial operators and relations, recombine them under functional constraints, test, retain provenance, and select by outcome |
| strongest ordinary null | program synthesis from an operator library, planning/search, evolutionary variation, retrieval plus composition, and stochastic sampling with verifier |
| exact dedup | P-004 diversity/selection; P-007 residual allocation; P-010 structural offloading; Candidates 004 and 019. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match operator library, examples, rollouts, evaluator calls, retries, and joules; reject if ordinary synthesis/search matches novelty, validity, and transfer |

**Failure and negative transfer.** “Insight” is a description of the observed
time course, not a mechanism. Report all unrewarded manipulation and husbandry
history; freeze the action vocabulary before judging whether a sequence is new.

## 13. Exploration and play as property sampling

Kea and New Caledonian crows that could explore novel objects before a later
tool-choice test sometimes selected functional objects above chance, whereas a
no-exploration control did not show the same pattern
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.170652)). The birds did not reliably change
the duration or form of exploration after learning the objects would become
task relevant, so the evidence supports incidental property acquisition more
strongly than strategic information seeking. In a multi-access box, only one
of six kea and one of five crows mastered all four solutions, and species-
typical manipulation styles affected which routes were discovered
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0020231)).

| Field | Audit record |
| --- | --- |
| task | manipulate unrewarded objects, then choose or deploy them in a later functional task |

| sensory/motor opportunity | haptic/visual access, object placement, bill/foot use, reachable action set and neophobia measured |
| training history | prior material and tool tasks, captivity enrichment, age, and unrewarded object experience declared |
| controls | no-exploration group, yoked contact time, task-before-exploration, visually matched nonfunctional objects, random manipulation |
| inferential limit | later use supports information acquisition; unchanged exploration after task exposure argues against strong strategic sampling |
| timescale/units | seconds/minutes of contact, manipulation count/diversity, later selection proportion, retention interval |
| possible system translation | maintain a bounded exploration buffer of object–action–effect observations and retrieve it when a later task exposes relevance |
| strongest ordinary null | random action, count-based exploration, novelty bonus, curiosity/information gain, and replay buffer |
| exact dedup | P-003 temporary trace; P-004 diversity/selection; P-007 prediction error; Candidate 004 curriculum. No new principle/candidate. |
| equal-budget falsification | match environment steps, action diversity, resets, memory, and energy; require better delayed functional transfer than curiosity and random exploration |

**Failure and negative transfer.** Persistent manipulation can increase
accidental solution probability without learning general properties. A useful
translation must predict which sampled property transfers, not merely correlate
total contact time with later success.

## 14. Inhibitory control as a task-specific plant interaction

MacLean et al. compared 36 species on A-not-B and transparent-cylinder tasks

possibility that the cylinder task interacts with species-specific vision,
motor approach, and experience rather than measuring one latent self-control
capacity
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-017-1131-5)). Cross-site sampling and
construct-validity analyses further caution against stable species ranks from

([DOI](https://doi.org/10.26451/abc.07.01.02.2020)).

| training history | opaque-cylinder criterion, prior detour experience, reinforcement, captivity and apparatus familiarity declared |

| equal-budget falsification | use multiple plant geometries and inhibition tasks; equalize practice, observations, actions, tuning and compute; reject a general gate if gains do not cross tasks |

**Failure and negative transfer.** A high inhibition threshold can prevent

delay, and adaptation speed; do not optimize cylinder accuracy alone.

## 15. Flexible multi-route problem solving

Kea and New Caledonian crows differed in how they opened a four-route
multi-access box, learned alternatives when used routes were blocked, and
showed pronounced individual variation
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0020231)). Goffin's cockatoos made
ratchet-like progress on a five-lock problem and changed behavior when locks
were removed or reordered
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068979)). Four elephants that had
been trained to remove a lid initially continued to remove it even when placed
beside an open bucket, a negative result for broad causal use of the obstacle
relation
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.32.1.91)).

| Field | Audit record |

| sensory/motor opportunity | every route's visibility, reach, material resistance, and species-compatible actuator measured |
| training history | route discovery order, prior successes, demonstrations and blocked-route experience preserved |

## 16. Peripheral motor programs in a flexible arm

Mechanically or electrically evoked extensions in octopus arms disconnected
from the brain shared kinematic features with intact reaching, supporting a
basic peripheral extension program
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1060976)). During fetching, octopuses
temporarily organized a flexible arm into a quasi-articulated structure whose
geometry and muscle activity varied with the grasp point
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/433595a)). These results identify local control
and dynamic reduction of degrees of freedom; they do not make an arm an
independent cognitive agent.

## 18. Ecological validity, morphology, and training history

The elephant cube study explicitly argued that earlier stick tasks interfered
with the trunk's sensory role

behavioral guidance despite requests for minimal intervention
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-022-01668-8)). Multi-access and cylinder
studies show that morphology, exploration style, persistence, site, and task
training change observed performance
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0020231);
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-017-1131-5)). Comparative replication is
therefore a transport problem, not a matter of replaying nominal instructions.

## Null, negative-result, and construct-validity register

1. **Trap-tube non-transfer:** rooks and capuchins could acquire terminal
   success while failing controls intended to preserve or invert the causal
   relation. This is positive evidence for learning and negative evidence for
   an unrestricted causal-reasoning interpretation.
2. **Aesop trial-course confound:** when learning across trials is modeled,
   terminal preference does not require prior water-displacement understanding.
3. **Elephant lid perseveration:** trained lid removal continued after the lid
   no longer blocked food, limiting a broad causal interpretation.
4. **Finite innovation failures:** most bumblebees did not independently learn
   string pulling, and chimpanzee groups did not solve one sequential puzzle
   during a three-month baseline. These bound sampled discovery rates, not
   logical capability.
5. **Single-subject innovation:** the famous crow hook and elephant cube
   demonstrations are important existence cases with weak prevalence
   estimates and incomplete lifetime-history control.
6. **Exploration without strategic retargeting:** prior object exploration
   improved some later choices, but kea and crows did not reliably increase or
   redirect exploration after learning its relevance.
7. **Cylinder construct dispute:** learning effects, morphology, transparency,
   site, and poor convergence with other inhibition tasks prevent a unitary
   self-control interpretation.
8. **Metacognition associative null:** difficulty, reward, response strength,
   and publicly visible test features can support adaptive decline behavior.
   Cross-domain and prospective information-seeking controls are stronger.
9. **Imitation failure with success recognition:** chimpanzees can recognize an
   effective action without reproducing its form, so result learning and action
   imitation remain separable.
10. **Ecological opportunity confound:** requiring an elephant to hold a stick
    in the trunk can occupy a major sensor; nominally equivalent tasks can be
    functionally unequal.
11. **Unplanned human cueing:** handler interventions in elephant training show
    that protocol text does not guarantee the animal's actual information set.
12. **Peripheral-control overclaim:** evoked extension after disconnection
    demonstrates a local motor program, not an independently deliberating arm.

Small samples are often unavoidable in comparative work, but they change the
claim. Trial count does not replace subject count; repeated measures improve
within-subject precision but do not estimate population prevalence. Null
results should report interval estimates or Bayes factors where appropriate,
not be converted into declarations that a taxon lacks a faculty.

## Equal-budget falsification suite

### E-CCOG-01 — Affordance and manufacture transfer

Give embodied agents matched object observations and manipulation primitives
across materials whose fracture, rigidity, length, and cost vary. Compare
affordance-plus-search, model-based RL, program synthesis, retrieval, and the
proposed architecture. Hold demonstrations, environment steps, resets,
planning expansions, parameters, actuator wear, human shaping, and joules
constant. Score first-trial functional transfer, manufacture time, material
waste, unsafe contacts, and later reuse. Reject the biological residual if
ordinary search reaches the same frontier.

### E-CCOG-02 — Causal transfer ladder

Train on a family of object–trap or displacement tasks, then test functional
inversion, surface-cue reversal, visually novel causal equivalence, hidden
connectors, and impossible controls. Freeze first-trial analysis and cap
intervention count. Compare feature RL, model-based RL, object-centric world
models, retrieval, and the proposal. Reject if gain appears only after test
reinforcement or vanishes when arbitrary cues are counterbalanced.

### E-CCOG-03 — Future resource reservation

Separate choice and use by variable delays, rooms, distractor tasks, and
current/future goal conflicts. Compare value tables, successor
representations, MPC/POMDP planning, retrieval, and a proposed prospective
trace. Match episodes, rollouts, context bits, storage, carrying cost, and
energy. Require transfer to new item–future-task recombinations; reject if a
cached context–item policy matches performance.

### E-CCOG-04 — Event memory under semantic nulls

Use unique events with what, where, elapsed time, changing decay laws, and
post-encoding updates. Give all methods equal bytes and retrieval latency.
Compare factorized semantic-plus-spatial memory, recurrent state, nearest-event
retrieval, external database, and a bound event store. Require correct
exceptions and update propagation; reject if familiar interval rules explain
the gain.

### E-CCOG-05 — Costed uncertainty control

Vary perceptual and memory difficulty independently of visible test features.
Offer abstain, repeat, active sensor, or human query at explicit cost. Compare
logit confidence, ensembles, conformal/selective prediction, POMDP value of
information, and the proposed monitor. Match labels, queries, calibration,
latency, and joules. Report full risk–coverage–cost surfaces and reject if a
public difficulty feature or ordinary VOI policy explains behavior.

### E-CCOG-06 — Copy target decomposition

Cross action form, end state, trajectory, demonstrator identity, embodiment,
and causal relevance. Include ghost, result-only, inefficient action, and
novel-action trials. Compare behavior cloning, goal-conditioned imitation,
inverse RL, affordance learning, retrieval, and structured decomposition.
Charge demonstrations, frames/tokens, environment actions, and compute. Reject
if matching terminal outcomes explains apparent imitation.

### E-CCOG-07 — Social acquisition versus shared replay

Give populations a skill with a measured independent-discovery distribution,
then seed correct, inefficient, and adversarial demonstrators under network
turnover. Compare social policies, central replay, curriculum/shaping, explicit
workflow artifacts, and Candidate 019. Charge demonstrator training, failed
search, exposure, evaluation, communication, storage, and human effort. Score
population task value, independent execution, diversity, lock-in, recovery,
and cumulative joules.

### E-CCOG-08 — Centralized versus peripheral plant control

Use the same compliant multi-segment plant, sensors, and task family for a
central controller, local reflex/controller stack, hierarchical goal-plus-local
control, and the proposal. Sweep occlusion, contact, load, delay, segment loss,
sensor failure, and changed morphology. Match model access, parameters,
training, bandwidth, latency hardware, and energy boundary. Score endpoint
error, stability, override latency, local recovery, message bytes, and joules.
Reject if passive mechanics or standard hierarchical control explains the gain.

## Analysis and reporting requirements

- Randomize and analyze at the unit receiving the intervention. Trials are not
  independent animals; animals are not automatically independent of social
  group, rearing cohort, handler, site, or population.
- Publish subject-level trajectories, including first trials, failures,
  exclusions, attrition, stopping rules, and prior participation in related
  tasks.
- Record the actual information channel: vision, echolocation, olfaction,
  chemotaction, haptics, demonstrator exposure, handler cue, apparatus sound,
  and residual food cues.
- Record the feasible actuator set and morphology. Equivalent desired outcomes
  do not imply equivalent motor problems.
- Separate existence, prevalence, population mean, within-subject learning,
  cross-task transfer, and species-level transport claims.
- Use blinded behavioral coding where feasible and publish inter-rater
  agreement, codebooks, video exclusions, and alternative scoring rules.
- Counterbalance side, material, demonstrator, payoff, object salience, and
  intervention order. Analyze trial course rather than pooling acquisition
  into a final success rate.
- For social diffusion, publish observation networks, opportunities, ecology,
  genetic relatedness where relevant, missing-data assumptions, and model
  sensitivity. Network association is not action copying.
- For computational translations, pair runs on identical base models, seeds,
  data order, action libraries, environment resets, hyperparameter trials, and
  hardware reservations.
- Report episodes and actions as counts; time in seconds/hours/days; distance in
  metres; mass in kilograms; force in newtons; memory/data in bytes; latency in
  seconds; human effort in person-hours; and energy in joules.
- Publish negative transfer, unsafe actions, redundant copying, teacher
  dependence, social lock-in, centralized-baseline wins, and unused budget.
- Replicate a residual across at least two task families, plant geometries,
  training histories, model families, and hardware classes before promotion.

## Promotion and retirement gates

Promote no comparative-cognition-derived principle from behavioral analogy.
The task/opportunity/history contract is an audit refinement, not a candidate.
It becomes part of Candidate 014 only if applying it changes at least one
architecture conclusion, catches a reproducible opportunity or history
confound missed by the existing evidence stack, and improves hidden-plant
prediction without materially increasing false rejection.

Retire any proposed residual when:

- affordance learning plus search matches manufacture or tool transfer;
- feature or model-based RL matches causal intervention transfer;
- a value table or successor representation matches future preparation;
- semantic decay plus spatial memory matches event-memory tasks;
- calibrated confidence or ordinary value of information matches checking;
- goal-conditioned imitation or result learning matches social acquisition;
- adaptive curriculum matches operational teaching;
- centralized replay matches population inheritance at lower cumulative cost;
- curiosity or random exploration matches delayed property use;
- a learned action gate matches “inhibitory control” across plant changes; or
- standard hierarchical control plus passive mechanics matches local octopus-
  inspired control under perturbation.

The burden is a Pareto improvement on literal task value, transfer, recovery,
latency, memory, human effort, and lifecycle energy—not resemblance to animal
behavior.

## Verdict

This audit promotes no new principle and no new experiment candidate.
Comparative cognition contributes a rigorous test grammar: define the task,
actual observation, feasible action, training history, intervention, control,
timescale, and sampling unit before naming the mechanism. Across tool use,
memory, uncertainty, social learning, innovation, inhibition, and distributed
control, the recurring transferable content already deduplicates into the
existing registry.

The next useful step is to run E-CCOG-01–08 as adversarial fixtures for
Candidates 004, 006, 007, 014, 018, and 019. If affordance learning, standard
planning, selective prediction, shared replay, or hierarchical control wins at
equal budget, retire the comparative residual instead of renaming the baseline.
