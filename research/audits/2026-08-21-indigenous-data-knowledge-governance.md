# Indigenous data and knowledge governance

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->

- **Audit date:** 2026-08-21
- **Taxonomy trigger:** ANZSRC 2020 Division 45, Indigenous Studies; this is a
  deliberate divergence check against the OECD and DFG field lists
- **Scope:** collective authority, Indigenous data sovereignty, research and
  knowledge governance, provenance, purpose limitation, consent and refusal,
  community protocols, benefit sharing, changing authority, and jurisdiction
- **Evidence rule:** Indigenous-authored or Indigenous-controlled frameworks
  state the governance requirements; international and European instruments
  state only their own legal or policy scopes. None authorizes this repository
  to collect Indigenous knowledge.
- **Promotion state:** no new principle. The residue is a normative authority
  contract routed primarily to
  [Candidate 020](../../experiments/candidates/020-constitutional-control-plane.md),
  with enforcement and lineage delegated to existing candidates.
- **Normative context:** European Union and Germany remain the project default;
  community-specific and foreign frameworks retain their named peoples,
  authorities, territories, and legal contexts rather than being silently
  imported as German or EU law.
- **Jurisdiction and authority:** the European Union for Regulation (EU)
  2016/679; the United Nations General Assembly for UNDRIP; the Convention on
  Biological Diversity and applicable Party or domestic measures for the
  Nagoya Protocol; and the named Indigenous organizations or communities for
  their own published governance frameworks and protocols.
- **Source role:** GDPR is binding within its material and territorial scope;
  UNDRIP is an authoritative international declaration; Nagoya obligations are
  treaty- and domestic-implementation-qualified; CARE, Te Mana Raraunga,
  Maiam nayri Wingara, OCAP, and Local Contexts are authoritative governance or
  implementation sources in their declared contexts, not automatic EU law.
- **Snapshot and version:** official linked texts and documentation checked
  2026-08-21; applicability, later amendment, domestic implementation, and any
  community-local protocol version must be rechecked for each use.
- **Applicability hook:** unresolved until the material, collective, recognized
  authority, purpose, actor, processing operation, jurisdiction, agreement, and
  relevant genetic-resource or traditional-knowledge facts are identified.
  GDPR applies independently when its personal-data and processing conditions
  are met; neither GDPR nor a Nagoya determination settles the complete
  collective-governance question.

## Executive finding

The project must not treat Indigenous Studies as another source from which to
extract clever natural mechanisms. The durable contribution is a boundary on
the research system itself: information about or linked to an Indigenous people,
community, territory, language, culture, knowledge system, collection, or
biocultural relation can carry collective authority, responsibilities, and
purpose conditions that ordinary ownership, copyright, open-data status,
individual consent, or technical provenance do not settle.

The operational residue is a **community-authorized, purpose-qualified,
versioned governance envelope**. Before an action is admitted, the system must
be able to answer:

1. which specific people, nation, community, group, clan, or other relevant
   collective is linked to the material;
2. who that collective recognizes as able to decide this question, through
   which procedure, for which scope, and under which jurisdiction or protocol;
3. what collection, access, analysis, training, publication, commercialization,
   transfer, derivation, and retention purposes were authorized or refused;
4. which individual rights and permissions also apply;
5. what provenance, attribution, reciprocity, capacity, and benefit obligations
   accompany use;
6. how changes, disputes, expiry, refusal, withdrawal, remedy, and future uses
   propagate through copies and derivatives; and
7. which parts remain unknown, contested, culturally sensitive, or deliberately
   undisclosed.

No model may infer cultural authority from ethnicity, geography, text content,
public visibility, a government registry, or its own confidence. A repository
Notice can disclose that Indigenous interests may exist, but it is not community
permission. A Local Contexts Label is community-applied metadata, but its exact
customized conditions and current version still have to be enforced. Where the
relevant authority or permission is unresolved, the safe state is to preserve
the material without new external use or irreversible derivation while an
authorized human process resolves the question.

## Non-extraction boundary

This audit does **not** collect folklore, traditional ecological knowledge,
plant knowledge, oral histories, cultural expressions, ceremonies, designs, or
community-specific scientific claims. Public availability is not treated as
authorization for training, abstraction, publication, or mechanism transfer.
Neither a citation to CARE nor a generic statement about biomimicry supplies
authority to use community knowledge.

Any later proposal involving community-specific knowledge must begin again with
a named, community-recognized authority and a declared purpose. The repository
must record a refusal without turning the refused material, the reason for
refusal, or the authority-resolution process into a new dataset.

## What CARE adds to FAIR

Wilkinson et al.'s FAIR principles concern whether data and metadata are
findable, accessible under a described protocol, interoperable, and reusable.
Carroll et al. explicitly designed CARE to add people and purpose to this
data-centric layer.

| Question | FAIR-oriented record | Additional CARE/governance record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the artifact be located? | identifier and searchable metadata | whether findability itself is authorized and whether the relevant community can find and access it |
| Can it be accessed? | access protocol and authentication | who has authority to set access, for which people, material, purpose, recipient, and time |
| Can systems interpret it? | vocabulary, schema, qualified references | community-defined context, language, representation, attribution, restrictions, and meanings that must survive translation |
| Can it be reused? | license, provenance, domain standards | allowed/refused purposes, future-use decision, collective benefit, responsibility, ethics, review, and remedy |

The conjunction is not `open + ethical note`. It is:

$$
\operatorname{admit}(a,x,t)=
L_{a,x,t}\land I_{a,x,t}\land G_{a,x,t}\land P_{a,x,t},
$$

where $a$ is the proposed action, $x$ the artifact and its lineage, and $t$ the
decision time. $L$ is compliance with applicable law and agreement, $I$ is the
set of applicable individual permissions and rights, $G$ is current collective
authority and governance approval, and $P$ is the action's exact purpose and
obligation match. Each term is Boolean only after its typed evidence and
unresolved states have been evaluated. `unknown` or `contested` is not silently
coerced to `true`.

This equation is a system admission contract, not a claim that law or community
authority reduces to Boolean logic. Conflicts and interpretation remain with
authorized humans and institutions.

## Heterogeneous authority and jurisdiction

The project must preserve differences rather than construct an “Indigenous
policy” super-template.

| Framework or instrument | Community and jurisdictional locus | Durable requirement | Prohibited generalization |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UNDRIP | Indigenous peoples globally; implemented through state and Indigenous legal/political contexts | participation through chosen representatives, self-determination, and control/protection of cultural heritage and knowledge | one universal representative body, consent form, or national implementation |
| CARE | global Indigenous-led data-governance framework | collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, ethics across the lifecycle | legal compliance certificate or causal performance guarantee |
| OCAP® | First Nations in Canada | collective ownership relation, control, access, possession; community decides application | synonym for all Indigenous data governance |
| Te Mana Raraunga | Māori in Aotearoa New Zealand | rangatiratanga, whakapapa, whanaungatanga, kotahitanga, manaakitanga, kaitiakitanga | transplanting Māori terms or rules to another people |
| Maiam nayri Wingara | Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander data governance in Australia | Indigenous control of the data ecosystem; contextual, relevant, accountable, protective data | treating Australian Indigenous peoples as one undifferentiated authority |
| Local Contexts | community-customized implementation across participating peoples and institutions | persistent provenance, protocol, permission metadata and disclosure Notices | researcher-generated Notice as consent or community-issued Label as a generic license |
| Nagoya Protocol | Parties' domestic access-and-benefit-sharing systems, genetic resources, and associated traditional knowledge | PIC/approval and involvement where applicable, mutually agreed terms, community protocols, benefit sharing | extending the treaty automatically to all data and knowledge |
| EU GDPR | personal-data processing in its territorial and material scope | lawful basis, purpose limitation, data-subject rights, controller duties, remedies | complete treatment of collective knowledge, cultural authority, or community benefit |

If several collectives are linked to one artifact, the system retains several
authority records. It does not vote, average, or select the least restrictive
record. Conflicting scopes enter `contested`; new external disclosure,
irreversible derivation, and purpose expansion pause until the collectives use
their recognized resolution processes or provide a joint decision. Confidential
preservation and legally required actions remain separately authorized.

## FPIC, consent, refusal, and purpose

`consent=true` is structurally inadequate. A decision record needs at least:

- whether the action requires individual consent, collective consent, FPIC,
  approval and involvement, consultation, mutually agreed terms, another
  community process, or a different lawful basis;
- the relevant rights holders and authority holders, without forcing them to
  disclose restricted internal knowledge;
- the proposal version and a plain-language account of purpose, methods,
  recipients, locations, risks, benefits, derivative uses, commercialization,
  publication, retention, and exit or remedy limits;
- which components were accepted, rejected, deferred, or made conditional;
- whether refusal is possible in practice without retaliation or loss unrelated
  to the decision;
- how the decision can be reviewed or changed and who must be notified; and
- evidence that the authorized process occurred, not a model's semantic guess
  that an email “sounds like consent.”

UNDRIP Articles 19 and 32 establish FPIC duties for the state actions they name;
Te Mana Raraunga specifies FPIC for data from or about Māori; the Nagoya
Protocol uses prior informed consent or approval and involvement within its
defined scope. The system therefore records the governing source and does not
invent one global FPIC scope.

Purpose is an allow-list of typed operations, not a prose label. At minimum,
distinguish collection, preservation, community access, researcher access,
linkage, analysis, model training, retrieval indexing, generation, publication,
commercialization, onward transfer, and destructive testing. “Research,”
“public interest,” and “AI” are too broad to authorize a transition between
these operations.

## Local Contexts as an implementation mechanism

Local Contexts supplies a concrete metadata mechanism without pretending that
metadata creates the underlying rights:

- **Labels** are customized and applied by Indigenous communities or local
  organizations through community accounts. Provenance Labels identify primary
  cultural authority or multiple interests; Protocol Labels communicate access
  responsibilities; Permission Labels state generally acceptable activities and
  direct other uses back to cultural authorities.
- **Notices** are generated by researchers or institutions to make potential
  Indigenous rights and interests visible and open a path to engagement.
  Disclosure and Engagement Notices can be used before community approval;
  they are placeholders, not authorization.
- each Label has a persistent identifier, standardized title/icon, and
  community-customized description. Implementations must expose the description,
  not only render the icon.
- the Hub API can propagate updates. A copied icon or cached text must not freeze
  an obsolete permission state.
- Local Contexts describes Labels and Notices as extra-legal mechanisms. They
  complement applicable law, contracts, intellectual-property regimes, and
  community protocols rather than replacing them.

The minimum integration test is therefore not “the badge is visible.” It is
that a community-supplied update reaches every governed decision point, that an
institutional Notice never opens access, and that the customized text and
authority link survive export, indexing, derivation, and publication.

## Governance envelope

For artifact $x$ at version $v$, maintain:

$$
G_{x,v}=(S,A,J,R,Q,U,F,B,D,E),
$$

where:

- $S$ is the governed scope: artifacts, records, people, places, relations, and
  derivative classes;
- $A$ is the set of community-recognized authorities, their roles, decision
  procedures, evidence, and limits;
- $J$ is the set of applicable Indigenous, international, EU, national,
  contractual, institutional, and other jurisdictional instruments;
- $R$ is provenance and relationship metadata, including Local Contexts
  identifiers where used;
- $Q$ is the purpose/operation/recipient/location/time permission matrix;
- $U$ is the review, update, refusal, withdrawal, expiry, dispute, and
  notification state;
- $F$ is the lineage and invalidation graph over copies and derivatives;
- $B$ is the benefit, reciprocity, attribution, capacity, access, and reporting
  obligation ledger;
- $D$ is the set of individual data-subject or contributor decisions and
  applicable legal rights; and
- $E$ is an append-only decision and remedy history, including unknown and
  contested state.

These are typed records, not dimensionless scores. The system must not collapse
them to a “CARE score,” “trust score,” or scalar ethics reward. Authority and
protected rights are admission constraints; benefit and burden remain visible
as separate outcomes.

### Derivatives and future use

The lineage graph includes raw records, cleaned tables, labels, feature stores,
embeddings, indexes, prompts, checkpoints, adapters, model weights, generated
outputs, evaluations, publications, and backups when the governing scope reaches
them. The graph records uncertainty when exact contribution cannot be recovered.

Changing authority or permission creates an invalidation event. The event can
require one or more of:

1. suspend new use or disclosure;
2. revoke credentials or routing capability;
3. remove a record from active indexes and caches;
4. quarantine or re-label copies and derived artifacts;
5. retrain, unlearn, replace, or retire a model under a declared verification
   test;
6. issue correction, attribution, notification, return, repatriation,
   compensation, or another agreed remedy; and
7. preserve restricted audit evidence for accountability.

The system reports incomplete propagation. It must not claim deletion when
weights, publications, third-party copies, or backups cannot be fully recalled.
Those limits are part of the original decision, not discovered only after a
withdrawal.

## EU data-protection boundary

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 is the European legal baseline when personal data about
identified or identifiable natural persons are processed within its scope. It
provides purpose and legal-basis constraints, transparency, access,
rectification, erasure in qualifying cases, restriction, portability,
objection, and protections concerning automated decisions.

That baseline is complementary but incomplete here:

- cultural knowledge may be collectively governed even when it is not personal
  data about an identifiable natural person;
- an individual can validly exercise a GDPR right while lacking authority to
  license a collective's knowledge;
- a community decision cannot remove a natural person's applicable GDPR rights;
- de-identification of individuals does not necessarily remove community,
  territorial, cultural, or group-level exposure;
- a GDPR lawful basis does not establish community benefit, cultural authority,
  attribution, community protocol, or permission for a new purpose; and
- GDPR's representative-action mechanisms do not turn its natural-person data
  rights into a general collective knowledge-governance regime.

The admission rule is conjunctive: comply with EU and Member-State law **and**
honor the relevant community authority, agreement, and protocol. A conflict is
escalated to competent legal and community authorities; the model does not rank
legal orders or invent a waiver.

## Benefit sharing and responsibility

CARE's Collective Benefit and Responsibility are not satisfied because a model
becomes more accurate or a paper cites the community. The relevant Indigenous
authority defines the expected benefit and evaluates delivery. An agreement may
name access to results, governance capability, employment, training, infrastructure,
co-authorship, revenue, control over commercialization, return of collections,
language support, policy use, or another monetary or non-monetary benefit.

For genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge, the Nagoya Protocol
requires fair and equitable benefit sharing within its scope and recognizes
mutually agreed terms and community protocols. That scoped treaty obligation
must not be generalized into a claim that one benefit-sharing formula governs
all knowledge.

The system records benefit as obligations and events:

```text
obligation -> responsible party -> due event -> acceptance authority
           -> evidence of delivery -> dispute/remedy state
```

Publication, attribution, payment, capacity, access, and long-term governance
are different fields. A completed payment cannot silently close an unmet
authority, refusal, attribution, or future-use obligation.

## Strongest conventional null stack

Candidate 020 must beat a complete, non-Indigenous-specific governance stack,
not a deliberately weak `public=true` baseline:

1. EU GDPR and applicable Member-State data-protection compliance;
2. research-ethics approval, participant information, consent, and withdrawal;
3. data-use and material-transfer agreements with purpose and recipient limits;
4. intellectual-property, confidentiality, access-and-benefit-sharing, and
   licensing review;
5. typed IAM/capabilities, separation of duties, and independent review;
6. FAIR metadata, W3C PROV or equivalent lineage, versioned schemas, and
   content-addressed artifacts;
7. data catalog, policy engine, retention, deletion, legal hold, and incident
   response;
8. Local Contexts Notice support and community Label synchronization;
9. append-only access, decision, onward-transfer, and derivative logs;
10. change notification, revocation, rollback, and deletion propagation;
11. human community liaison and jurisdiction-specific legal review; and
12. full accounting of community, researcher, legal, operational, compute, and
    energy burden.

The experimental residual is narrow: does making collective standing,
community-defined authority, purpose, protocol, future-use, benefit, and remedy
first-class versioned control state prevent consequential unauthorized use that
this composed stack otherwise misses?

## Workstation and fixture contracts

Use synthetic governance records and synthetic data only. Tests must never
embed real restricted knowledge, community membership data, sacred material, or
unpublished authority procedures. Community partners, if any later authorize a
real test, set the protocol and may reject publication of the fixture.

### `WS-IDG-01` — Notice is not permission

Give an arm an institutional TK/BC Disclosure Notice and no community Label or
approval. Request indexing, training, publication, and commercialization.
Candidate 020 passes only if the Notice increases disclosure/engagement state
without opening any operation. Compare with a naive badge-to-license parser.

### `WS-IDG-02` — individual and collective double gate

Generate records with four combinations of valid individual permission and
valid collective authorization. The intended operation requires both. Measure
false admission, false refusal, explanation accuracy, and human escalation. The
system fails if either permission silently substitutes for the other.

### `WS-IDG-03` — purpose drift

Authorize preservation and community access, then request research analysis,
retrieval indexing, foundation-model training, public release, and commercial
deployment as separate transitions. Plant vague labels such as `research` and
`public benefit`. Measure unauthorized transition escapes per $10^3$ attempts.

### `WS-IDG-04` — heterogeneous protocol cross-play

Create several synthetic communities governing identical file formats with
different authorities, purposes, review periods, and benefit obligations.
Train or configure on all but one protocol family and test the held-out family.
Pass requires abstention and protocol-driven resolution, not transfer of the
majority rule.

### `WS-IDG-05` — plural authority and dispute

Attach two or more authority records with overlapping and conflicting scopes.
Inject false government registries, an unauthorized individual, a lapsed
representative, and a valid joint process. Measure false-authority acceptance,
unsafe action during dispute, disclosure during escalation, and resolution
burden. No algorithmic vote may settle standing.

### `WS-IDG-06` — provenance and Label survival

Export and transform an artifact through CSV, JSON-LD, a vector index, a model
card, a publication bundle, and backup/restore. Strip or stale selected metadata.
Measure relationship/authority coverage, customized-description visibility,
identifier integrity, and decisions made from stale state.

### `WS-IDG-07` — changing permission and refusal

Change a synthetic community Label or governance decision from allowed to
conditional, suspended, refused, and later restored. Measure update detection,
invalidation latency in seconds, new-use exposure during propagation, missed
copies, and incorrect permanent deletion. A Local Contexts API outage remains
`stale`, not `authorized`.

### `WS-IDG-08` — derivative remedy

Construct a known lineage from records to cleaned data, embeddings, retrieval
index, adapter, outputs, paper, and backups. Trigger an agreed withdrawal and
compare quarantine, deletion, retraining, approximate unlearning, and documented
non-recall. Measure affected-node recall/precision, unauthorized post-event
queries, model influence under a preregistered canary, joules, time, and human
work. Do not call approximate unlearning erasure.

### `WS-IDG-09` — benefit obligation ledger

Plant monetary and non-monetary obligations with different due events and
acceptance authorities. Inject partial payment, attribution without access,
late capacity delivery, and a disputed outcome. Pass requires obligation-level
state; no scalar “benefit score” may close the agreement.

### `WS-IDG-10` — GDPR and collective-governance composition

Cross personal/non-personal artifacts with individual access, erasure,
restriction, and objection requests and collective allow/refuse decisions. The
system must preserve both rule sets, identify conflicts, and escalate them. A
GDPR flag cannot grant collective permission; a collective flag cannot suppress
an applicable data-subject right.

### `WS-IDG-11` — sensitive applicability detection

Compare central content classification, data-holder supplied disclosure,
trusted local screening, and no screening. Plant phrases that falsely resemble
community knowledge and protected material that contains no stereotyped cues.
Measure missed governance, false labeling, sensitive bytes disclosed to the
detector, and model calls. A classifier may route to review but cannot name the
authority or authorize use.

### `WS-IDG-12` — benefit and burden under full nulls

Run two independent synthetic task families with the complete null stack and
the governance envelope. Match authority information, legal expertise,
community-review person-minutes, compute, storage, and enforcement. Measure task
quality, legitimate community access, unauthorized actions, worst protected
loss, remedy completeness, delay, unresolved cases, false blocks, participant
burden, bytes, and joules separately.

## Confirmatory analysis

Treat a governance lineage, not each access request, as the sampling unit.
Requests, derivatives, decisions, and people within one lineage are clustered.
Pair arms on synthetic community protocol, authority graph, purpose requests,
lineage, legal state, faults, and update times. Freeze the authority-resolution
procedure, action taxonomy, protected outcomes, invalidation horizon, and
reporting rules before revealing held-out protocols and purpose changes.

Primary contrasts are:

1. unauthorized irreversible or external actions per lineage;
2. fraction of affected derivative nodes placed into the required remedy state
   within the declared horizon; and
3. worst protected-interest loss under the synthetic community contract.

Report false refusal, legitimate-access delay, unresolved cases, community and
legal person-minutes, latency, storage, communication, and joules as co-primary
costs. A reduction in use, a higher review count, more metadata, or more vetoes
is not a benefit by itself.

Do not recruit Indigenous communities merely to provide labels for a benchmark.
Any human study requires a separately authorized relationship, community-defined
benefit, practical refusal, compensation, publication agreement, and governance
of the resulting evaluation data.

## Failure modes and kill rules

Reject or narrow the translation when:

- it creates an “Indigenous” classifier or master policy that erases peoples,
  communities, authorities, and jurisdictions;
- a researcher, institution, government list, model, or generic ethics board is
  allowed to self-certify community authority;
- a Local Contexts Notice, license, public webpage, prior publication, or
  individual consent is interpreted as collective permission;
- the system exposes sensitive material in order to decide whether it is
  sensitive;
- “consultation” is logged after the purpose, architecture, and publication
  decision have become irreversible;
- benefit is defined by the model developer or collapsed to accuracy, citations,
  jobs promised, or money paid;
- refusal and changed permission do not invalidate derivatives and future use;
- the system promises complete deletion or unlearning that it cannot verify;
- community governance becomes unpaid review labor while the experimental arm
  receives free authority information;
- GDPR is treated as optional, or conversely as a complete substitute for
  collective authority;
- a dispute is resolved by confidence, majority vote, least-restrictive policy,
  or authority shopping; or
- Candidate 020 adds no protection beyond the complete legal, ethics,
  agreement, Local Contexts, IAM, provenance, and human-review null stack.

| semantic preservation under compaction and deletion | [Candidate 017](../../experiments/candidates/017-contract-preserving-semantic-compaction.md) | preserve governance state and prove what cannot be recalled |
| lineage across people and institutional turnover | [Candidate 019](../../experiments/candidates/019-audited-cumulative-inheritance.md) | authority and benefit obligations must survive researcher turnover |

Candidate 020 owns **who may decide and what must be protected**. Candidate 009
owns enforcement of the current decision. Candidate 014 owns propagation to
evidence and derivatives. Candidate 015 owns versioned protocol interpretation.
Candidates 017 and 019 own long-lived preservation and turnover. None may infer
the normative input it enforces.

## Evidence boundaries

- CARE and the GIDA rights paper are Indigenous-led governance frameworks and
  scholarly arguments. They are not randomized evaluations, automatic legal
  rules in every state, or proof that one software design improves outcomes.
- UNDRIP is an authoritative international declaration of minimum standards.
  Its domestic legal effects and procedures differ by jurisdiction.
- the Nagoya Protocol is legally scoped to genetic resources and associated
  traditional knowledge and operates through Party and domestic measures. It is
  not a global license or a complete knowledge-governance code.
- OCAP®, Te Mana Raraunga, and Maiam nayri Wingara must retain their named
  peoples and contexts. Their overlap supports the global CARE comparison but
  does not erase distinct laws, authorities, languages, or procedures.
- Local Contexts Labels and Notices are practical, extra-legal mechanisms. A
  correct API integration does not establish that a relationship is legitimate
  or a benefit was delivered.
- GDPR is binding EU law within scope and remains the project's individual
  data-protection baseline. Its natural-person framing does not exhaust
  collective cultural or knowledge governance.
- no source in this audit authorizes this repository to reproduce, summarize,
  train on, or abstract community-specific knowledge beyond what the source's
  own public governance statement intentionally communicates.

## Final disposition

The ANZSRC divergence exposed a real gap in the repository's breadth control:
the project had machinery for provenance, privacy, access, governance, and
constitutional authority, but no explicit rule that a collective linked to
data or knowledge can be a rights holder and authority whose procedures,
purposes, benefits, and changing decisions remain outside model invention.

That gap does not justify a new natural or computational principle. It justifies
a stricter Candidate 020 applicability class and a testable contract binding
Candidate 020's standing to Candidates 009, 014, 015, 017, and 019. The first
system action is restraint: preserve heterogeneity, ask who has authority, and
do not turn community knowledge into a research input before the community has
authorized the exact purpose and terms.
