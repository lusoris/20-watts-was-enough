# Visual art practice and design cognition: primary-source audit

- **Audit date:** 2026-08-05
- **Scope:** drawing and sketching, external representations, mental imagery,
  visual expertise, style learning, exemplar use, fixation, analogy, divergent
  and convergent ideation, critique, iterative refinement, material
  affordances, embodied making, collaborative design, aesthetic judgment,
  reconstructive and transformational generation, cultural transmission, and
  conservation/version history
- **Evidence rule:** behavioral interventions and measured practice support
  scoped task claims; protocol studies identify candidate processes but not
  unique causal algorithms; neuroimaging and retrospective reports do not by
  themselves establish a transferable mechanism
- **Promotion state:** audit-local `VADC-` claims only; this audit promotes no
  stable claim, principle, or experiment candidate

## Executive finding

Visual art and design provide unusually observable records of thinking because
marks, mock-ups, discarded variants, revisions, materials, critiques, and final
artifacts can all survive outside the maker. The literature does not support one
general “visual creativity” mechanism. It supports a more useful decomposition:

- a sketch can be an external memory, a test surface, a communication object,
  or a source of newly perceived relations; those functions must be measured
  separately;
- moving, rotating, tracing, drawing, and making can acquire information, not
  only execute a prior internal plan;
- representation format changes which operations are cheap, visible, and
  reversible, but paper, pixels, clay, and prototypes have no context-free
  ranking;
- imagery, object memory, spatial memory, perception, and drawing skill are
  dissociable; successful visual work does not require one uniform internal
  picture;
- expertise changes task-specific segmentation, attention, categorization, and
  eye–hand coordination, while selection effects and limited transfer prevent
  treating an expert label as a causal variable;
- generation is reconstructive: learned category structure, exemplars, styles,
  analogies, constraints, and material responses shape what can be proposed;
- copying and examples can either transform a maker's representation or induce
  fixation. Distance, familiarity, timing, task, and depth of engagement decide
  which effect is observed;
- generating many variants does not solve evaluation. People often trade
  originality against feasibility, usefulness, risk, and personal or social
  value when selecting;
- parallel prototypes and well-timed critique can improve exploration, but
  interaction also propagates category fixation and conformity;
- aesthetic response is an outcome vector conditioned on observer, context,
  exposure, knowledge, task, and artifact—not an intrinsic scalar recoverable
  from surface statistics alone;
- cultural accumulation can retain improvements across turnover, but ordinary
  archives, version control, retrieval, explicit teaching, search, and
  population optimization are mandatory nulls; and
- conservation imaging shows that a finished surface can conceal materially
  distinct prior states. Provenance records preserve derivation claims; they do
  not reconstruct an author's complete cognition or establish authenticity by
  themselves.

The recurring transferable pattern is therefore not random mixing. It is a
**versioned reconstruct–externalize–inspect–transform–evaluate loop** whose
proposal distribution is learned and history-dependent, whose interventions
change available evidence, and whose retained result remains attributable to
its sources and tests. Every part already has strong ordinary counterparts in
retrieval, generative modeling, search, optimization, interactive tools, and
version control. No new principle survives this audit. The most relevant held
composition is [Candidate 004](../../experiments/candidates/004-closed-endogenous-curriculum.md)
with [Candidate 019](../../experiments/candidates/019-audited-cumulative-inheritance.md),
constrained by [Candidate 014](../../experiments/candidates/014-versioned-observation-contract.md)
and the representational-loss questions in
[Candidate 017](../../experiments/candidates/017-contract-preserving-semantic-compaction.md).

## Outcome firewall

“Creative,” “good,” “expert,” and “inspired” are unusable as untyped endpoints.
Every result must identify the measured row or rows.

| Outcome | Operational question | Minimum unit or score | Invalid substitution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| perceptual accuracy | does the produced representation preserve declared source relations? | landmark error in mm or pixels, angular error in degrees, scale/proportion error, topology violations | expert rating alone |
| external-memory value | what state remains available after internal memory is loaded or delayed? | task accuracy, latency in seconds, omissions/intrusions, retained bits under a declared query set | number of marks |
| reinterpretation | does inspecting an artifact produce a relation not encoded in the preceding verbalized plan? | preregistered coded transition with inter-rater reliability | any edit after looking |
| epistemic action | does an action reduce uncertainty or computation before commitment? | information gain in bits, error reduction, latency, action and energy cost | movement count |
| ideational fluency | how many admissible proposals are generated? | unique valid proposals per minute | novelty or usefulness |
| diversity | how broadly does the set cover declared feature or function space? | category coverage, pairwise distance distribution, entropy with a fixed embedding | proposal count |
| novelty | how unlike the declared reference corpus is an output? | corpus- and metric-qualified distance or rarity | subjective liking |
| usefulness/fit | does an output satisfy the task and stakeholder constraints? | constraint-specific vector, failure rate, user outcome | novelty |
| selection quality | does the selected proposal dominate available alternatives on the declared objective? | regret against blinded evaluation or realized outcome | generator's confidence |
| style recognition | can style be discriminated independently of content, artist identity, and reproduction artifacts? | held-out accuracy/log loss by crossed content and style | nearest-image retrieval |
| aesthetic response | what does which observer report or do, under which context? | rating scale, choice probability, viewing time, physiological measure, each separately | universal artistic quality |
| collaboration | does interaction improve the joint artifact beyond nominal pooled individuals? | quality, diversity, time, coordination load, contribution distribution | conversational activity |
| transmission | what survives learner or team turnover? | generation-indexed performance, fidelity, innovation, lineage coverage | final score without ancestry |
| conservation state | what material layer, intervention, or derivation is observed? | modality, spatial resolution, elemental/spectral uncertainty, timestamp, chain of custody | artist intention |

An output can be novel but invalid, beautiful to one group but not another,
accurately copied but unoriginal, or diverse yet impossible to manufacture.
Report the vector rather than averaging it into one creativity number.

## Transfer rule and mature ordinary null stack

A visual-art or design-derived mechanism is eligible for architectural work
only if all of the following are true:

1. the proposed state, operation, and retention rule are explicit rather than
   named “inspiration,” “embodiment,” “intuition,” or “style”;
2. the source corpus, prior exposure, prompt, tool affordances, representation
   format, material, evaluator, and task constraints are recorded;
3. novelty, diversity, validity, usefulness, aesthetic judgment, and selection
   are scored separately with blinded or preregistered evaluators where
   possible;
4. process claims survive intervention: hide or alter the sketch, reorder
   critique, perturb material feedback, replace exemplars, or remove version
   history;
5. parameters, source examples, proposals, renderings, tool calls, user time,
   evaluator calls, physical material, latency, compute, and energy are charged;
6. immediate task performance is separated from retention, transfer to new
   styles/problems/materials, and cumulative improvement across turnover; and
7. the complete ordinary stack below is implemented or bounded.

The ordinary null stack is:

- exact and approximate image retrieval, case-based design, search engines,
  mood boards, catalogs, precedent databases, and nearest-neighbor recombination;
- raster/vector editors, CAD, constraint solvers, parametric design, direct
  manipulation, undo/redo, layers, branches, and ordinary version control;
- latent-variable and diffusion generators, neural style transfer, inpainting,
  conditional sampling, interpolation, augmentation, and prompt ensembles;
- beam search, Monte Carlo search, evolutionary optimization, Bayesian
  optimization, quality-diversity search, rejection sampling, and reranking;
- working-memory aids, scratchpads, whiteboards, blackboards, retrieval-augmented
  generation, and explicit intermediate-state serialization;
- active learning, value of information, experiment design, simulation,
  digital twins, finite-element analysis, rendering, and physical prototyping;
- independent generation followed by pooling, nominal groups, structured
  critique, pairwise comparison, voting, and multi-objective optimization;
- supervised representation learning for content, style, artist, period, and
  medium with leakage-controlled splits;
- Git-like snapshots, diffs, branches, merges, immutable object stores, W3C
  PROV-compatible lineage, checksums, and signed metadata; and
- controlled copying, imitation learning, curriculum, fine-tuning, distillation,
  ensemble selection, and population-based training.

A biological, artistic, or craft analogy earns no baseline credit. The proposed
composition must beat these nulls on the same outcome vector and full lifecycle
budget.

## Shared quantitative contract

### State, action, and artifact history

Let $x_t$ denote internal task state, $a_t$ an action, $e_t$ the observed
environment/material response, and $r_t$ the external representation after the
action. A minimal closed loop is

$$
r_{t+1}=F(r_t,a_t,e_t),
\qquad
x_{t+1}=G(x_t,r_{t+1},e_t).
$$

This notation does not assume that $x_t$ is verbal, symbolic, or fully
observable. A claim that an external mark changes cognition requires a
counterfactual manipulation of $r_t$ or its available operations, not merely a
correlation between marks and later ideas.

For artifact version $v_k$, retain a derivation tuple

$$
v_k=(p_k,\,o_k,\,u_k,\,s_k,\,q_k,\,\tau_k),
$$

where $p_k$ is the set of parent versions, $o_k$ the operation, $u_k$ the actor
or process identity, $s_k$ the source set, $q_k$ the test/evaluation record, and
$\tau_k$ the timestamp. The tuple is provenance, not proof that $v_k$ is true,
valuable, original, authentic, or correctly attributed.

### Epistemic action

For action $a$ under belief state $b$, expected information gain is

$$
\operatorname{EIG}(a\mid b)
=H(\Theta\mid b)
-\mathbb E_{y\sim p(y\mid a,b)}
 H(\Theta\mid b,a,y)
\quad [\mathrm{bit}],
$$

where $\Theta$ is the uncertain task quantity and $y$ is the resulting
observation. Charge action cost $C_a$ in time, energy, material, and risk. A
useful intervention must improve a declared objective beyond passive inference
or simulation at matched

$$
\frac{\operatorname{EIG}(a\mid b)}{C_a}.
$$

Do not infer information gain from motion alone; a rotation, sketch, or mock-up
can be redundant or misleading.

### Proposal and outcome vectors

For proposal $z_i$, record

$$
\mathbf y_i=
(n_i,d_i,f_i,u_i,a_i,r_i,c_i),
$$

where $n$ is novelty, $d$ diversity contribution, $f$ feasibility, $u$ task or
user utility, $a$ observer-qualified aesthetic response, $r$ risk, and $c$
full cost. Units and scales are declared per task. No fixed weighted sum is a
scientific constant. If a scalar is needed for a decision, publish the weights,
normalization, and sensitivity analysis.

For a generated set $Z$, diversity can be reported as the distribution of
pairwise distances $D(z_i,z_j)$ under a frozen representation and as category
coverage. Because embedding choice can create the conclusion, repeat with
task-native features and at least one independent representation.

Selection regret for chosen $z^*$ under declared utility $U$ is

$$
R_{\mathrm{sel}}=
\max_{z\in Z} U(z)-U(z^*).
$$

Report evaluator uncertainty and realized post-selection outcomes. Low regret
to a biased evaluator is not useful selection.

### Equal-budget lifecycle accounting

For method $m$, report

$$
B_m=(N_{\mathrm{src}},N_{\mathrm{prop}},N_{\mathrm{eval}},
t_{\mathrm{human}},t_{\mathrm{wall}},M_{\mathrm{peak}},E_{\mathrm{elec}},
M_{\mathrm{material}},W_{\mathrm{waste}}),
$$

with counts, seconds, bytes, joules or kWh, kilograms, and kilograms
respectively. Physical fabrication and human critique are not free because GPU
energy is easy to meter. Equal-budget tests either match every relevant term or
place methods on a preregistered Pareto frontier.

## Mechanism cards

### VADC-01 — A sketch can be an active external workspace

**Audit-local claim.** In small protocol studies of architectural design,
designers did not only deposit already-formed ideas into sketches; inspecting
their own marks was associated with new perceptual relations and functional or
conceptual thoughts. This supports an external workspace function, not a claim
that sketching always causes better design.

**Observation or task.** Suwa and Tversky analyzed retrospective reports from
architectural design sessions, and Suwa, Purcell, and Gero coded physical,
perceptual, functional, and conceptual actions in an architect's protocol
([Suwa and Tversky 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0142-694X(97)00008-2);
[Suwa, Purcell, and Gero 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0142-694X(98)00016-7)).
Goldschmidt described alternating “seeing as” and “seeing that” modes in design
sketching ([Goldschmidt 1991](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400419109534381)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Architectural brief, prior domain education,
personal precedent memory, sequential hand-drawn marks, and the participant's
own verbal reconstruction. Marks inherit the entire preceding design path.

**Intervention and control.** The cited protocol work is mainly observational.
A causal test must randomize visibility, persistence, manipulability, or
semantic detail of intermediate representations while preserving task time and
motor demands. A blank scratchpad, textual notes, exact CAD, and memory-only
condition are separate controls.

**State and timescale.** Stroke- and inspection-level state over seconds;
candidate layout and function state over minutes; learned drawing and domain
schemas over years.

**Units and outcome vector.** Coded new relations per inspection, transitions
between perceptual and functional codes, solution validity, spatial error,
time in seconds, mark count, coder agreement, delayed reconstruction, and final
quality/diversity. Counts require a published coding manual and reliability.

**Inferential limits.** Retrospective protocol coding is vulnerable to omitted
thoughts, post-hoc rationalization, segmentation choices, and tiny expert
samples. Temporal adjacency between seeing and saying does not identify a
unique causal computation.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Maintain a queryable external design state
whose partial structures can be resegmented, annotated, simulated, and revised;
let generators inspect relations induced by their own intermediate artifacts,
not only append tokens to a hidden trajectory.

**Mature ordinary null.** A versioned scratchpad, scene graph, CAD constraint
model, retrieval-backed blackboard, tool-augmented language model, and ordinary
beam/tree search over serialized intermediate states.

**Exact deduplication.** Primary: [P-013 externalized shared state](../principle-registry.md#p-013--externalized-shared-state).
Secondary: [P-003 temporary trace](../principle-registry.md#p-003--temporary-trace-before-commitment)
and [P-012 memory matched to information lifetime](../principle-registry.md#p-012--memory-matched-to-information-lifetime).
The versioned query surface stress-tests
[Candidate 017](../../experiments/candidates/017-contract-preserving-semantic-compaction.md)
and [Candidate 018](../../experiments/candidates/018-value-reconstructability-aware-tiering.md);
it does not justify another workspace principle.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Give matched agents the same model, examples,
operations, proposal count, token budget, and wall time. Compare hidden state,
append-only notes, editable structured workspace, and full search tree. Reject
the art-derived transfer if a conventional scratchpad or search tree matches
held-out validity, diversity, recovery after an early wrong turn, and energy.

### VADC-02 — Actions can acquire information rather than only execute plans

**Audit-local claim.** Manipulating a representation or material can make a
problem cheaper to perceive or compute. The effect is task- and affordance-
specific and is already covered by value-of-information and interactive
computation.

**Observation or task.** In Tetris, players used rotations and translations
that simplified perceptual judgment rather than directly advancing the final
placement ([Kirsh and Maglio 1994](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1)).
Zhang and Norman showed analytically and experimentally that external
representations change the operators and memory demands of structurally
equivalent tasks ([Zhang and Norman 1994](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1801_3)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Current board or artifact state, learned
sensorimotor operations, display conventions, time pressure, motor fluency, and
the representation's visible constraints.

**Intervention and control.** Disable or price rotations; present isomorphic
tasks in different notations; provide physical, simulated, and internally
computed operations; control input latency and visibility.

**State and timescale.** Belief and display state update over tens of
milliseconds to seconds; operator fluency develops across sessions.

**Units and outcome vector.** Decision latency in milliseconds, error rate,
operations, expected information gain in bits, motor distance, joules, and
task score. Separate informative actions from goal-progress actions.

**Inferential limits.** Tetris is not drawing or sculpture. An action classified
as epistemic by a model can also be habitual, motor-preparatory, or pragmatic.
The studies do not show that physical execution is better than a faithful
low-cost simulator.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Let a system buy a render, crop, rotate,
probe, simulate, fabricate, or sensor view when the expected decision value
exceeds its cost.

**Mature ordinary null.** Active perception, Bayesian experimental design,
POMDP planning, value-of-information policies, programmatic image transforms,
test-time augmentation, and deterministic geometry engines.

**Exact deduplication.** Primary: [P-007 prediction-error allocation](../principle-registry.md#p-007--prediction-error-allocation).
Secondary: P-013 when the action changes shared external state. The
action-conditioned observation model is already
[Candidate 007](../../experiments/candidates/007-endogenous-observation-surveillance.md)
and dependency record is [Candidate 014](../../experiments/candidates/014-versioned-observation-contract.md).

**Equal-budget falsification.** Compare learned intervention choice with passive
inference, fixed view augmentation, exhaustive cheap transforms, and an oracle
value-of-information policy. Match sensor/render calls, latency, compute, and
failures. Reject if movement adds no held-out information or a simulator
delivers the same outcome at lower lifecycle cost.

### VADC-03 — Representation format changes reinterpretation opportunities

**Audit-local claim.** Ambiguous, low-commitment marks can support
reinterpretation during early ideation, while more finished representations can
encourage refinement of an assumed form. This is a contingent operation-cost
claim, not “paper good, digital bad.”

**Observation or task.** Stones and Cassidy compared paper sketches and digital
marks in student graphic-design ideation. Participants reinterpreted both, but
paper-supported reinterpretations more often became new ideas in that study
([Stones and Cassidy 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2010.05.003)).
The result complements, but does not experimentally validate every proposal in,
the broader sketch review by Purcell and Gero
([Purcell and Gero 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0142-694X(98)00015-5)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Tool familiarity, mark vocabulary, undo
model, rendering defaults, pressure/latency, graphic-design education, prior
idea, and the exact sequence of marks.

**Intervention and control.** Cross tool and representation fidelity: paper,
freehand tablet, vector primitives, rendered mock-up, and deliberately noisy or
abstract digital marks. Train participants equally and preserve operations,
display size, time, and resolution where possible.

**State and timescale.** Marks persist for minutes to days; interpretation can
switch in seconds; tool habits and representational literacy develop over
months or years.

**Units and outcome vector.** Coded reinterpretations, proportion that lead to
distinct functions or forms, edit distance, branch count, idea diversity,
validity, time, undo operations, and confidence.

**Inferential limits.** Small student samples, tool-era effects, unequal
fluency, and subjective coding limit generality. Ambiguity can also hide errors,
create coordination failures, and waste downstream work.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Use representation schedules: lossy,
editable abstractions for search; progressively stricter geometry, constraints,
and rendering near commitment; preserve links between abstraction levels.

**Mature ordinary null.** Multi-resolution modeling, coarse-to-fine search,
wireframes, level-of-detail rendering, latent diffusion drafts, parametric CAD,
and explicit branch/merge workflows.

**Exact deduplication.** P-003 covers cheap provisional traces; P-010 covers
moving mature constraints into structure; P-012 covers lifetime matching. The
loss contract is exactly [Candidate 017](../../experiments/candidates/017-contract-preserving-semantic-compaction.md),
not a new ambiguity principle.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Equalize training, operations, view area, time,
proposal count, and final rendering budget. Vary only representational
commitment and reversibility. Reject if conventional multi-resolution search
matches diversity and final constraint satisfaction, or if ambiguity gains are
paid back as more invalid downstream branches.

### VADC-04 — Mental imagery, object memory, and spatial memory are separable

**Audit-local claim.** Voluntary visual imagery can affect later perception and
memory reports, but drawing-relevant object and spatial information can
dissociate. An architecture should not require one uniform internal bitmap to
explain visual construction.

**Observation or task.** Imagining oriented gratings biased subsequent
binocular-rivalry perception under controls for mock displays
([Pearson, Rademaker, and Tong 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417134)).
In an online drawing study, participants reporting congenital aphantasia
recalled fewer objects and less color but retained spatial accuracy comparable
to controls and made fewer intrusions; copying performance did not differ
([Bainbridge et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2020.11.014)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Recently viewed scenes or gratings, long-term
object and spatial knowledge, verbal strategies, self-reported imagery
experience, and drawing interface skill.

**Intervention and control.** Perception, imagery, mock-rivalry catch trials,
memory drawing, matched copying, category-only drawing, and nonvisual verbal or
spatial scaffolds. Aphantasia is a selected individual difference, not a
randomized lesion.

**State and timescale.** Imagery induction over seconds; scene retention over
minutes; lifelong imagery phenotype and strategy history.

**Units and outcome vector.** Rivalry bias probability, object count, spatial
displacement, color details, intrusion rate, drawing time, confidence, and
self-report. Do not replace objective task behavior with vividness rating.

**Inferential limits.** Aphantasia recruitment and self-identification create
selection concerns; compensation can equalize some outcomes; drawing adds
motor and symbolic choices. These data do not show that imagery is irrelevant
or that one neural module stores each content type.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Maintain factorized object, spatial,
relational, and renderable states; allow symbolic/spatial plans to guide image
generation without requiring a high-resolution image at every step.

**Mature ordinary null.** Scene graphs plus renderers, object-centric models,
spatial maps, latent-variable generators, text-to-image systems, and retrieval
of reference imagery.

**Exact deduplication.** P-008 covers compartmentalized state; P-012 covers
memory by content lifetime and access; P-010 covers rendering/structure
offload. Candidate 004 may use factorized proposal state, but the dissociation
does not create a new generator candidate.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Compare monolithic image-latent, factorized
scene-graph, symbolic-only, and retrieval-render pipelines at matched parameters,
training images, memory bytes, samples, and renderer calls. Test object,
spatial, compositional, and transformation tasks separately. Reject factorized
transfer if it provides no Pareto gain or fails cross-component consistency.

### VADC-05 — Observational drawing expertise includes learned segmentation and attention

**Audit-local claim.** Expert copying can use task-specific eye–hand
segmentation and flexible visual attention acquired with practice. This does
not establish a general perceptual upgrade or a fixed expert gaze policy.

**Observation or task.** Tchalenko recorded 16 participants copying a line
drawing; experts segmented the source into simple line sections and used a
direct eye–hand strategy associated with greater accuracy
([Tchalenko 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.02.012)).
Chamberlain and Wagemans found different local/global attention measures
predicted art-student membership and drawing skill in a small cross-sectional
sample ([Chamberlain and Wagemans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2015.08.012)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Years and type of drawing practice, chosen
subjects and media, visual acuity, source image, gaze history, stroke state,
motor control, and education/selection into art programs.

**Intervention and control.** Longitudinal randomized training is the strong
test. Cross-sectional expert/novice contrasts require matching age, vision,
motor skill, motivation, and task familiarity; guided-gaze and segmentation
interventions test mechanism.

**State and timescale.** Fixations and strokes over tens to hundreds of
milliseconds; segment copying over seconds; expertise acquisition over years.

**Units and outcome vector.** Fixation duration and saccade distance in ms and
degrees, gaze–stroke lag, segment length, landmark/proportion error, completion
time, retention, transfer to novel subject/medium, and cognitive load.

**Inferential limits.** Expert selection, small samples, task specificity, and
multiple successful strategies preclude causal claims about training. Accurate
copying is not originality, composition, or aesthetic judgment.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Learn coarse-to-fine, task-conditioned
visual sampling and action chunks jointly with a renderer or controller; adapt
the segmentation policy to error and medium.

**Mature ordinary null.** Image pyramids, attention models, saliency sampling,
vectorization, contour tracing, hierarchical planning, imitation learning, and
fixed expert demonstrations.

**Exact deduplication.** P-001 covers sparse selection, P-007 unresolved-error
sampling, P-008 local segments, and P-010 compiled skill. Candidate 004 may
generate targeted practice; Candidate 006 is not supported because no
rewritable physical substrate was tested.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Train learned gaze–stroke segmentation against
uniform crops, saliency, image pyramids, oracle contours, and end-to-end
attention. Match pixels inspected, parameters, training drawings, strokes, and
latency. Test unfamiliar media and non-copying composition. Reject if benefits
are only source tracing or disappear outside the trained style.

### VADC-06 — Style and content are time-, task-, and expertise-dependent representations

**Audit-local claim.** Viewers can discriminate style and content on different
time courses, and expertise changes which features support classification and
evaluation. “Style” is therefore a learned, task-conditioned relation—not one
context-free texture statistic.

**Observation or task.** With artist and motif crossed, content influenced
similarity judgments at the shortest tested exposures while style effects were
detectable from 50 ms onward
([Augustin et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2007.11.006)).
Art-history experts and lay viewers differed in judgments and scan patterns as
paintings varied from representational to abstract
([Pihko et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00094)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Artist/period exposure, art-historical labels,
motif familiarity, reproduction quality, education, museum context, and
presentation duration.

**Intervention and control.** Cross artist, content, style, period, medium, and
capture pipeline; hold out artists and motifs; manipulate exposure duration and
training; strip frames, labels, and metadata when testing visual recognition.

**State and timescale.** Early perceptual evidence at 10–3000 ms; categorical
learning over sessions; art-historical expertise over years and cultures.

**Units and outcome vector.** Style/content classification accuracy and log
loss, reaction time in ms, calibration, gaze metrics, transfer to unseen
artists, confusion matrix, liking and understanding separately.

**Inferential limits.** Artist can leak style, content can correlate with era,
and digital reproductions alter scale, color, texture, and surface. Expert
groups differ in more than training. A decoder's accuracy does not show that
people or artists use its representation.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Learn factorized but interacting content,
style, medium, process, and provenance variables; require cross-artist and
cross-content generalization before calling a feature stylistic.

**Mature ordinary null.** Supervised multi-task classifiers, contrastive
learning, domain adaptation, Gram-matrix style representations, artist-held-out
benchmarks, and nearest-neighbor retrieval.

**Exact deduplication.** P-008 covers factorized interfaces and P-012 stores
different histories. Candidate 014 should record dataset and reproduction
dependencies; Candidate 015 covers explicit learned conventions where style
labels coordinate interpretation. No style principle remains.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Compare a proposed style state with multi-task
CNN/ViT, contrastive, Gram-statistic, and retrieval models at matched data,
parameters, resolution, and augmentations. Use artist-, motif-, medium-, and
institution-held-out splits. Reject if gains vanish after leakage controls or
do not predict controlled human discrimination.

### VADC-07 — Imagination reconstructs from learned category structure

**Audit-local claim.** Novel visual proposals inherit typical and correlated
properties from learned categories even when the requested entity is fictional.
This supports reconstructive generation from acquired structure; it neither
reduces generation to random mixing nor requires novelty independent of prior
experience.

**Observation or task.** Across five experiments, participants imagining
animals on another planet frequently retained Earth-animal properties such as
symmetry, appendages, and correlated features; prompts shifted which properties
appeared ([Ward 1994](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1994.1010)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Lifetime experience of animals and depictions,
task wording, supplied feature cues, category accessibility, cultural history,
and drawing/verbal reporting skill.

**Intervention and control.** Manipulate category labels, feature correlations,
exemplar frequency, counterfactual constraints, and available source corpus;
compare recall, recombination, rule induction, and random feature sampling.

**State and timescale.** Cue activation and proposal generation over minutes;
category learning over years.

**Units and outcome vector.** Feature frequency, dependency preservation,
distance from training/reference distribution, constraint validity, rated
novelty, duplicate rate, and usefulness where a task exists.

**Inferential limits.** Student drawings of fictional animals are not real-world
artistic invention. Typicality can reflect response ease, task interpretation,
or scorer ontology. Novelty relative to a sample is not historical novelty.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Make proposal ancestry measurable: identify
retrieved exemplars, transformed relations, constraint changes, and unsupported
additions; deliberately manipulate abstraction level and dependency strength.

**Mature ordinary null.** Conditional generative models, compositional scene
graphs, probabilistic programs, retrieval-and-edit, latent interpolation, and
constraint-guided sampling.

**Exact deduplication.** P-004 covers diversity and selection, P-008 structured

### VADC-11 — Mental simulation turns some uncertainty into approximation

**Audit-local claim.** Designers report and exhibit mental simulations more

approximation type, and downstream redesign.

**Mature ordinary null.** Surrogate models, model-predictive control, finite

Two novice charrettes documented extensive manipulation of paper, foam, and
cardboard for both exploration and communication, but were observational
([Bousseau et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858159)).

**Inputs, training, and history.** Material stiffness, friction, mass,
thickness, tolerances, joining methods, tool access, scale, prior craft skill,
fabrication plan, source example, and virtual-model fidelity.

**Intervention and control.** Cross physical, virtual, sketch, and hybrid
prototypes; preserve which variables each represents; manipulate material
fidelity and tool access; include sham handling and exact simulation controls;
counterbalance modality order.

**State and timescale.** Haptic/contact feedback in milliseconds; manipulation
and assembly over seconds to hours; material aging and maker skill over months
to years.

**Units and outcome vector.** Force in newtons, displacement in mm, mass in kg,
stiffness in N/m, tolerance and fit error, assembly time, failures, inherited
features, branch count, rework, waste in kg, human time, and full lifecycle
energy.

**Inferential limits.** A material affords actions only relative to a body,
tool, goal, and environment. Physical conditions often expose more variables
while also receiving more time, attention, and irreversible commitment. Small
design-build studies disagree across tasks. Handling an object does not prove a
distinct embodied representation, and physical prototypes can themselves
induce sunk-cost fixation.

**Possible AI/system translation.** Bind artifact state to measured plant and
material properties; choose the cheapest representation that preserves the
uncertain variable; escalate from sketch to simulation to instrumented physical
probe; feed failures back with calibration and provenance.

**Mature ordinary null.** CAD constraints, multibody and finite-element
simulation, digital twins, hardware-in-the-loop, rapid prototyping, robotic
system identification, design-of-experiments, and ordinary fabrication tests.

**Exact deduplication.** P-007 covers buying evidence where uncertainty remains;
P-010 covers physical/computational co-design; P-013 covers the manipulated
artifact as shared state. Candidate 007 covers action-conditioned observation,
Candidate 010 staged verification, Candidate 014 measurement dependencies, and
Candidate 006 only if a rewritable local physical substrate performs a mature
recurring mapping—none of the cited studies tests that condition.

**Equal-budget falsification.** Match represented variables, designer/model
time, trials, simulation calls, material, fabrication latency, and energy.
Include tasks whose relevant uncertainty is geometric, dynamic, tactile, and
purely symbolic. Reject a material-engagement transfer if a calibrated
simulator or low-fidelity mock-up matches decisions, or if physical gains vanish
after unrepresented variables and irreversible rework are charged.

## Failures, negative transfer, and construct-validity register

## Conservative system fixture

The literature justifies a benchmark fixture, not an architectural commitment:

```text
attributed sources + learned task history + current constraints
  -> independent, reconstructive proposal branches
  -> external editable representations at declared fidelity
  -> optional epistemic actions / simulation / material probes
  -> source-, constraint-, and uncertainty-aware evaluation
  -> critique with private alternatives preserved
  -> selection on an explicit outcome vector
  -> versioned artifact + tests + rejected branches + provenance
  -> retention, expiry, recombination, or rollback across turnover
```

The fixture is accepted only if the complete composition beats retrieval,
generative sampling, population search, independent reranking, interactive
tools, and ordinary version control at matched lifecycle budget. Individual
cards can be useful without the whole composition winning.

## First cross-domain falsification program

Use three tasks with different truth conditions:

1. **visual reconstruction:** recreate a scene after controlled observation,
   with object, spatial, and source-memory scoring;
2. **functional design:** create an artifact satisfying hidden physical and user
   constraints, with simulation and physical tests; and
3. **open visual communication:** produce a graphic for heterogeneous viewers,
   with source-blind preference, comprehension, and delayed recall.

For each task, cross the following factors:

- no external workspace, append-only notes, editable sketch, and structured CAD;
- no examples, one common example, diverse examples, and structural retrieval;
- serial versus parallel proposals;
- no critique, immediate critique, delayed critique, and independent blinded
  critique;
- hidden lineage versus source-attributed version graph; and
- individual, nominal pool, shared canvas, and selective-reveal collaboration.

Pre-register equal budgets, frozen evaluators, novelty representations, source
corpus, constraints, failure costs, and stopping rules. Include task changes and
team turnover. Measure the full outcome and lifecycle vectors above.

The art/design composition fails if any of the following holds:

- ordinary retrieval plus constrained sampling matches valid novelty;
- beam or quality-diversity search matches parallel prototyping;
- a conventional scratchpad or CAD state matches reinterpretation and recovery;
- independent pooling matches the shared workspace with less fixation;
- standard multi-objective ranking matches critique/selection regret;
- Git/W3C-PROV lineage matches retention, rollback, and invalidation; or
- gains disappear on new media, new source distributions, new evaluators, or
  after proposal count, human labor, and physical material are charged.

## Promotion verdict

**No new principle and no new candidate.** Visual art and design cognition add
high-value experiment fixtures and failure cases to existing bundles:

- external artifacts are active only when their available operations change
  inference or coordination beyond an ordinary scratchpad;
- reconstruction from learned structure is the default causal account, but
  useful generation still depends on controlled proposal, intervention,
  evaluation, and retention;
- examples and copying require paired positive- and negative-transfer tests;
- divergence, aesthetic response, feasibility, and selection remain separate;
- shared workspaces must be credited for coordination and charged for
  correlated fixation; and
- version history and conservation evidence preserve state and derivation, not
  complete cognition or truth.

The narrowest research opportunity is to test whether one attributable,
multi-fidelity external loop can preserve alternative lineages and learn when
to copy, abstract, simulate, materialize, solicit critique, and consolidate—
beyond the complete ordinary stack and at equal lifecycle cost. Until that
test passes, the contribution is a rigorous benchmark composition for
Candidates 004, 014, 017, 018, and 019.
